## Introduction

Czech folklore survives not only in old stories about [water spirits]({{ 'water-spirits-eaeb82/' | relative_url }}), witches, and haunted castles. It also appears every year in village streets, churchyards, fields, and public squares through seasonal customs that are still actively performed. Among the most striking of these traditions is the Ride of the Kings, a colourful horseback procession from south-eastern Moravia that has become one of the best-known examples of living Czech folk culture. Recognised by UNESCO in 2011, it combines costume, ritual, music, community memory, and mystery in a way that connects modern villages with centuries of tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ride-of-the-kings-in-the-south-east-of-the-czech-republic-00564" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ride of the kings in the south east of the czech republic 00564</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageRide of the Kings in the south-east of the Czech RepublicInscribed in 2011 (6.COM) on the Representative List...</span></span></span>

Some traditions have changed dramatically since the twentieth century, while others have been revived after periods of decline. ## How Czech Folklore Still Takes to the Streets

Seasonal customs have long marked the rhythm of life in Czech villages. Traditionally they celebrated the turning of the agricultural year, reinforced community bonds, and offered symbolic protection for people, livestock, and crops. Many customs combined Christian feast days with older local practices whose origins are difficult to trace precisely. Ethnographers often view them as layered traditions in which medieval, early modern, and possibly pre-Christian elements coexist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nulk.cz/en/2019/08/28/rides-of-the-kings-in-the-south-east-of-the-czech-republic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nulk.cz">[Národní ústav lidové kultury]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nulk.cz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Ride of the Kings is – as an annual custom – a ceremonious horseback procession of boys...Read more...</span></span></span>

What makes Czech seasonal folklore distinctive is its public character. Rather than being confined to private belief, customs frequently involve processions, music, elaborate dress, communal feasting, and performances that move through the village itself. The streets become a stage on which local identity is displayed and renewed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitczechia.com/en-us/news/2022/03/n-traditions-customs-unesco" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitczechia.com">[Visit Czechia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitczechia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">n traditions customs unesco</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kings? ## Ride of the Kings in South-Eastern Moravia

The Ride of the Kings is the most famous surviving example of this tradition. Known from the Moravian Slovakia region of south-eastern Czechia, it is celebrated most prominently in the village of Vlčnov and survives in a small number of other communities including Hluk, Kunovice, and Skoronice. UNESCO placed it on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2011 because of its cultural significance and continued transmission between generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ride-of-the-kings-in-the-south-east-of-the-czech-republic-00564" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ride of the kings in the south east of the czech republic 00564</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageRide of the Kings in the south-east of the Czech RepublicInscribed in 2011 (6.COM) on the Representative List...</span></span></span>

The procession centres on a young boy chosen to serve as the king. His face is partly concealed by ribbons, and he traditionally holds a rose in his mouth. The king wears ceremonial clothing associated with female costume, while attendants and guards accompany him on richly decorated horses. Riders move through the village, chanting traditional verses, collecting gifts, and engaging with spectators. Women and families play a major role in preparing costumes and decorations, making the event a collective community effort rather than a performance by a small specialist group.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ride-of-the-kings-in-the-south-east-of-the-czech-republic-00564" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ride of the kings in the south east of the czech republic 00564</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageRide of the Kings in the south-east of the Czech RepublicInscribed in 2011 (6.COM) on the Representative List...</span></span></span>

One reason the custom fascinates folklorists is that nobody can fully explain its origin. Several competing interpretations exist:

### From Village Ritual to Cultural Symbol

The Ride of the Kings was once more widespread. Sources indicate that similar rides occurred across broader areas of Moravia and neighbouring regions before declining during the twentieth century. Industrialisation, social change, war, and changing lifestyles reduced the number of communities able to maintain such labour-intensive customs. Today only a handful of places continue the tradition regularly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nulk.cz/en/2019/08/28/rides-of-the-kings-in-the-south-east-of-the-czech-republic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nulk.cz">[nulk.cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nulk.cz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Ride of the Kings is – as an annual custom – a ceremonious horseback procession of boys...Read more...</span></span></span>

Yet decline was followed by renewed interest. Folklore festivals, heritage organisations, local museums, and community groups helped preserve the custom. UNESCO recognition increased international visibility, turning the Ride of the Kings into a symbol of regional identity while encouraging documentation and safeguarding efforts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ride-of-the-kings-in-the-south-east-of-the-czech-republic-00564" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ride of the kings in the south east of the czech republic 00564</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageRide of the Kings in the south-east of the Czech RepublicInscribed in 2011 (6.COM) on the Representative List...</span></span></span>

## Spring, Harvest, Easter, and Christmas Customs

Although the Ride of the Kings is the most famous example, it belongs to a wider calendar of living Czech seasonal traditions.

Spring customs traditionally marked the end of winter and the return of fertility to the landscape. Across different regions, communities organised processions, singing rituals, and symbolic acts connected with renewal. Such events often blended Christian observance with local folk practices focused on prosperity and good harvests.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nulk.cz/en/2019/08/28/rides-of-the-kings-in-the-south-east-of-the-czech-republic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nulk.cz">[Národní ústav lidové kultury]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nulk.cz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Ride of the Kings is – as an annual custom – a ceremonious horseback procession of boys...Read more...</span></span></span>

Easter remains one of the strongest expressions of Czech folk culture. Rural celebrations often include decorated eggs, traditional costumes, communal visits between households, and symbolic rituals associated with health, fertility, and social renewal. Although modern attitudes have changed some practices, Easter remains one of the moments when folk traditions are most visibly performed in public.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitczechia.com/en-us/news/2022/03/n-traditions-customs-unesco" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitczechia.com">[Visit Czechia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitczechia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">n traditions customs unesco</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kings? ## Why Costumes, Horses, and Performance Matter

To an outside observer, the most memorable aspect of Czech seasonal customs may be their visual richness. Embroidered clothing, decorated horses, music, banners, and ritualised movement transform ordinary public spaces into ceremonial landscapes. These elements are not merely decorative. They communicate local identity, family history, and regional distinctiveness.

The Ride of the Kings demonstrates this especially clearly. Costumes vary according to local traditions, and villagers often recognise subtle details that outsiders might miss. Preparing clothing and horse decorations requires specialised knowledge passed through families and community networks. ## A Living Form of Czech Folklore

The enduring importance of Czech seasonal customs lies in their ability to connect past and present. Legends about spirits and heroes may survive through books and storytelling, but traditions such as the Ride of the Kings keep folklore visible in everyday life. They bring together costume, ritual, music, memory, and local pride in ways that remain meaningful to contemporary communities.

For many visitors, the Ride of the Kings appears mysterious because its original purpose is still debated. For the communities that maintain it, however, the mystery is less important than the act of continuation itself.
