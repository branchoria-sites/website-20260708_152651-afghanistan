## Introduction

Few figures in Somali folklore provoke as much debate as Arawelo, the legendary queen said to have overturned the normal order of society and placed women above men. Depending on who tells the story, she was either a cruel tyrant who terrorised male subjects, a clever satirical warning about power, or a heroic ruler who defended women in a violent world. Unlike many legendary rulers whose meaning remains stable, Arawelo has been repeatedly reinvented. Her story has become a battleground for arguments about gender, authority, history and identity in [Somalia]({{ 'somalias-living-world-of-story-and/' | relative_url }}) and across the Somali diaspora.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it/bitstream/2307/2733/1/Caroweelo%20-%20When%20an%20Abyssinian%20Queen%20Dominates%20the%20Horn%20of%20Africa_%20Teen%20Centuries%20ago....pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it">[ArcAdiA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arc Adi ACaroweelo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Caroweelo - When an Abyssinian Queen Dominates the...by AM Ismail · 2014 — as for the dominant features of Caroweelo: the cruelty...</span></span></span>

## The Queen Who Reversed Power

The best-known versions of the legend describe Arawelo as a ruler who established a society dominated by women. Men were allegedly stripped of authority, while women occupied positions of power and influence. Many retellings portray her as a formidable military leader and political organiser who challenged existing social structures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it/bitstream/2307/2733/1/Caroweelo%20-%20When%20an%20Abyssinian%20Queen%20Dominates%20the%20Horn%20of%20Africa_%20Teen%20Centuries%20ago....pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it">[ArcAdiA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arc Adi ACaroweelo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Caroweelo - When an Abyssinian Queen Dominates the...by AM Ismail · 2014 — as for the dominant features of Caroweelo: the cruelty...</span></span></span>

A striking feature of the story is its repeated emphasis on reversal. Tasks, privileges and social expectations traditionally associated with men are transferred to women. In some versions, Arawelo punishes male opponents through mutilation or symbolic humiliation. These dramatic elements are among the most memorable parts of the tale and have helped preserve it in oral tradition for generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it/bitstream/2307/2733/1/Caroweelo%20-%20When%20an%20Abyssinian%20Queen%20Dominates%20the%20Horn%20of%20Africa_%20Teen%20Centuries%20ago....pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it">[ArcAdiA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arc Adi ACaroweelo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Caroweelo - When an Abyssinian Queen Dominates the...by AM Ismail · 2014 — as for the dominant features of Caroweelo: the cruelty...</span></span></span>

The story is difficult to date. No firm historical evidence proves that Arawelo existed as a real ruler, and accounts vary enormously between regions and storytellers. Some scholars have suggested that the legend may preserve distant memories of powerful women in the Horn of Africa, while others see it primarily as a mythic narrative rather than a historical record. Comparisons have often been made with stories of powerful queens elsewhere in the region, including traditions associated with Gudit and Furra.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawelo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AraweloQueen Arawelo also known as Ebla Awad, was a proto-Somali Queen in traditional folklore. The landscape itself has become part of the legend. Oral traditions place Arawelo's kingdom in northern Somali territories, and a burial mound associated with her remains a well-known landmark in folklore. ## Why Was She Remembered as Dangerous?

Arawelo's reputation rests not merely on her power but on the fear she inspired. In many versions, male storytellers present her reign as unnatural, excessive or threatening. The recurring motif of punishing men has led some interpreters to view the tale as a cautionary narrative about what happens when established social hierarchies are overturned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it/bitstream/2307/2733/1/Caroweelo%20-%20When%20an%20Abyssinian%20Queen%20Dominates%20the%20Horn%20of%20Africa_%20Teen%20Centuries%20ago....pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it">[ArcAdiA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arc Adi ACaroweelo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Caroweelo - When an Abyssinian Queen Dominates the...by AM Ismail · 2014 — as for the dominant features of Caroweelo: the cruelty...</span></span></span>

This pattern is not unusual in folklore. Around the world, stories of powerful women are often remembered through a mixture of admiration and anxiety. Arawelo's legend exaggerates this tension. She is rarely portrayed as simply a good ruler or a bad ruler. Instead, she becomes a test case for questions about gender and authority.

Several features of the tradition stand out:

* Her strength is repeatedly described in terms normally associated with male heroes.
* Her rule is framed as a challenge to accepted social norms.
* Her alleged cruelty becomes inseparable from discussions of female leadership.

Because oral traditions change over time, it is impossible to know which elements are oldest. Some scholars and writers argue that stories emphasising extreme cruelty may reflect later patriarchal retellings designed to portray female authority as dangerous. Others view those elements as essential parts of the original narrative. ## Cruel Ruler, Comic Warning or Feminist Symbol?

The most fascinating aspect of Arawelo's story is that there is no single accepted interpretation.

For some Somalis, especially in older male-centred tellings, Arawelo functions as a warning. Her reign demonstrates the supposed dangers of giving too much power to any one group. The humour and exaggeration found in some retellings suggest that audiences were often expected to laugh at the absurdity of a world turned upside down.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it/bitstream/2307/2733/1/Caroweelo%20-%20When%20an%20Abyssinian%20Queen%20Dominates%20the%20Horn%20of%20Africa_%20Teen%20Centuries%20ago....pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it">[ArcAdiA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arc Adi ACaroweelo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Caroweelo - When an Abyssinian Queen Dominates the...by AM Ismail · 2014 — as for the dominant features of Caroweelo: the cruelty...</span></span></span>

For others, particularly modern writers and women's advocates, Arawelo represents resistance rather than tyranny. In these interpretations, her actions are understood as responses to the mistreatment of women. The story becomes less about hatred of men and more about confronting systems of inequality. Her legend is read as evidence that powerful female leadership has long existed within Somali cultural imagination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geeska.com/en/somali-women-between-memory-myth-and-merit" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geeska.com">[geeska.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geeska.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">somali women between memory myth and merit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Somali women between memory, myth and merit20 Nov 2025 — In Somali folklore, there is a story about a Queen named Araweello... She...</span></span></span>

Literary reinterpretations have pushed this reading further. Somali novelist Nuruddin Farah and other writers have explored Arawelo as a symbol of deeper historical tensions between patriarchal and possibly earlier matriarchal traditions. In these readings, the queen becomes less a person than an idea: a memory of alternative social possibilities preserved in folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawelo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The result is a remarkable cultural phenomenon. Arawelo can simultaneously function as:

* A feminist icon in contemporary discourse.
