## Introduction

In [Botswana]({{ 'where-botswanas-stories-live-in-the-land/' | relative_url }}), rain has never been just a weather event. For Tswana-speaking communities living in a country where drought can threaten crops, cattle and survival, rain traditionally carried moral, political and spiritual meaning. Rainmaking rituals, communication with ancestors and the work of traditional healers formed part of a connected worldview in which the well-being of people, livestock, leaders and the natural environment were closely linked. Rather than treating drought as a purely physical problem, older Tswana belief often interpreted prolonged dryness as a sign that relationships within the community—or between the living and the ancestral world—had fallen out of balance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://limpopocommission.org/the-basin/people-and-the-river/people-of-the-basin/culture-and-water/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: limpopocommission.org">[limpopocommission.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">limpopocommission.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on limpopocommission.org.</span></span></span>

## Why Rain Became a Sacred Question

Botswana lies largely within semi-arid environments where rainfall is unpredictable. In such conditions, rain naturally became associated with prosperity, political legitimacy and collective survival. Tswana communities developed elaborate ideas about the causes of drought and the means by which rain might be encouraged through ritual action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://limpopocommission.org/the-basin/people-and-the-river/people-of-the-basin/culture-and-water/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: limpopocommission.org">[limpopocommission.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">limpopocommission.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on limpopocommission.org.</span></span></span>

Rainmaking was not usually understood as a simple act of magical control over nature. Instead, it reflected a broader belief that human conduct, ancestral approval and communal order affected environmental well-being. Rain rituals were often communal affairs involving chiefs, ritual specialists and ordinary villagers. Songs, prayers, offerings and ceremonial gatherings expressed the hope that harmony between the visible and invisible worlds would be restored.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S1011-76012016000200003&amp;script=sci_arttext" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scielo.org.za">[scielo.org.za]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scielo.org.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Missionary colonial mentality and the expansion of...by F Nkomazana · 2016 · Cited by 97 — Belief in Modimo manifested itself in many Ts...</span></span></span>

The importance of rain can still be seen in modern Botswana's national culture. The Setswana word for rain, *pula*, became a national symbol and the name of the country's currency, reflecting the enduring association between rainfall and prosperity. Although modern meteorology explains drought differently, the cultural significance of rain remains deeply rooted in historical memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/791041882/W3-Livingston-Rainmaking-and-Other-Forgotten-Things" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rainmaking and Water Crisis in Botswana &#124; PDF &#124; AgricultureTraditional rainmaking practices in Botswana centered on a socially inte...</span></span></span>

## Drought, Survival and Social Order

Traditional rainmaking was closely connected to leadership. Chiefs were expected not only to govern but also to maintain the moral and ritual health of the community. A failure of rainfall could therefore raise questions about whether social obligations had been neglected or whether ancestors had become displeased. Historical studies of Tswana societies note that rainmaking rituals reinforced the authority of leaders while also making them accountable to communal expectations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=112947255" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[africabib.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Afric ABib Rainmaking and Community in a Tswana Village, c</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PS Landau · 1993 · Cited by 37 — An example is the Komana cult, which came to prominence in the village of Moremi during the 1...</span></span></span>

This connection between weather and governance created a powerful social mechanism. Drought was rarely viewed as an isolated natural disaster. It could be interpreted as evidence of broken relationships, ritual neglect, conflict within the community or failures of leadership. Rainmaking ceremonies therefore served practical and symbolic purposes at the same time: they expressed collective anxiety, encouraged cooperation and reaffirmed social bonds during periods of environmental stress.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/446e/bf5d48795374f1bd7bec5d4f79c3e40cd167.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pdfs.semanticscholar.org">[semanticscholar.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pdfs.semanticscholar.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Semantic ScholarRainmaking rituals: Song and dance for climate change in...by M Ombati · 2017 · Cited by 40 — The rainmaking prayers are...</span></span></span>

Historical records show that rainmaking traditions adapted rather than disappearing when communities faced new crises. During severe droughts in the twentieth century, new ritual movements and renewed interest in rainmaking sometimes emerged, demonstrating the resilience of these beliefs even in changing political and religious circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=112947255" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricABib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Afric ABib Rainmaking and Community in a Tswana Village, c</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PS Landau · 1993 · Cited by 37 — An example is the Komana cult, which came to prominence in the village of Moremi during the 1...</span></span></span>

## Ancestors, Divination and Misfortune

At the centre of Tswana traditional religion stands the belief that ancestors remain involved in the lives of their descendants. Ancestors were not generally imagined as distant gods but as continuing members of the family and community who could influence health, fertility, success and misfortune. Maintaining respectful relationships with them was therefore considered essential.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4651463/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[nih.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Central Understanding traditional African healing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It is therefore the aim of this paper to clarify this relationship by...Read more...</span></span></span>

When illness, repeated bad luck or unexplained problems occurred, people often sought explanations beyond immediate physical causes. Divination offered a way to investigate hidden reasons for misfortune. Traditional specialists interpreted signs, dreams or divinatory systems to determine whether ancestral displeasure, neglected obligations, interpersonal conflict or harmful spiritual influences might be involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bulletin.hds.harvard.edu/the-silent-voices-of-african-divination/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bulletin.hds.harvard.edu">[harvard.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bulletin.hds.harvard.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Harvard Divinity BulletinThe Silent Voices of African Divination &#124; Harvard Divinity BulletinIn African divination systems, this cross-wor...</span></span></span>

In this worldview, misfortune was frequently understood in relational terms. Questions were asked not only about what had happened but also about why it had happened to a particular person or family at a particular moment. Explanations could involve neglected ancestral duties, jealousy, social tensions or accusations of witchcraft. Such interpretations helped communities make sense of suffering within a wider moral framework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sahistory.org.za/sites/default/files/archive-files/james_denbow_phenyo_c._thebe_culture_and_custombook4you.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sahistory.org.za">[sahistory.org.za]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sahistory.org.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">South African History OnlineCulture and Customs of Botswanathought to be associated in some way with witchcraft, evil spirits, spurned an...</span></span></span>

This does not mean every illness was attributed to supernatural causes. Rather, ancestral and spiritual explanations existed alongside practical remedies, herbal knowledge and observation of the natural world. The boundaries between medicine, religion and social healing were often less rigid than modern categories suggest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4651463/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Central Understanding traditional African healing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It is therefore the aim of this paper to clarify this relationship by...Read more...</span></span></span>

## The Role of Traditional Healers

Traditional healers occupied an important position within Tswana society because they were expected to address problems that crossed physical, social and spiritual boundaries. Their work could include herbal treatment, diagnosis through divination, protection against perceived harmful influences and guidance on restoring harmony with ancestors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4651463/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[nih.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Central Understanding traditional African healing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It is therefore the aim of this paper to clarify this relationship by...Read more...</span></span></span>

The healer's authority rested not only on knowledge of medicines but also on recognised relationships with the ancestral realm. Dreams, visions, family traditions and specialised training could all contribute to a healer's status. Communities often consulted such practitioners when conventional explanations seemed insufficient or when a problem appeared linked to deeper social or spiritual concerns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.studocu.com/en-za/document/university-of-cape-town/religions-past-and-present/dennis-1978-the-role-of-dingaka-tsa-setswana-from-the-19th-century-to-the-present/76219916" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: studocu.com">[studocu.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">studocu.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">detect the source of suspected sorcery; c) practised as a witchdoctor and supplied witchcraft.Read more...</span></span></span>

Traditional healing also functioned as a form of social mediation. By identifying sources of conflict, prescribing rituals of reconciliation or encouraging renewed respect for family obligations, healers could help repair strained relationships. ## Christianity and Continuing Traditional Practice

From the nineteenth century onward, Christian missionaries challenged many aspects of Tswana religious life, including rainmaking rituals and the authority of traditional healers. Some missionaries regarded these practices as incompatible with Christianity and sought to replace them with Christian forms of worship and prayer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S1011-76012016000200003&amp;script=sci_arttext" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scielo.org.za">[scielo.org.za]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scielo.org.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Missionary colonial mentality and the expansion of...by F Nkomazana · 2016 · Cited by 97 — Belief in Modimo manifested itself in many Ts...</span></span></span>
