## Introduction

Among the most striking ritual objects in [Guinea-Bissau]({{ 'where-spirits-live-in-guinea-bissau/' | relative_url }}) are the animal masks of the Bijagó people of the Bijagós Archipelago. Far more than decorative carvings, these masks play a role in initiation ceremonies that mark the passage from youth to adulthood. In public performances, young initiates become oxen, buffaloes or other powerful creatures, acting out lessons about strength, self-control, courage and social responsibility. The masks transform abstract ideas into visible drama: the community can literally watch a young person learn what it means to become an adult. These ceremonies are among the best-documented examples of ritual art in Guinea-Bissau and remain an important symbol of Bijagó cultural identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/ko-KR/objects/148087" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brooklynmuseum.org">[brooklynmuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brooklynmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brooklyn MuseumOx Mask (Dugn&#x27;be)Masks representing dugn&#x27;be, meaning “the ox raised in the village,” are used in young men&#x27;s initiation ce...</span></span></span>

## What Do Bijago Animal Masks Mean?

The Bijagó are renowned for zoomorphic, or animal-shaped, masks. Unlike many West African masking traditions that emphasise human ancestors or spirits, Bijagó masks often represent animals associated with particular qualities or forces. Cattle, sharks, rays and other creatures appear in ritual performances connected to initiation and communal celebrations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://umma.umich.edu/objects/mask-vaca-bruto-dugnbe-type-1984-1-273/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: umma.umich.edu">[umich.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">umma.umich.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of Michigan Museum of ArtArt Object: Mask (vaca bruto, dugn&#x27;be type)Bidjogo masking traditions are distinguished by their robu...</span></span></span>

Animal imagery is not chosen at random. The islands' culture has long been shaped by close relationships with both the sea and the land. Marine animals reflect the surrounding ocean environment, while cattle masks embody ideas of power, endurance and social status. Through masks, these animals become symbols that help teach younger generations how to behave within the community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://umma.umich.edu/objects/mask-vaca-bruto-dugnbe-type-1984-1-273/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: umma.umich.edu">[umich.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">umma.umich.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of Michigan Museum of ArtArt Object: Mask (vaca bruto, dugn&#x27;be type)Bidjogo masking traditions are distinguished by their robu...</span></span></span>

The masks are not merely displayed. They are activated through dance, movement and performance. During ceremonies, participants imitate the behaviour of the animals represented, turning the mask into a living lesson rather than a static object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zyama.com/bijago/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zyama.com">[Zyama]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zyama.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guinea-Bissau - African art museumThe masks are danced by boys and young men during the ceremonies that precede and follow the phase...</span></span></span>

## Ox and Buffalo Masks in Initiation

The best-known Bijagó initiation masks are the cattle masks often called ox, bull or buffalo masks. Museums in Europe and North America preserve numerous examples collected during the twentieth century, while ethnographic accounts describe their continued ceremonial importance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/ko-KR/objects/148087" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brooklynmuseum.org">[brooklynmuseum.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brooklynmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brooklyn MuseumOx Mask (Dugn&#x27;be)Masks representing dugn&#x27;be, meaning “the ox raised in the village,” are used in young men&#x27;s initiation ce...</span></span></span>

One famous type is the *dugn'be* mask, usually translated as the village ox. According to museum documentation, these masks are used in young men's initiation ceremonies. The initiate wears the mask and imitates the movements of a bull, crawling, charging or displaying animal-like behaviour before the community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/ko-KR/objects/148087" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brooklynmuseum.org">[Brooklyn Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brooklynmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brooklyn MuseumOx Mask (Dugn&#x27;be)Masks representing dugn&#x27;be, meaning “the ox raised in the village,” are used in young men&#x27;s initiation ce...</span></span></span>

Several forms of cattle mask are known. Some represent domesticated oxen, while others evoke wilder bovine animals. Real horns are often incorporated into the construction, creating a naturalistic appearance. Glass eyes, fibre skirts, bells and raffia elements add movement and sound to the performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dispatch.happyvalley.com/blog/cattle-masks-from-bissagos-islands-are-new-on-view-at-palmer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dispatch.happyvalley.com">[happyvalley.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dispatch.happyvalley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cattle masks from bissagos islands are new on view at palmer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Adventure Bureau DispatchCattle Masks from Bissagos Islands are New on View at Palmer4 Mar 2025 — The initiate wears a fiber skirt, b...</span></span></span>

The dramatic performance is central to the ritual. Observers do not simply see a masked dancer; they see a young person temporarily transformed into a powerful and potentially dangerous animal. The challenge is learning how that power should be managed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/ko-KR/objects/148087" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brooklynmuseum.org">[Brooklyn Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brooklynmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brooklyn MuseumOx Mask (Dugn&#x27;be)Masks representing dugn&#x27;be, meaning “the ox raised in the village,” are used in young men&#x27;s initiation ce...</span></span></span>

## Strength, Control and Adulthood

The symbolism of cattle masks reveals the deeper purpose of initiation. An ox is strong, energetic and useful, but it can also be difficult to control. This dual meaning appears explicitly in descriptions of the *dugn'be* mask. A rope passing through the mask's nostrils resembles the tether used to lead an ox, suggesting that the initiate's strength must be guided rather than allowed to become destructive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/ko-KR/objects/148087" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brooklynmuseum.org">[Brooklyn Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brooklynmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brooklyn MuseumOx Mask (Dugn&#x27;be)Masks representing dugn&#x27;be, meaning “the ox raised in the village,” are used in young men&#x27;s initiation ce...</span></span></span>

This symbolism helps explain why cattle occupy such an important place in initiation performances. Adolescence is often understood as a period of raw energy and unpredictability. By acting as a bull before elders, relatives and fellow initiates, the young person publicly demonstrates both power and the need for discipline. The ritual turns a personal transition into a communal lesson.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dispatch.happyvalley.com/blog/cattle-masks-from-bissagos-islands-are-new-on-view-at-palmer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dispatch.happyvalley.com">[The Adventure Bureau Dispatch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dispatch.happyvalley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cattle masks from bissagos islands are new on view at palmer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Adventure Bureau DispatchCattle Masks from Bissagos Islands are New on View at Palmer4 Mar 2025 — The initiate wears a fiber skirt, b...</span></span></span>

Some ethnographic interpretations describe a stage in which initiates celebrate the untamed qualities of youth before being gradually integrated into adult society. The aggressive behaviour associated with the bull is therefore not simply encouraged; it is displayed, controlled and ultimately brought within accepted social limits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dispatch.happyvalley.com/blog/cattle-masks-from-bissagos-islands-are-new-on-view-at-palmer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dispatch.happyvalley.com">[The Adventure Bureau Dispatch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dispatch.happyvalley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cattle masks from bissagos islands are new on view at palmer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Adventure Bureau DispatchCattle Masks from Bissagos Islands are New on View at Palmer4 Mar 2025 — The initiate wears a fiber skirt, b...</span></span></span>

The result is a ritual drama about adulthood itself. Physical strength matters, but maturity requires restraint, responsibility and obedience to communal norms. The mask makes those values visible in a way that speeches or instructions alone could not achieve.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/ko-KR/objects/148087" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brooklynmuseum.org">[Brooklyn Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brooklynmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brooklyn MuseumOx Mask (Dugn&#x27;be)Masks representing dugn&#x27;be, meaning “the ox raised in the village,” are used in young men&#x27;s initiation ce...</span></span></span>

## Why Animal Performance Matters

To outside observers, the masks may appear to be works of art. Within initiation ceremonies, however, their importance lies in performance. The movements, sounds and interactions surrounding the mask communicate meanings that cannot be separated from the object itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zyama.com/bijago/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zyama.com">[Zyama]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zyama.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guinea-Bissau - African art museumThe masks are danced by boys and young men during the ceremonies that precede and follow the phase...</span></span></span>

Boys and young men traditionally perform many of these masked dances during ceremonies before and after key initiation stages. Through repeated participation, initiates learn expected behaviours while spectators witness the continuity of community traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/100083510270146/posts/traditional-dance-and-mask-bijagos-islandsguinea-bissau-/290939053699768/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Traditional dance and mask, Bijagos Islands. Guinea...The masks are danced by boys and young men during the ceremonies that pre...</span></span></span>

This helps explain why many Bijagó masks look unusually naturalistic compared with some other African masking traditions. Their purpose is not only symbolic representation but also convincing animal imitation.
