## Introduction

Pchum Ben is [Cambodia]({{ 'where-cambodias-legends-still-live/' | relative_url }})’s great festival of the dead, a fifteen-day season during which families honour ancestors, make offerings at pagodas, and seek to help souls believed to be suffering between rebirths. Often translated as “Ancestors’ Day” or the “Festival of the Dead”, it combines Theravada Buddhist teachings about karma and merit with older Cambodian ideas about family duty, remembrance and the uneasy presence of the dead. Rather than treating death as a distant event, Pchum Ben brings ancestors back into the centre of community life. Across the country, people return to their home villages, gather with relatives, visit temples before dawn and make offerings intended to reach both loved ones and forgotten spirits. It is one of the most important ritual seasons in Cambodia and one of the clearest examples of how folklore, religion and family memory continue to overlap in everyday life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vietnamnews.vn/world/1664128/cambodians-celebrate-pchum-ben-festival.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vietnamnews.vn">[vietnamnews.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vietnamnews.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cambodians celebrate pchum ben festival</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2 Oct 2024 — Pchum Ben, or the Festival of the Ancestors, one of the biggest and most important festivals in Cambodia, is being celebrate...</span></span></span>

## The 15-day ancestor festival

Pchum Ben takes place during the tenth month of the Khmer lunar calendar and traditionally lasts fifteen days. The first fourteen days are often known as Kan Ben, a period when families take turns bringing food and offerings to local pagodas. The festival culminates on the fifteenth day, the most important moment of the observance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vietnamnews.vn/world/1664128/cambodians-celebrate-pchum-ben-festival.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vietnamnews.vn">[vietnamnews.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vietnamnews.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cambodians celebrate pchum ben festival</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2 Oct 2024 — Pchum Ben, or the Festival of the Ancestors, one of the biggest and most important festivals in Cambodia, is being celebrate...</span></span></span>

The structure of the festival helps explain why it occupies such a powerful place in Cambodian culture. It is not a single holiday but a ritual season. Throughout the fifteen days, people travel between temples, participate in chanting ceremonies, and make offerings on behalf of deceased relatives. Many traditions hold that ancestors from as many as seven generations may benefit from these acts of devotion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visit-angkor.org/blog/pchum-ben-ancestors-day-history-legend/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visit-angkor.org">[visit-angkor.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visit-angkor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visit Angkor Pchum Ben</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit AngkorPchum Ben - Ancestors&#x27; Day, history and legendA 15-day Cambodian religious festival that culminates in the Khmer calendar on...</span></span></span>

For many Cambodians, the festival is also a time of homecoming. Family members working in cities often return to ancestral villages, creating a nationwide movement of reunion and remembrance. The result is a ritual calendar that links living relatives, dead ancestors and local religious communities into a single network of obligations and memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://berkleycenter.georgetown.edu/posts/nourishing-spirit-nourishing-nation-feasts-for-the-living-and-the-dead-at-the-pchum-ben-festival" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berkleycenter.georgetown.edu">[georgetown.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berkleycenter.georgetown.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Many Cambodians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berkley CenterFeasts for the living and the dead at the Pchum Ben FestivalOct 23, 2012 — Pchum Ben Festival is particularly important as...</span></span></span>

## Hungry ghosts, merit and family duty

At the heart of Pchum Ben lies a powerful story about spirits who cannot easily find peace. Cambodian explanations often draw on Buddhist ideas about hungry ghosts—beings trapped by the consequences of past actions and condemned to states of hunger, deprivation or wandering. During Pchum Ben, these spirits are believed to gain a rare opportunity to receive help from the living.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wondersofcambodia.com/pchum-ben-cambodias-festival-of-ancestors/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wondersofcambodia.com">[wondersofcambodia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wondersofcambodia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pchum ben cambodias festival of ancestors</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wonders of CambodiaPchum Ben: Cambodia&#x27;s Festival of AncestorsMar 6, 2026 — Among them are the “preta” or “hungry ghosts,” tormented spir...</span></span></span>

This belief gives the festival much of its emotional force. Offerings are not made only for honoured ancestors who are remembered fondly. They are also directed towards forgotten souls, relatives who died without descendants, wandering spirits and those believed to be suffering in unfortunate states of rebirth. In many popular explanations, a spirit that receives no offerings may continue to suffer, while prayers and gifts offered through the pagoda can improve its condition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://khmer.voanews.com/a/annual-ceremonies-honor-ancestors-and-wandering-spirits/2984026.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: khmer.voanews.com">[voanews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">khmer.voanews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">វីអូអេAnnual Ceremonies Honor Ancestors and Wandering SpiritsSep 29, 2015 — Cambodia&#x27;s annual ritual of Pchum Bun has begun, in a 15-day...</span></span></span>

The mechanism is usually described through the Buddhist concept of merit. Rather than physically feeding ghosts in a literal sense, merit generated through generosity, prayer and support for the monastic community is transferred to the dead. Yet Cambodian popular tradition often adds a more vivid image: food is also imagined as reaching hungry spirits directly. Anthropologists have noted that these two understandings—the formal Buddhist transfer of merit and the folk image of literally feeding the dead—exist side by side.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pchum_Ben" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pchum Ben</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pchum Ben</span></span></span>

This combination of religious doctrine and folk imagination is one reason Pchum Ben occupies such an important place in Cambodian belief culture. It transforms remembrance into an obligation. Caring for ancestors is not simply an act of affection; it becomes a moral duty extending across generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sereyrathlearning.wordpress.com/2024/10/01/exploring-the-cultural-significance-of-cambodian-pchum-ben-days-traditions-rituals-and-historical-context/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sereyrathlearning.wordpress.com">[wordpress.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sereyrathlearning.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">English Academic Essay (EAE)Exploring the Cultural Significance of Cambodian Pchum Ben...Oct 1, 2024 — Pchum Ben is recognized as a vita...</span></span></span>

## How pagodas become places of memory

During Pchum Ben, pagodas become the centre of spiritual and social life. Before dawn, people arrive carrying food, flowers and offerings. Monks chant prayers, families gather together, and temple grounds fill with activity long before sunrise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://khmer.voanews.com/a/annual-ceremonies-honor-ancestors-and-wandering-spirits/2984026.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: khmer.voanews.com">[voanews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">khmer.voanews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">វីអូអេAnnual Ceremonies Honor Ancestors and Wandering SpiritsSep 29, 2015 — Cambodia&#x27;s annual ritual of Pchum Bun has begun, in a 15-day...</span></span></span>

One of the festival’s most striking customs involves the offering of rice. In many places, worshippers scatter rice or rice balls around temple grounds in the darkness of early morning. According to popular belief, wandering spirits may be unable to enter formal ceremonies or receive ordinary offerings, so food is cast into the darkness where they can find it. This dramatic act has become one of the most recognisable images associated with Pchum Ben.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mekong.net/cambodia/pchum.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mekong.net">[mekong.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mekong.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Feeding the Ancestors: The Cambodian Pchum Ben FestivalAt four in the morning, nearly all of the residents of Phnom Penh gather at...</span></span></span>

The pagoda serves another role as well: it acts as a meeting place between memory and community. Families bring the names and stories of deceased relatives into a shared public space. Monks perform rituals that connect individual grief with collective religious practice. The dead are remembered not privately but through participation in a wider community of worshippers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://berkleycenter.georgetown.edu/posts/nourishing-spirit-nourishing-nation-feasts-for-the-living-and-the-dead-at-the-pchum-ben-festival" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berkleycenter.georgetown.edu">[georgetown.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berkleycenter.georgetown.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Many Cambodians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berkley CenterFeasts for the living and the dead at the Pchum Ben FestivalOct 23, 2012 — Pchum Ben Festival is particularly important as...</span></span></span>

In some traditions, families attempt to visit multiple pagodas during the festival because wandering spirits are believed to search different temples for offerings. Whether understood literally or symbolically, the practice reinforces the idea that care for ancestors requires effort, generosity and participation in communal religious life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mekongtourism.org/events/pchum-ben-cambodia-2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mekongtourism.org">[Mekong Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mekongtourism.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mekong TourismPchum Ben, CambodiaPchum Ben: A 15-day Khmer festival honoring ancestors, feeding hungry ghosts, visiting seven pagodas, an...</span></span></span>

## Folklore, Buddhism and the uneasy dead

Pchum Ben illustrates a recurring pattern in Cambodian folklore: Buddhist teachings and older spirit traditions rarely exist in isolation. The festival is rooted in Buddhist concepts of rebirth and merit, yet much of its popular imagery comes from stories about ghosts, wandering souls and ancestral presences that remain close to the living world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wondersofcambodia.com/pchum-ben-cambodias-festival-of-ancestors/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wondersofcambodia.com">[wondersofcambodia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wondersofcambodia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pchum ben cambodias festival of ancestors</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wonders of CambodiaPchum Ben: Cambodia&#x27;s Festival of AncestorsMar 6, 2026 — Among them are the “preta” or “hungry ghosts,” tormented spir...</span></span></span>

This is why the festival often appears in discussions of Cambodian ghost beliefs. The spirits honoured during Pchum Ben are not necessarily terrifying monsters. They are familiar dead—parents, grandparents, forgotten relatives and lonely souls who still require recognition. The emotional tone is therefore different from many Western ghost traditions.
