## Introduction

Among the frightening beings of Kazakh folklore, none is more famous than Zhalmauyz Kempir, a terrifying witch-crone whose name is often understood as something close to “the devouring old woman”. In folktales she appears as a man-eater, a kidnapper, a shape-shifter and a supernatural enemy who tests heroes through cunning rather than brute force. Yet she is more than a simple fairy-tale villain. Her stories preserve traces of older Turkic spirit beliefs, ideas about dangerous female supernatural power, and the long blending of steppe traditions with Islamic language and imagery. In [Kazakhstan]({{ 'why-kazakhstans-folklore-still-sings/' | relative_url }})’s oral tradition, Zhalmauyz Kempir stands at the centre of a wider world of hostile spirits that haunted lonely roads, childbirth, wilderness and the uncertain edges of human life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/232125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dergipark.org.tr">[dergipark.org.tr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dergipark.org.tr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dergi Park CHARACTER OF ZHALMAUYZ IN THE FOLKLORE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CHARACTER OF ZHALMAUYZ IN THE FOLKLORE...August 24, 2016 — by P Auyesbayeva · Cited by 3 — Finally, we can say that the image o...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 24, 2016</span></span></span>

## Who Is Zhalmauyz Kempir in Kazakh Folklore?

Zhalmauyz Kempir is usually described as an old woman of monstrous appearance. Depending on the tale, she may have a single eye, iron-like claws, supernatural strength, multiple heads, or the ability to change shape. Many stories portray her as a cannibal who hunts travellers and children, while others emphasise her magical knowledge and ability to deceive. She is one of the best-known evil figures in Kazakh storytelling and appears throughout the folklore of the wider Turkic world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhalmauyz_Kempir" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zhalmauyz Kempir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">December 24, 2024 — Zhalmauyz Kempir is described as a witch with magic power who can change her shape at will. The figure is often compared to the Slavic witch Baba Yaga because both appear as frightening old women who live outside ordinary society. However, Kazakh traditions developed their own versions of the character. Folklore researchers note that Zhalmauyz Kempir belongs to a much broader Turkic family of supernatural female beings and cannot simply be treated as a local copy of a Russian folk character.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eurasia-science.org/index.php/pub/article/view/385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eurasia-science.org">[eurasia-science.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eurasia-science.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zhalmauyz Kempir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Female Image in Kazakh Fairy...by B Rysalieva · 2025 — This research work is dedicated to the study of the female image of the chara...</span></span></span>

Her name itself hints at her role. Modern explanations commonly connect the first element with devouring or swallowing, while “kempir” means an old woman or crone. The result is a figure whose identity is tied directly to hunger, predation and danger.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://astanatimes.com/2025/03/kazakh-fairy-tales-heroes-villains-and-timeless-legends/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astanatimes.com">[astanatimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astanatimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">kazakh fairy tales heroes villains and timeless legends</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Astana TimesKazakh Fairy Tales: Heroes, Villains, and Timeless LegendsMar 25, 2025 — Every fairy tale needs a formidable villain, and...</span></span></span>

## The Devouring Witch-Crone in Turkic Tales

Many Kazakh fairy tales use Zhalmauyz Kempir as the obstacle standing between a hero and success. She may imprison captives, lure travellers into traps, steal children or attempt to consume human victims. Some descriptions depict her cooking prisoners in giant cauldrons, while others present her as a blood-drinking or flesh-eating monster.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://qalam.global/en/articles/women-in-kazakh-mythology-en-3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: qalam.global">[qalam.global]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">qalam.global</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WITCH - Qalam GlobalSep 29, 2023 — Jalmauyz Kempir. Yet the character is not always completely one-dimensional. In several Turkic narratives, including variants connected with heroic tales, an old supernatural woman identified as Zhalmauyz Kempir can also provide advice, reveal hidden knowledge or guide a hero toward magical helpers. This ambiguity suggests that the figure may preserve memories of older mythological roles that were not purely evil.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarlik_and_Munglik" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zarlik and Munglik</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zarlik and Munglik</span></span></span>

Folklore scholars have argued that the character changed over time. Studies of Turkic traditions suggest that earlier forms may have been linked to ancient supernatural beings or spirits before evolving into the familiar witch-crone of later fairy tales. Some researchers describe a long transformation from a more abstract mythological force into the recognisable old woman who dominates modern retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/232125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dergipark.org.tr">[dergipark.org.tr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dergipark.org.tr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dergi Park CHARACTER OF ZHALMAUYZ IN THE FOLKLORE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CHARACTER OF ZHALMAUYZ IN THE FOLKLORE...August 24, 2016 — by P Auyesbayeva · Cited by 3 — Finally, we can say that the image o...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 24, 2016</span></span></span>

This evolution helps explain why the same character can appear as both a terrifying monster and a keeper of hidden knowledge. Folktales often preserve layers from different periods rather than a single original meaning.

### Why So Many Female Monsters?

Modern readers sometimes wonder why so many dangerous spirits in Turkic folklore are female. Folklore specialists caution against reading these stories as simple reflections of real women. Instead, these beings often represent supernatural forces associated with birth, death, fertility, wilderness and transitions between worlds. Some scholars interpret them as echoes of very old mythological structures in which powerful female entities controlled access to life, knowledge or the realm of the dead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tsj.enu.kz/index.php/new/article/view/618" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tsj.enu.kz">[enu.kz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tsj.enu.kz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Turkic Studies Journalthe role and symbolism of Zhalmauyz Kempir, Albasty...by A Nurbayeva · 2025 — This article examines female demonic...</span></span></span>

In this reading, Zhalmauyz Kempir is frightening precisely because she occupies a space between human and spirit, civilisation and wilderness, life and death.

## Shamanic Traces Beneath the Stories

Kazakh folklore developed within a cultural world that long included Turkic spirit beliefs and shamanic practices. Before and after the spread of Islam, ritual specialists known as baqsy interacted with a universe populated by spirits, ancestors and unseen forces. This background helps explain some unusual features of Zhalmauyz Kempir. In certain interpretations she resembles a guardian of the boundary between worlds, a role found in many ancient mythologies. ## Islamic Language and Older Steppe Belief

As Islam spread across the Kazakh steppe, older supernatural traditions were rarely erased outright. Instead, they were often reinterpreted. Folklore and popular belief came to combine Islamic terminology with much older ideas about spirits, sacred places and unseen forces. Scholars studying Kazakh religious culture regularly describe this process as a blending or synthesis rather than a complete replacement of earlier traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ejst.tuiasi.ro/Files/54/20_Moldagaliyev%20et%20al.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ejst.tuiasi.ro">[tuiasi.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ejst.tuiasi.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-title">20 Moldagaliyev et al</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Islam, in the form of. Zhalmauyz Kempir therefore belongs to a tradition that is neither purely Islamic nor purely pre-Islamic. She survives in a cultural landscape where both influences coexist.

## How Zhalmauyz Kempir Is Understood Today

Today, Zhalmauyz Kempir remains one of the most recognisable figures in Kazakh folk culture. She appears in children’s adaptations of fairy tales, academic studies of Turkic mythology, contemporary art projects and popular retellings of traditional stories. Modern presentations often describe her as Kazakhstan’s classic witch figure, but scholars continue to explore the older mythological layers hidden beneath that familiar image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://astanatimes.com/2025/03/kazakh-fairy-tales-heroes-villains-and-timeless-legends/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astanatimes.com">[astanatimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astanatimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">kazakh fairy tales heroes villains and timeless legends</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Astana TimesKazakh Fairy Tales: Heroes, Villains, and Timeless LegendsMar 25, 2025 — Every fairy tale needs a formidable villain, and...</span></span></span>
