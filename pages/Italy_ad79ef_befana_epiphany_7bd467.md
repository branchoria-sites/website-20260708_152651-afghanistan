## Introduction

Why does Befana visit Italian children? According to one of [Italy]({{ 'italy/' | relative_url }})’s best-loved folk traditions, an elderly woman riding a broomstick flies across the country on the night of 5 January, leaving sweets, small gifts, or symbolic coal in children’s stockings before the Feast of the Epiphany on 6 January. To many Italians, she is as familiar as Father Christmas, yet she belongs to a distinctly Italian blend of folklore, seasonal ritual and Christian storytelling. The Befana tradition matters because it preserves layers of belief stretching from religious narratives about the Three Wise Men to older ideas about winter renewal, household fortune and the turning of the year. Today she remains one of the few folk figures recognised across almost all of Italy, appearing in family celebrations, public festivals, markets and popular culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.italia.it/en/italy/things-to-do/epiphany-in-italy-befana-meaning-and-traditions" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: italia.it">[italia.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">italia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Epiphany in Italy: Befana meaning and traditions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">about the history and origins of the Befana (old witch) and the main events for the Epiphany in keeping with Italian traditions.Read more...</span></span></span>

## The Epiphany Night Story

The most widespread legend explains Befana through the Christian story of the Epiphany. According to tradition, the Three Wise Men stopped at the house of an elderly woman while searching for the infant Jesus. They invited her to join their journey, but she declined because she was busy with household tasks. Later she regretted her decision, gathered gifts and set out to find the child. Unable to locate him, she began leaving presents for every child she encountered, hoping one might be Jesus himself. In folk retellings, she continues that search every year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://orderisda.org/culture/la-nostra-voce/the-story-of-la-befana/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: orderisda.org">[orderisda.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">orderisda.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the story of la befana</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Italian Sons and Daughters of AmericaThe Story of La Befana27 Nov 2022 — Tradition has it that La Befana, an old woman of modest means, l...</span></span></span>

The figure is easy to recognise. Befana is usually portrayed as a very old woman in worn clothing, travelling on a broomstick and entering homes through the chimney. Unlike many witches of European folklore, she is generally benevolent. Good children receive sweets, nuts, chocolates or small toys, while naughty children may find symbolic coal. Modern versions often use black-coloured sugar candy instead of real coal, turning punishment into another treat.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/befana/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[treccani.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Befana - Significato ed etimologia - VocabolarioPersonificazione dell&#x27;Epifania: la vecchia, bruttissima ma benefica, che di notte...</span></span></span>

Several household customs surround her visit. Children hang stockings before going to bed, much as they might prepare for Father Christmas elsewhere. In some traditions families leave food or drink for the visitor, and folk explanations sometimes describe her sweeping the house before leaving, symbolically clearing away the troubles of the old year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.italiarail.com/culture/legend-la-befana" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: italiarail.com">[italiarail.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">italiarail.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">legend la befana</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The legend of La Befana20 Dec 2019 — Befana, a grandmotherly woman who resembles a kindly witch, brings gifts to good little ch...</span></span></span>

## Older Winter and Harvest Layers

Although the Christian legend is the best-known explanation, historians and folklorists have long noted that Befana probably combines several traditions rather than emerging from a single source. Even official Italian cultural explanations describe the Epiphany celebrations as a meeting point between Christian observance and older seasonal customs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.italia.it/en/italy/things-to-do/epiphany-in-italy-befana-meaning-and-traditions" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: italia.it">[Italia.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">italia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Epiphany in Italy: Befana meaning and traditions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">about the history and origins of the Befana (old witch) and the main events for the Epiphany in keeping with Italian traditions.Read more...</span></span></span>

One clue lies in her appearance. Befana is often interpreted as a personification of the old year itself: an elderly woman who arrives at the end of the Christmas season and then disappears as a new cycle begins. In many European folk traditions, winter festivals involve symbolic figures representing the passing year, fertility, renewal or the transition between seasons. Befana fits comfortably within this broader pattern while remaining distinctly Italian.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citylightstours.com/blog/la-befana-in-italy-epiphany-traditions-folklore-and-festivities" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citylightstours.com">[citylightstours.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citylightstours.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">City Lights ToursLa Befana in Italy: Epiphany traditions, folklore and festivities6 Jan 2026 — In Italian folklore, La Befana is a famili...</span></span></span>

Another clue is the timing. Epiphany falls twelve days after Christmas, a period that many European folk cultures treated as a liminal season when ordinary rules were loosened and supernatural visitors were expected. Befana belongs to a wider family of gift-bringers who appear during the winter holidays, rewarding good behaviour and marking the transition from one year to the next.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Befana" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Some local customs preserve agricultural themes that seem older than the familiar story of the Magi. In parts of southern Italy, Epiphany traditions include songs, blessings and rituals connected to hopes for a successful farming year. Calabria, for example, preserves traditions linking the season to good fortune, harvest expectations and legends about animals speaking on Epiphany night.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.italia.it/en/calabria/things-to-do/epiphany-in-calabria" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: italia.it">[Italia.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">italia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Epiphany celebrations in Calabria</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ItalyCelebrate Epiphany in Calabria: meet the Befana in the villages of Reggio Calabria, Soverato and Tropea, with entertainment, games a...</span></span></span>

Because evidence for early origins is fragmentary, scholars remain cautious. Claims that Befana comes directly from a specific ancient Roman goddess or a single pagan festival are difficult to prove. The strongest conclusion is that the modern figure developed through centuries of interaction between Christian Epiphany celebrations and older seasonal customs rather than from one identifiable source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.italia.it/en/italy/things-to-do/epiphany-in-italy-befana-meaning-and-traditions" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: italia.it">[italia.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">italia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Epiphany in Italy: Befana meaning and traditions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">about the history and origins of the Befana (old witch) and the main events for the Epiphany in keeping with Italian traditions.Read more...</span></span></span>

## How a Household Visitor Became a National Icon

Many folk figures remain local, but Befana achieved something unusual: she became recognised across most of Italy. Her name itself is widely understood as a popular linguistic transformation of the word “Epiphany,” showing how closely the character became attached to the feast day.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/befana/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Befana - Significato ed etimologia - VocabolarioPersonificazione dell&#x27;Epifania: la vecchia, bruttissima ma benefica, che di notte...</span></span></span>

The tradition spread through family practice rather than through a single official institution. Children learned the stories from parents and grandparents, schools marked the holiday, and local communities organised celebrations. By the twentieth century, Befana had become one of the country's most familiar seasonal characters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Befana" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Public celebrations helped reinforce that status. Rome’s Piazza Navona became famous for Epiphany markets selling sweets, toys and Befana-themed gifts. Elsewhere, towns developed their own distinctive festivals, combining folklore, entertainment and religious observance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Befana" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Urbania and Other Living Traditions

No place is more closely associated with Befana today than the town of Urbania in the Marche region. Modern tourism branding has even promoted it as the “home” or “capital” of the Befana. Each year the town hosts a large Epiphany festival featuring costumed performers, parades, family events and public appearances by hundreds of Befana figures. The celebration attracts visitors from across Italy and demonstrates how a folk tradition can become both cultural heritage and contemporary spectacle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.italia.it/en/marche/things-to-do/befana-history-and-festival-in-urbania" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: italia.it">[Italia.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">italia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Befana festival in Urbania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Epiphany in Italy - Italia.itDiscover the Epiphany tradition in Italy and the history of Befana... Yet Urbania is only one example. Across Italy, local versions of the celebration continue to reflect regional identities. In Tuscany, Calabria, Lazio and other regions, Befana appears in parades, children's events, religious festivals and charity activities. The details vary, but the core story remains recognisable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visittuscany.com/en/ideas/who-is-la-befana/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visittuscany.com">[visittuscany.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visittuscany.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit TuscanyWho is La Befana?According to Italian tradition, La Befana is a folklore figure who brings presents to all good children dur...</span></span></span>

## Why Befana Still Matters

Befana survives because she occupies several cultural roles at once. She is a folk character, a moral teacher, a seasonal visitor, a symbol of Epiphany and a reminder that Christmas in Italy traditionally ends on 6 January rather than on New Year's Day.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wantedinrome.com/news/la-befana-an-italian-christmas-tradition.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wantedinrome.com">[Wanted in Rome]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wantedinrome.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wanted in RomeLa Befana: an Epiphany tradition in ItalyItalians celebrate the religious feast of the Epiphany, or the more popular folklo...</span></span></span>

Her popularity also reflects a balance between old and new. Children encounter her through sweets and presents, while adults often recognise deeper themes of memory, renewal and continuity.
