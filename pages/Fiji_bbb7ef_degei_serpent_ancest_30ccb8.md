## Introduction

Degei is one of the most important figures in Fijian traditional belief and one of the clearest examples of how mythology, ancestry, landscape and social order are woven together in Fiji. Often described as a great serpent ancestor, Degei appears in stories about the origins of people, the settlement of the islands, the fertility of the land, earthquakes, sacred mountains and the destiny of souls after death. Unlike many mythological figures who belong only to a distant past, Degei is closely tied to specific places, especially the Nakauvadra mountain range in northern Viti Levu, where generations of Fijians have located his dwelling. Early written accounts, oral traditions and later ethnographic studies all record Degei as a central ancestral being whose influence extended far beyond a single district.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p289571/html/ch06.xhtml?page=12&amp;referer=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: press-files.anu.edu.au">[edu.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">press-files.anu.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Degei&#x27;s Descendants: Spirits, Place and People in Pre...He is cited by Kaplan (1988:152) as saying that &#x27;Degei the Kalouvu or Fijian Cre...</span></span></span>

## The Serpent Ancestor of Nakauvadra

The heart of the Degei tradition lies in the Nakauvadra range near Rakiraki on Viti Levu. In many accounts, this mountainous region is not merely a backdrop but a sacred centre from which ancestors, clans and political groups trace their origins. Oral traditions collected by researchers describe Nakauvadra as a place where ancestral spirits emerged, where important founding figures lived and where Degei himself resided.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Matthew-Spriggs-2/publication/282577594_Degei%27s_Descendants_Spirits_Place_and_People_in_Pre-Cession_Fiji/links/5613132f08aec7900afb10b7/Degeis-Descendants-Spirits-Place-and-People-in-Pre-Cession-Fiji.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">terra australis 41Nakauvadra that many of the original ancestors of Fijian polities had their mythical origin. Degei is usually represented as a gigantic serpent. Some traditions describe him as wholly snake-like, while others allow him to assume human form. Nineteenth-century accounts recorded stories of a cave in the Nakauvadra mountains where Degei dwelt and where visitors occasionally claimed encounters with the sacred being. Such stories helped make the landscape itself part of the tradition: mountains, caves and valleys became evidence of ancestral presence rather than merely physical features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p289571/html/ch06.xhtml?page=12&amp;referer=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: press-files.anu.edu.au">[press-files.anu.edu.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">press-files.anu.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Degei&#x27;s Descendants: Spirits, Place and People in Pre...He is cited by Kaplan (1988:152) as saying that &#x27;Degei the Kalouvu or Fijian Cre...</span></span></span>

The serpent form is especially significant. In many parts of the world, snakes are feared or associated with danger, but in Fijian tradition Degei was often viewed as an ancestral protector and source of life. Researchers have noted that snakes connected with Degei could be regarded as descendants or manifestations of the primordial ancestor, giving the serpent a sacred status within older belief systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Fiji" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Religion in Fiji</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Religion in Fiji</span></span></span>

## Origins, Rank and Sacred Power

One reason Degei occupies such a prominent place in Fijian tradition is that he combines several roles that elsewhere might belong to separate figures.

He is remembered as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* An ancestor connected with the origins of many Fijian groups.
* A creator associated with the formation of the world and the first people.
* A guardian of social and sacred order.
* A force behind fertility, weather and seasonal abundance.
* A judge who determines the fate of the dead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Older ethnographic studies often classified Degei among the most powerful ancestral spirits, sometimes presenting him as the supreme figure in pre-Christian Fijian religion. Modern scholarship tends to be more cautious, noting that Fiji contained many regional traditions and local spiritual powers. Many traditions link Degei with the ancestry of entire communities. Studies of Fijian social organisation note that numerous groups traced descent from shared ancestral spirits connected with Nakauvadra. Through these traditions, Degei became part of a larger framework explaining kinship, alliances and chiefly authority. ## Why Earthquakes and Fertility Were Linked to Degei

One of the most distinctive features of the Degei tradition is the connection between the serpent ancestor and the physical behaviour of the land.

According to many accounts, earthquakes occurred when Degei moved or shifted within his underground dwelling. Thunder, tremors and other dramatic natural events were sometimes interpreted as signs of his activity. The explanation tied unpredictable geological events to a familiar sacred narrative rooted in the local landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Yet Degei was not simply a destructive force. His movements were also associated with fertility and abundance. Traditions describe beneficial rains, productive yam harvests and flourishing crops as signs of his favour. In this way, Degei embodied both the power and uncertainty of nature. The same being capable of causing tremors or floods could also sustain agriculture and prosperity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

This dual role reflects a broader pattern in traditional societies, where supernatural beings often explain both blessing and hardship. Degei's power was not divided into neat categories of good and evil; rather, he represented the living forces that shaped human survival in an island environment dependent on weather, fertile soils and stable harvests.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Degei and the Fate of the Dead

Perhaps the most memorable aspect of Degei's mythology concerns what happens after death.

In many recorded traditions, the soul embarks on a journey through the spirit world before reaching Degei. There the serpent ancestor acts as judge, determining the fate of the deceased. A fortunate minority are allowed to proceed to Burotu, a blessed realm often described as a paradise of abundance and happiness. Others are sent elsewhere, commonly to Murimuria, where their fate reflects the lives they lived.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

This role made Degei more than a creator or ancestor. He became the final authority over human destiny. The judgement traditions also connected morality, social obligations and the afterlife. Although details varied between regions and over time, the basic idea remained powerful: life continued beyond death, and ancestral powers watched over that transition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Importantly, these beliefs were attached to real locations. Some traditions identified specific caves or routes used by spirits on their journey. The landscape of Fiji therefore became both a physical environment and a sacred map linking the living with ancestors and the dead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Changing Interpretations Through Time
