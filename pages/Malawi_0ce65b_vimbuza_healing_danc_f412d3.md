## Introduction

Vimbuza is one of [Malawi]({{ 'where-malawis-spirits-still-tell-stories/' | relative_url }})’s most distinctive healing traditions: a ritual in which illness is understood not only as a physical or psychological problem, but also as a disturbance involving spirits, emotions, social relationships and personal suffering. Practised mainly among the Tumbuka people of northern Malawi, Vimbuza combines drumming, singing, dance and trance as part of a recognised system of diagnosis and treatment. UNESCO describes it as an important example of the wider ng’oma tradition of “drums of affliction” found across Bantu-speaking Africa.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vimbuza-healing-dance-00158" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural HeritageVimbuza healing danceVimbuza is a healing dance popular among the Tumbuka people living in northern Ma...</span></span></span>

To outsiders, Vimbuza may look like a dance performance. Within the communities that practise it, however, it has long been understood as a way of identifying and addressing spirit-related afflictions, emotional distress and forms of suffering that cannot easily be explained through ordinary social categories. The tradition occupies a fascinating place where folklore, healing, religion and performance meet.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vimbuza-healing-dance-00158" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural HeritageVimbuza healing danceVimbuza is a healing dance popular among the Tumbuka people living in northern Ma...</span></span></span>

## Tumbuka Spirit Affliction and Diagnosis

A key idea behind Vimbuza is that certain illnesses arise from spirit affliction. In traditional Tumbuka belief, the term “Vimbuza” can refer both to the spirits associated with these conditions and to the healing ritual itself. Rather than treating symptoms alone, healers seek to understand which spirit is involved, why the affliction has appeared and what social or emotional tensions may lie behind it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumbuka_people" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tumbuka people</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tumbuka people</span></span></span>

Many accounts note that the majority of patients have historically been women, particularly those experiencing forms of mental or emotional distress. Researchers have argued that Vimbuza provided a culturally recognised language through which anxiety, grief, social pressure, trauma or conflict could be expressed publicly without simply being dismissed as personal weakness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vimbuza-healing-dance-00158" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural HeritageVimbuza healing danceVimbuza is a healing dance popular among the Tumbuka people living in northern Ma...</span></span></span>

Diagnosis is carried out by specialist healers. Patients may spend weeks or months under a healer’s care, often in a dedicated village residence. The healer interprets symptoms, identifies the nature of the affliction and prepares the patient for ritual treatment. Unlike some traditions that focus on expelling a spirit entirely, Vimbuza often aims to restore balance between the afflicted person and the spirit world, reducing suffering and allowing normal life to continue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vimbuza-healing-dance-00158" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural HeritageVimbuza healing danceVimbuza is a healing dance popular among the Tumbuka people living in northern Ma...</span></span></span>

This approach helps explain why Vimbuza is frequently described as both a medical and a social institution. The illness is not viewed as belonging solely to the individual; family members, neighbours and the wider community often become participants in the healing process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.2979/jfolkrese.52.2-3.199" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[JSTOR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Demonic or Cultural Treasure?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Local Perspectives on...by L Gilman · 2015 · Cited by 50 — Vimbuza healers diagnose and treat spirit- related illnesses in rituals that...</span></span></span>

## Drums, Songs and Trance in Healing

The most striking feature of Vimbuza is the healing ceremony itself. Once diagnosis has taken place, the patient enters a ritual setting where music and movement are carefully organised to encourage trance and communication with helping spirits. UNESCO describes women and children forming a circle around the patient while songs are sung to call the spirits. Men usually participate through drumming, producing rhythms associated with particular spirits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vimbuza-healing-dance-00158" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural HeritageVimbuza healing danceVimbuza is a healing dance popular among the Tumbuka people living in northern Ma...</span></span></span>

The drumming is not merely background music. Within the tradition, different rhythms carry specific meanings and are believed to affect the patient’s condition. Singing, clapping and repetitive movement gradually build an atmosphere in which trance can occur. As the patient dances, emotional and spiritual tensions are expressed physically before the community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vimbuza-healing-dance-00158" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural HeritageVimbuza healing danceVimbuza is a healing dance popular among the Tumbuka people living in northern Ma...</span></span></span>

One of the most memorable descriptions of Vimbuza is UNESCO’s observation that it creates a space where people can “dance their disease”. This phrase captures an important idea: suffering becomes visible and performative. What might otherwise remain hidden inside an individual is given a recognised social form through movement, music and ritual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vimbuza-healing-dance-00158" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural HeritageVimbuza healing danceVimbuza is a healing dance popular among the Tumbuka people living in northern Ma...</span></span></span>

The ceremony is therefore more than entertainment, even though it may appear highly theatrical. Costumes, dramatic gestures, songs and dance all serve therapeutic purposes. Scholars studying the tradition often emphasise that its effectiveness cannot be separated from communal participation. Healing is achieved not only through the patient’s actions but through the collective response of those surrounding them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.2979/jfolkrese.52.2-3.199" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[JSTOR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Demonic or Cultural Treasure?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Local Perspectives on...by L Gilman · 2015 · Cited by 50 — Vimbuza healers diagnose and treat spirit- related illnesses in rituals that...</span></span></span>

## Why the Tradition Endured

Vimbuza has considerable historical depth. Sources linked to UNESCO place its development in the nineteenth century and connect its growth to periods of social disruption, political domination and cultural change in northern Malawi. Some scholars argue that the tradition became a way for communities to process experiences of oppression and uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cid-ds.org/malawi/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cid-ds.org">[cid-ds.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cid-ds.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DANCE AND SPIRITUALITYMalawi - DANCE AND SPIRITUALITYBy becoming possessed by Vimbuza spirits, people could express these mental problems...</span></span></span>

The practice survived repeated attempts at suppression. Christian missionaries frequently condemned spirit possession rituals and regarded them as incompatible with Christianity. Colonial authorities and later advocates of exclusively biomedical approaches often viewed the tradition with suspicion as well. Yet Vimbuza persisted because many communities continued to see it as an effective means of addressing problems that neither churches nor clinics fully explained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vimbuza-healing-dance-00158" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural HeritageVimbuza healing danceVimbuza is a healing dance popular among the Tumbuka people living in northern Ma...</span></span></span>

Its survival also reflects its flexibility. While rooted in spirit belief, Vimbuza has often coexisted with other forms of treatment. UNESCO explicitly notes that practitioners regard it as a complement to other medical approaches rather than necessarily a replacement for them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.emb-japan.go.jp/htm/d_ich_vimbuza.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.emb-japan.go.jp">[UNESCO Japan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.emb-japan.go.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Japanafeguarding of the Vimbuza Healing Dance&quot; (Malawi)Vimbuza is a dance performed as part of a traditional healing procedure, to...</span></span></span>

## Modern Pressure from Churches and Medicine

Today, Vimbuza occupies a complicated position in Malawi. It is internationally celebrated as cultural heritage, yet locally it can remain controversial. UNESCO added the Vimbuza Healing Dance to the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, recognising its cultural importance and encouraging safeguarding efforts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/vimbuza-healing-dance-00158" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Intangible Cultural HeritageVimbuza healing danceVimbuza is a healing dance popular among the Tumbuka people living in northern Ma...</span></span></span>

At the same time, some churches continue to interpret spirit possession traditions through a religious framework that views them negatively. Certain Christian groups regard Vimbuza as spiritually dangerous, while supporters see it as a valuable expression of Tumbuka identity and cultural memory. These disagreements have created ongoing debates over whether the tradition should be treated primarily as religion, therapy, performance, heritage or some combination of all three.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hts.org.za/index.php/hts/article/view/10464/29192" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hts.org.za">[HTS Teologiese Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hts.org.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HTS Teologiese StudiesWazungu missionaries and Vimbuza performersby CN Kamanga · 2025 — This article describes the confusion, inconsisten...</span></span></span>

Modern healthcare presents another challenge. Biomedical practitioners generally explain mental illness through psychological and physiological causes rather than spirit affliction. As hospitals and clinics expand, younger generations may be less familiar with traditional healing systems. ## A Folklore Tradition That Is Still Alive

For readers interested in Malawian folklore, Vimbuza is especially significant because it blurs boundaries that modern societies often separate. It is simultaneously a belief about spirits, a healing practice, a musical tradition, a dance form, a social institution and a body of inherited knowledge.

Its enduring power lies in the way it transforms suffering into a shared experience. Through drums, songs and trance, private distress becomes something the community can recognise, interpret and respond to.
