## Introduction

One of the most distinctive ideas in Icelandic folklore is that trolls cannot survive daylight. If they remain outside when the sun rises, they are transformed into stone and become part of the landscape itself. This simple rule explains countless cliffs, sea stacks, isolated rocks and strange mountain shapes across the country. In story after story, a troll misjudges the time, becomes distracted, or stays too long in the world of humans. Dawn arrives, and the creature is frozen forever.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://guidetoiceland.is/history-culture/trolls-in-iceland" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guidetoiceland.is">[Guide to Iceland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guidetoiceland.is</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Guide to Iceland The Ultimate Guide to Icelandic Troll Folklore</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guide to IcelandThe Ultimate Guide to Icelandic Troll FolkloreMay 20, 2026 — According to folklore, trolls turn to stone in sunlight, whi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 20, 2026</span></span></span>

These tales do more than provide colourful explanations for unusual geology. They connect [Iceland]({{ 'why-icelands-landscape-still-tells/' | relative_url }})'s dramatic scenery to memory, danger, morality and local identity. A traveller looking at a rock formation sees basalt, erosion and volcanic history; a traditional story sees the final moment of a giant creature caught by the first light of day. Across Iceland, the landscape itself becomes evidence that the stories are true—or at least that they deserve to be remembered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://guidetoiceland.is/history-culture/trolls-in-iceland" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guidetoiceland.is">[guidetoiceland.is]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guidetoiceland.is</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Guide to Iceland The Ultimate Guide to Icelandic Troll Folklore</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guide to IcelandThe Ultimate Guide to Icelandic Troll FolkloreMay 20, 2026 — According to folklore, trolls turn to stone in sunlight, whi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 20, 2026</span></span></span>

## Night Trolls and the Coming of Sunrise

The transformation from troll to stone is not an occasional feature of Icelandic tradition. It is one of the defining characteristics of many Icelandic trolls, especially the so-called night trolls. These beings belong to darkness, mountains, caves and remote wilderness. Their power ends with daylight. Once sunlight touches them, they lose movement and become rock.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitakureyri.is/en/see-and-do/culture/icelandic-trolls-creatures-of-mountains-myths-and-mystery" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitakureyri.is">[visitakureyri.is]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitakureyri.is</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Halló AkureyriIcelandic Trolls – Creatures of Mountains, Myths and Mystery2 days ago — If sunlight touched them, they would instantly tur...</span></span></span>

The rule creates a natural source of tension in stories. Trolls may be strong enough to move mountains or drag ships from the sea, but they are vulnerable to a force they cannot fight: time. Many tales therefore revolve around a race against dawn. A troll is working, hunting, travelling or pursuing humans through the night. The audience knows that sunrise is approaching, and the story turns on whether the creature can reach shelter before daylight arrives. Usually it cannot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://grayline.is/blog/folklore-of-reynisdrangar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grayline.is">[Gray Line Iceland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grayline.is</span><span class="citation-popover-title">folklore of reynisdrangar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gray Line IcelandFolklore of Reynisdrangar6 Oct 2024 — You see, trolls can&#x27;t survive in sunlight; if they are still out in the open at da...</span></span></span>

This theme also reflects a broader contrast found in northern European folklore between darkness and daylight. In Icelandic versions, daylight is not merely natural illumination. It is a force that restores order, protects people and drives dangerous beings back into the wild places beyond settlement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitakureyri.is/en/see-and-do/culture/icelandic-trolls-creatures-of-mountains-myths-and-mystery" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitakureyri.is">[Halló Akureyri]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitakureyri.is</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Halló AkureyriIcelandic Trolls – Creatures of Mountains, Myths and Mystery2 days ago — If sunlight touched them, they would instantly tur...</span></span></span>

## Why So Many Rocks Are Said to Be Trolls

Iceland is filled with striking geological formations: volcanic plugs, basalt pillars, sea stacks, isolated cliffs and oddly shaped mountains. Traditional storytellers rarely left such landmarks unexplained. Instead, they connected them to memorable events involving trolls, giants or other supernatural beings. The result is a landscape covered with narrative landmarks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://guidetoiceland.is/history-culture/trolls-in-iceland" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guidetoiceland.is">[Guide to Iceland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guidetoiceland.is</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Guide to Iceland The Ultimate Guide to Icelandic Troll Folklore</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guide to IcelandThe Ultimate Guide to Icelandic Troll FolkloreMay 20, 2026 — According to folklore, trolls turn to stone in sunlight, whi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 20, 2026</span></span></span>

The logic is straightforward. If a rock resembles a person, an animal or a group of figures, folklore asks how those figures came to be there. The answer is often that they once were living beings caught by daylight. A strange formation therefore becomes a visible reminder of an ancient story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://guidetoiceland.is/history-culture/trolls-in-iceland" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guidetoiceland.is">[Guide to Iceland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guidetoiceland.is</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Guide to Iceland The Ultimate Guide to Icelandic Troll Folklore</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guide to IcelandThe Ultimate Guide to Icelandic Troll FolkloreMay 20, 2026 — According to folklore, trolls turn to stone in sunlight, whi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 20, 2026</span></span></span>

These stories also help explain why so many Icelandic troll legends are intensely local. The tale belongs to a specific cliff, beach, mountain or valley. A visitor can stand in front of the formation and see the supposed result of the event. The landscape acts almost like a permanent monument to the legend.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icelandmonitor.mbl.is/news/culture_and_living/2016/05/28/a_map_to_the_unearthly_beings_of_iceland/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icelandmonitor.mbl.is">[Iceland Monitor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icelandmonitor.mbl.is</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a map to the unearthly beings of iceland</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">trolls. ## The Famous Stone Trolls of Iceland

The best-known example is Reynisdrangar, the black basalt sea stacks rising from the Atlantic near Reynisfjara on Iceland's south coast. Several versions of the legend exist. In the most familiar, trolls were dragging a ship toward shore during the night. They worked too slowly, failed to notice dawn approaching, and were turned into stone when sunlight struck them. The sea stacks visible today are said to be those petrified trolls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://grayline.is/blog/folklore-of-reynisdrangar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grayline.is">[grayline.is]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grayline.is</span><span class="citation-popover-title">folklore of reynisdrangar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gray Line IcelandFolklore of Reynisdrangar6 Oct 2024 — You see, trolls can&#x27;t survive in sunlight; if they are still out in the open at da...</span></span></span>

Reynisdrangar is famous partly because the rock formations themselves are so dramatic. Their shape makes the legend easy to imagine, and the story has become one of the most widely retold pieces of Icelandic folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/folklore-myths" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[nationalgeographic.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThe Eerie Folktales Behind Iceland&#x27;s Natural WondersAug 8, 2017 — According to Icelandic folklore, these pillars actua...</span></span></span>

The details vary from region to region, but the mechanism remains recognisable: a troll stays outside too long, and the landscape preserves the consequences.

## Trickery, Survival and Human Cleverness

Not every stone-troll story centres on carelessness. Many tales involve deliberate trickery. Humans often survive encounters with trolls not by overpowering them but by outsmarting them until sunrise arrives.

In these stories, a traveller, farmer or shepherd delays a troll through conversation, deception or stubborn resistance. The goal is simple: keep the creature occupied until dawn. Once daylight appears, nature itself defeats the monster. The hero's victory comes through patience and intelligence rather than physical strength.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://yourfriendinreykjavik.com/icelandic-troll-folktale/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yourfriendinreykjavik.com">[Your Friend in Reykjavik]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yourfriendinreykjavik.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Our famous Yule-lads and their parents Grýla and Leppalúði are trolls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Your Friend in ReykjavikThe Shepherd of Silfrúnarstaðir: Iceland Troll Stories &amp; ToursJun 23, 2022 — But as you might know, trolls turn i...</span></span></span>

This pattern reflects a wider theme in Icelandic folk narrative. Trolls may be enormous and frightening, but they are frequently portrayed as impulsive, short-sighted or unable to understand human cunning.
