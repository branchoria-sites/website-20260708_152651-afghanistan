## Introduction

Among Somali folktales, Wiil Waal stands out because the heroism is not based on strength, wealth or warfare. Instead, the story turns on a riddle. In the best-known version, Wiil Waal, a wise ruler, challenges the men of his domain to solve a puzzle about a sheep. Most powerful and prosperous men fail, while the answer comes from the daughter of a poor farmer. The tale has become one of the clearest Somali examples of a wider folklore theme: wisdom can emerge from unexpected places, and clever speech can overturn social rank.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://islamicschoollibrarian.com/2022/03/30/wiil-waal-a-somali-folktale-retold-by-kathleen-moriarty-illustrated-by-amin-amir-and-somali-translation-by-jamal-adam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islamicschoollibrarian.com">[islamicschoollibrarian.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islamicschoollibrarian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Islamic School LibrarianWiil Waal: A Somali Folktale retold by Kathleen Moriarty...March 30, 2022 — 30 Mar 2022 — This bilingual (Englis...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 30, 2022</span></span></span>

The story survives today through oral tradition and modern retellings, including bilingual Somali-English publications produced for cultural education. Although Wiil Waal is linked to a historical Somali leader remembered in regional tradition, the folktale itself is valued less as history than as a lesson about judgement, communication and the power of intelligence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiil_Waal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiil Waal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiil Waal</span></span></span>

## The ruler who tests his people

In many versions of the tale, Wiil Waal gathers the men of his territory and presents a challenge. He asks them to bring the part of a sheep that best represents something capable of both dividing people and uniting them. The problem appears simple, but it is designed to reveal how people think rather than how much property they own.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.biblioguides.com/pub/book/wiil-waal-a-somali-folktale-2007" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: biblioguides.com">[biblioguides.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">biblioguides.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiil Waal: A Somali FolktaleWhen wise Somali leader Wiil Waal asks the men in his province to bring him the part of a sheep t...</span></span></span>

The men return home and search for answers. They focus on valuable parts of the animal: the heart, the ribs, the leg, or other prized cuts. Their reasoning tends to be practical or material. Yet the riddle is really about human relationships and social life, not livestock. The challenge resembles the “wisdom contests” found in many traditional cultures, where rulers test judgement through riddles rather than direct examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.biblioguides.com/pub/book/wiil-waal-a-somali-folktale-2007" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: biblioguides.com">[biblioguides.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">biblioguides.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiil Waal: A Somali FolktaleWhen wise Somali leader Wiil Waal asks the men in his province to bring him the part of a sheep t...</span></span></span>

This reflects an important feature of Somali storytelling. Oral traditions often celebrate skill in language, debate and interpretation. A person who can understand hidden meanings demonstrates a kind of leadership that is more valuable than simple authority.

## The poor farmer's daughter and the sheep riddle

The most memorable twist arrives when a poor farmer's daughter proposes an answer that none of the prominent men have considered. The daughter identifies the sheep's tongue as the correct answer. The reasoning is elegant. Speech can unite communities through cooperation, friendship, truth and wise counsel. Yet the same tongue can divide people through insults, lies, gossip and reckless words. The answer transforms the riddle from a question about an animal into a reflection on human behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://futurefile.wordpress.com/2013/03/12/a-somali-tale/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: futurefile.wordpress.com">[wordpress.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">futurefile.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Future File Wiil Waal: A Somali Folktale &#124; Future File</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Future FileWiil Waal: A Somali Folktale &#124; Future File - WordPress.com12 Mar 2013 — Wiil Waal was a Somali leader (a sultan) who lived in...</span></span></span>

When the solution is revealed, the ruler recognises that genuine wisdom has come from someone outside the circle of wealth and power. The tale therefore contains two lessons at once:

* Words are among the most powerful forces in society.
* Intelligence should be judged by insight rather than status.

Because the answer depends on symbolic thinking, audiences remember both the riddle and the moral attached to it. The story works as entertainment, but it also teaches listeners to think beyond the obvious.

## Why wisdom often comes from below

The social message is as important as the riddle itself. Folktales across the world often feature kings who are corrected by shepherds, servants or children. In the Wiil Waal story, the crucial figure is a poor young woman whose understanding surpasses that of respected men.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://islamicschoollibrarian.com/2022/03/30/wiil-waal-a-somali-folktale-retold-by-kathleen-moriarty-illustrated-by-amin-amir-and-somali-translation-by-jamal-adam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islamicschoollibrarian.com">[Islamic School Librarian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islamicschoollibrarian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Islamic School LibrarianWiil Waal: A Somali Folktale retold by Kathleen Moriarty...March 30, 2022 — 30 Mar 2022 — This bilingual (Englis...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 30, 2022</span></span></span>

This does not mean the tale rejects leadership. Wiil Waal is portrayed as wise precisely because he creates a test that allows hidden talent to emerge. A good ruler, according to the story, is someone capable of recognising wisdom wherever it appears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/HarvardEducation/videos/books-of-belonging-episode-4-wiil-waal-a-somali-folktale/577024702887961/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Facebook&quot;Wiil Waal: A Somali Folktake&quot; by Kathleen Moriarity...It is said that Wiil Waal was a great leader with the ability to unite pe...</span></span></span>

The emphasis on a farmer's daughter also broadens the lesson beyond politics. Listeners are encouraged to question assumptions about age, wealth, gender and social position. Someone overlooked by society may still possess the most valuable understanding.

## What the riddle reveals about Somali storytelling

The sheep's tongue is a fitting answer in a culture that places high value on eloquence. [Somalia]({{ 'somalias-living-world-of-story-and/' | relative_url }}) is often described as a society with exceptionally rich oral traditions, where poetry, public speaking, proverbs and storytelling carry social prestige. In that context, a folktale that treats speech itself as the force that can either unite or divide people carries special significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://humanitieslearning.org/resource/uploads/Wiil%20Waal%20Guide%20-%20English_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: humanitieslearning.org">[Humanities Learning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">humanitieslearning.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Humanities Learning Wiil Waal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Humanities LearningWiil WaalSummary: This is a retelling of a traditional Somali folktale. The story also demonstrates how Somali folktales frequently teach through indirect reasoning. Instead of presenting a moral statement outright, the tale invites listeners to solve a puzzle. The lesson becomes more memorable because the audience arrives at the conclusion alongside the characters.

This approach helps explain why riddles occupy an important place in oral culture. They are not merely games. They train observation, interpretation and social judgement. In the Wiil Waal tradition, wisdom is shown not by possessing information but by understanding how language shapes human relationships.

## How the tale is understood today

Modern readers most often encounter Wiil Waal through children's books, educational programmes and cultural heritage projects that preserve Somali oral traditions for new generations. These retellings present the story as a celebration of intelligence, critical thinking and respect for overlooked voices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://humanitieslearning.org/resource/uploads/Wiil%20Waal%20Guide%20-%20English_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: humanitieslearning.org">[Humanities Learning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">humanitieslearning.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Humanities Learning Wiil Waal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Humanities LearningWiil WaalSummary: This is a retelling of a traditional Somali folktale. The tale remains relevant because its central insight is timeless. Communities are built and damaged through speech. Leaders succeed or fail through communication.
