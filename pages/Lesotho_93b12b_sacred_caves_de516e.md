## Introduction

In [Lesotho]({{ 'what-lives-in-lesothos-mountain-stories/' | relative_url }}), caves and mountains are not simply dramatic features of the landscape. They are places where history, religion, folklore, archaeology, healing, and collective memory overlap. For many Basotho communities, certain caves are remembered as shelters used during times of conflict, repositories of ancient rock art, sites associated with ancestors, and locations where ritual practices continue into the present. The same rock shelter may simultaneously be an archaeological site, a place of prayer, a destination for pilgrims, and a symbol of national identity. This layered meaning is one of the most distinctive features of Lesotho’s sacred geography.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://environment.gov.ls/protected-places/liphofung-cave-and-cultural-heritage-site/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: environment.gov.ls">[environment.gov.ls]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">environment.gov.ls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liphofung Cave and Cultural Heritage SiteLiphofung Cave and Cultural Heritage Site is endowed with rich cultural, historical, and archaeo...</span></span></span>

Unlike folklore traditions centred on a single shrine or temple, Basotho sacred landscapes are spread across mountains, cliffs, springs, and caves. The beliefs attached to them are not always separated neatly into “traditional” and “Christian” categories. Instead, many ritual places have become meeting points where ancestral traditions, healing practices, and Christian devotion coexist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248962823_Land_from_the_Ancestors_Popular_Religious_Pilgrimage_along_the_South_Africa-Lesotho_Border" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Land from the Ancestors: Popular Religious...Pilgrims to the sacred caves practise every form of African religion from...</span></span></span>

## Caves as Shelters, Sacred Places, and Memory Sites

Lesotho’s mountainous terrain has long made caves and rock shelters important places of refuge. Archaeological evidence shows that many were occupied thousands of years ago by hunter-gatherer communities whose paintings survive on rock walls across the country. Later generations of Basotho incorporated some of these same places into their own historical and spiritual traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://environment.gov.ls/protected-places/liphofung-cave-and-cultural-heritage-site/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: environment.gov.ls">[environment.gov.ls]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">environment.gov.ls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liphofung Cave and Cultural Heritage SiteLiphofung Cave and Cultural Heritage Site is endowed with rich cultural, historical, and archaeo...</span></span></span>

One of the best-known examples is Liphofung Cave in northern Lesotho. The site contains evidence of long human occupation, including Stone Age remains and San rock art, while also being linked to the nineteenth-century kingdom of King Moshoeshoe I. Modern heritage authorities present it as a place where archaeological, historical, and cultural memories intersect rather than as a monument belonging to a single era.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://environment.gov.ls/protected-places/liphofung-cave-and-cultural-heritage-site/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: environment.gov.ls">[environment.gov.ls]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">environment.gov.ls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liphofung Cave and Cultural Heritage SiteLiphofung Cave and Cultural Heritage Site is endowed with rich cultural, historical, and archaeo...</span></span></span>

This combination of meanings is common in Lesotho. A cave may preserve traces of ancient hunter-gatherers, memories of Basotho state formation, and contemporary ritual activity all at once. As a result, sacred caves are often treated not as abandoned relics but as living places whose significance continues to evolve.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://environment.gov.ls/protected-places/liphofung-cave-and-cultural-heritage-site/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: environment.gov.ls">[environment.gov.ls]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">environment.gov.ls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liphofung Cave and Cultural Heritage SiteLiphofung Cave and Cultural Heritage Site is endowed with rich cultural, historical, and archaeo...</span></span></span>

The importance of caves is reinforced by broader Basotho ideas about sacred places. Studies of Basotho religion note that mountains, hills, caves, rivers, and unusual natural features have long been associated with spiritual power and communication with ancestors. Such places are valued because they stand apart from everyday life and create a setting where contact with the unseen world is thought to be easier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://open.uct.ac.za/bitstreams/6132021c-0558-42b9-84af-896b7b89724c/download" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: open.uct.ac.za">[OpenUCT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">open.uct.ac.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Some places in Africa have been set aside as... ## Why Ancestors Are Connected to Mountains and Caves

A recurring theme in Basotho belief is the idea that ancestors remain active participants in family and community life. Ancestors are not generally understood as distant mythological figures but as continuing members of the lineage who can offer protection, guidance, warnings, or healing. Ritual specialists, healers, and ordinary families may seek contact with them through prayer, offerings, dreams, or ceremonies performed in significant landscapes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://open.uct.ac.za/bitstreams/6132021c-0558-42b9-84af-896b7b89724c/download" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: open.uct.ac.za">[OpenUCT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">open.uct.ac.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Some places in Africa have been set aside as... Caves are often viewed as especially suitable places for such encounters because they occupy a symbolic position between worlds. Their darkness, isolation, and connection to water sources have made them powerful ritual settings across southern Africa. Research on Basotho sacred sites repeatedly notes that caves are regarded as places where ancestral presence can be felt more strongly than in ordinary locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scholar.ufs.ac.za/bitstreams/9302255a-1265-4f9d-854d-13b0795a15a4/download" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scholar.ufs.ac.za">[ufs.ac.za]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scholar.ufs.ac.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a study of rituals performed at two sacred sites in the...by MS Mensele · 2011 · Cited by 12 — In this regard, Basotho often use caves a...</span></span></span>

Water is particularly important. Many sacred caves contain springs, streams, or pools, and ritual use of water appears frequently in accounts of pilgrimage and healing. In local traditions, water is often associated with purification, blessing, and ancestral power. The significance of cave water helps explain why some pilgrimage destinations continue to attract visitors from a wide range of religious backgrounds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_caves_of_the_Basotho" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sacred caves of the Basotho</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sacred caves of the Basotho</span></span></span>

## Healing, Initiation, and Pilgrimage

Research on sacred sites in the Lesotho–Free State highlands shows that caves and mountains are still used for a variety of ritual purposes. These include healing ceremonies, consultations by traditional healers, prayers for protection, rites connected with life transitions, and gatherings linked to ancestral observance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scholar.ufs.ac.za/bitstreams/9302255a-1265-4f9d-854d-13b0795a15a4/download" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scholar.ufs.ac.za">[scholar.ufs.ac.za]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scholar.ufs.ac.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a study of rituals performed at two sacred sites in the...by MS Mensele · 2011 · Cited by 12 — In this regard, Basotho often use caves a...</span></span></span>

One of the strongest themes is healing. Many healers describe their vocation as arising from a calling received through dreams, visions, illness, or ancestral communication. Mountain caves are sometimes understood as places where that calling can be clarified, strengthened, or fulfilled. Certain cave complexes on the Lesotho border have become well known as centres where healers train, perform rituals, and seek spiritual guidance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_caves_of_the_Basotho" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sacred caves of the Basotho</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sacred caves of the Basotho</span></span></span>

Initiation traditions also contribute to the sacred status of mountain landscapes. Academic studies note that mountains and caves have historically been used during some of the most important ritual passages in Basotho society, especially ceremonies connected with the transition from youth to adulthood. These locations provide privacy, separation from ordinary life, and a setting charged with cultural authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scholar.ufs.ac.za/bitstreams/9302255a-1265-4f9d-854d-13b0795a15a4/download" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scholar.ufs.ac.za">[scholar.ufs.ac.za]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scholar.ufs.ac.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a study of rituals performed at two sacred sites in the...by MS Mensele · 2011 · Cited by 12 — In this regard, Basotho often use caves a...</span></span></span>

Pilgrimage adds another layer. Along the Lesotho–South Africa border, sacred caves attract people from different religious traditions, including practitioners of ancestral religion, independent African churches, and mainstream Christian denominations. Rather than replacing older traditions, Christianity often became woven into existing understandings of sacred landscape. Pilgrims may pray, seek healing, collect sacred water, make offerings, or honour ancestors within the same location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248962823_Land_from_the_Ancestors_Popular_Religious_Pilgrimage_along_the_South_Africa-Lesotho_Border" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Land from the Ancestors: Popular Religious...Pilgrims to the sacred caves practise every form of African religion from...</span></span></span>

## Liphofung and Other Sacred Landscapes

Liphofung is important partly because it demonstrates how many meanings can accumulate in one place. Archaeologists value its ancient occupation history, heritage authorities protect its rock art, historians connect it with Moshoeshoe I, and visitors encounter it as a cultural landmark. The site illustrates how sacred landscapes in Lesotho often resist simple classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://environment.gov.ls/protected-places/liphofung-cave-and-cultural-heritage-site/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: environment.gov.ls">[environment.gov.ls]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">environment.gov.ls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liphofung Cave and Cultural Heritage SiteLiphofung Cave and Cultural Heritage Site is endowed with rich cultural, historical, and archaeo...</span></span></span>

Other cave traditions extend beyond modern national boundaries. The Basotho cultural world historically crossed what is now the Lesotho–South Africa frontier, and several major pilgrimage caves remain part of a shared sacred geography. Sites such as Motouleng and Badimong are frequently described as places where ancestral traditions, healing practices, and Christian devotion converge. Their continued use demonstrates that sacred landscapes are defined as much by living practice as by archaeology or history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248962823_Land_from_the_Ancestors_Popular_Religious_Pilgrimage_along_the_South_Africa-Lesotho_Border" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Land from the Ancestors: Popular Religious...Pilgrims to the sacred caves practise every form of African religion from...</span></span></span>

The persistence of pilgrimage has occasionally produced disputes over land ownership, access rights, and heritage management. Yet these tensions also reveal how deeply many people value the caves.
