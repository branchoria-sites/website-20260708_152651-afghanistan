## Introduction

Among [Botswana]({{ 'where-botswanas-stories-live-in-the-land/' | relative_url }})’s many legendary landscapes, few places combine visible traces in stone with a national origin story as powerfully as Matsieng. Located near the village of Rasesa north of Gaborone, the Matsieng Footprints are a protected national monument marked by rock engravings, footprint-like carvings and deep natural holes in sandstone. For many Batswana, these features are not merely archaeological curiosities. They are linked to a creation tradition in which the first ancestor, Matsieng, emerged from the earth together with people, animals and livestock, leaving marks behind in still-soft ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.botswanatourism.co.bw/explore/matsieng-footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: botswanatourism.co.bw">[Botswana Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">botswanatourism.co.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Botswana TourismMatsieng FootprintsFurther north on the main Gaborone to Francistown road, just beyond the village of Rasesa, travellers...</span></span></span>

The importance of Matsieng lies in the way it joins landscape and story. The legend explains the physical features of the site, while the site gives the legend a tangible home. As a result, Matsieng occupies a special place in Botswana’s cultural imagination, where questions of ancestry, belonging and the relationship between people and land are expressed through a single, memorable location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thebotswanasociety.net/matsieng" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thebotswanasociety.net">[TheBotswanaSociety]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thebotswanasociety.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Matsieng &#124; TheBotswanaSocietyLocated just northeast of Gaborone, Matsieng is believed by the people of Botswana to be t...</span></span></span>

## The Legend of Matsieng's Emergence

In the best-known version of the tradition, Matsieng was the first ancestor of the Batswana. He emerged from a deep hole in the earth and was followed by human beings, cattle and wild animals. Some tellings describe him as a giant with only one leg, while others portray him as a great hunter or culture hero. Despite these differences, the central idea remains the same: humanity and the animal world entered the surface of the earth together at Matsieng.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.botswanatourism.co.bw/explore/matsieng-footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: botswanatourism.co.bw">[botswanatourism.co.bw]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">botswanatourism.co.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Botswana TourismMatsieng FootprintsFurther north on the main Gaborone to Francistown road, just beyond the village of Rasesa, travellers...</span></span></span>

The emergence motif is especially significant because it differs from creation stories in which humans are fashioned by a distant deity. At Matsieng, people come directly from the land itself. The earth is not merely a setting for human life but the source from which life appears. This gives the legend a strong connection to place and ancestry, making the landscape part of the community’s identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.oxfordreference.com/display/10.1093/oi/authority.20110803100140457" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oxfordreference.com">[Oxford Reference]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oxfordreference.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The first human beings and animals came out of Lowe, near Mochudi, in Botswana, emerging from a hole nine to twelve feet deep in a...Rea...</span></span></span>

Different communities in Botswana have preserved related versions of the story. In some accounts, Matsieng is followed first by animals and later by human groups. In others, various peoples emerge behind him. The details vary, but the theme of shared origins and common emergence remains remarkably consistent across retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsieng_Footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Matsieng Footprints</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Matsieng Footprints</span></span></span>

## Footprints, Animals and Softened Stone

What makes the Matsieng legend particularly memorable is its attempt to explain physical marks that visitors can still see today. The site contains numerous engraved footprints and animal-like tracks around natural holes in the sandstone. According to tradition, these impressions were made when the ground was soft immediately after creation. As Matsieng, people and animals walked away from the emergence point, their tracks hardened into stone and remained visible forever.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.botswanatourism.co.bw/explore/matsieng-footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: botswanatourism.co.bw">[Botswana Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">botswanatourism.co.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Botswana TourismMatsieng FootprintsFurther north on the main Gaborone to Francistown road, just beyond the village of Rasesa, travellers...</span></span></span>

Archaeological research offers a different explanation. The footprints are generally understood as petroglyphs—engravings deliberately created by ancient people rather than literal fossilised tracks. The site contains more than a hundred engravings, including human footprints, feline-like prints and images of animals such as giraffes. Estimates for their age vary, but many researchers place them somewhere between roughly 3,000 and 10,000 years old.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsieng_Footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Matsieng Footprints</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Matsieng Footprints</span></span></span>

Rather than cancelling the legend, the archaeological evidence highlights how folklore often works. The carvings existed as striking features in the landscape. Communities developed stories that gave them meaning, linking unusual marks in stone to deeper questions about where people came from and how the world began. Matsieng therefore functions simultaneously as an archaeological site and a mythic landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsieng_Footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Matsieng Footprints</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Matsieng Footprints</span></span></span>

The presence of animal tracks in the engravings is especially important. In the legend, humans do not emerge alone. Livestock and wildlife accompany the first ancestor, reflecting a worldview in which people, animals and land are bound together. The story turns the visible mixture of human and animal marks into a narrative about a shared beginning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.botswanatourism.co.bw/explore/matsieng-footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: botswanatourism.co.bw">[Botswana Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">botswanatourism.co.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Botswana TourismMatsieng FootprintsFurther north on the main Gaborone to Francistown road, just beyond the village of Rasesa, travellers...</span></span></span>

## Why the Site Became a Place of Power

The natural holes at Matsieng collect rainwater and have long attracted attention. Researchers have suggested that the location may have served ceremonial purposes in the past, while local traditions connect it with [rainmaking]({{ 'rainmaking/' | relative_url }}) practices and ritual activity. The combination of water, unusual rock formations and engraved footprints helped establish the site's reputation as a place of spiritual significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsieng_Footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Matsieng Footprints</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Matsieng Footprints</span></span></span>

Stories of emergence often become attached to locations that appear unusual or sacred. Matsieng fits this pattern closely. The deep holes suggest an opening between worlds, while the surrounding engravings seem to record movement away from that opening. Whether viewed through folklore or archaeology, the arrangement encourages interpretation and storytelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsieng_Footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Matsieng Footprints</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Matsieng Footprints</span></span></span>

Over time, the site became more than a local curiosity. It developed into one of Botswana’s most recognisable origin landscapes, frequently described as a place connected with the beginnings of humanity or the beginnings of the Batswana people.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thebotswanasociety.net/matsieng" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thebotswanasociety.net">[TheBotswanaSociety]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thebotswanasociety.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Matsieng &#124; TheBotswanaSocietyLocated just northeast of Gaborone, Matsieng is believed by the people of Botswana to be t...</span></span></span>

## National Monument, Tourism and Belief

Today Matsieng is protected as a national monument and promoted as one of Botswana’s key cultural heritage sites. Visitors encounter both the physical remains—the sandstone slab, engravings and water-filled holes—and the stories attached to them. Tourism materials commonly present the legend alongside archaeological information, allowing travellers to understand why the site matters beyond its visible features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.botswanatourism.co.bw/explore/matsieng-footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: botswanatourism.co.bw">[Botswana Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">botswanatourism.co.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Botswana TourismMatsieng FootprintsFurther north on the main Gaborone to Francistown road, just beyond the village of Rasesa, travellers...</span></span></span>

The modern presentation of Matsieng illustrates an important aspect of contemporary folklore. The site is not preserved simply as evidence of the distant past. It remains meaningful because people continue to tell the story. Guides, heritage organisations and local communities often recount the emergence legend, ensuring that the footprints are understood not only as ancient carvings but also as symbols of origin and belonging.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.botswanatourism.co.bw/explore/matsieng-footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: botswanatourism.co.bw">[botswanatourism.co.bw]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">botswanatourism.co.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Botswana TourismMatsieng FootprintsFurther north on the main Gaborone to Francistown road, just beyond the village of Rasesa, travellers...</span></span></span>

For many visitors, the most striking feature is the coexistence of two ways of seeing the site. Archaeology examines who made the engravings and when. Folklore asks what the marks mean and why generations of people connected them to a foundational ancestor. Matsieng endures because both perspectives add value. One reveals the antiquity of the carvings; the other explains why they became part of Botswana’s national story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsieng_Footprints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Matsieng Footprints</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Matsieng Footprints</span></span></span>

## What Matsieng Reveals About Botswana's Folklore

The Matsieng tradition demonstrates a recurring feature of Botswana’s legendary culture: stories are often anchored to specific places. Instead of existing only in memory or oral performance, they become attached to hills, waterholes, rock formations and other landmarks that people can visit. Matsieng is one of the clearest examples of this process.

The legend transforms engraved stone into a narrative about first ancestors, emergence and community. The footprints are not important because they prove the story. They are important because they help generations of people imagine a shared beginning.
