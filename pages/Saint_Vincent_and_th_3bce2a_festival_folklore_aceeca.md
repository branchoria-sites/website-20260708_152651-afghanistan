## Introduction

The folklore of [Saint Vincent and the Grenadines]({{ 'saint-vincent-and-the-grenadines/' | relative_url }}) is not preserved only in old stories. It also survives in public celebrations where music, dance, ritual timing and collective memory are performed in front of entire communities. Two of the clearest examples are the Nine Mornings festival before Christmas and the Big Drum traditions of the southern Grenadines, especially Union Island. Together they show how folklore remains a living part of Vincentian culture rather than a relic of the past.

These festivals are important because they carry older beliefs, historical memories and community identities into the present. They connect contemporary celebrations with African-Caribbean heritage, Christian seasonal customs, village traditions and ancestral remembrance. ## Nine Mornings Before Christmas

Nine Mornings is widely regarded as one of the most distinctive cultural traditions of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. For the nine mornings before Christmas, communities gather before dawn for activities that can include concerts, storytelling, games, bicycle rides, sea bathing, carol singing, dances and street performances. Participants often begin gathering around four o'clock in the morning, transforming ordinary streets and public spaces into temporary festival grounds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tourism.gov.vc/tourism/index.php/festivals-a-events/66-nine-mornings-festival" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tourism.gov.vc">[tourism.gov.vc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tourism.gov.vc</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nine Mornings Festival</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nine Mornings before Christmas, Vincentians awake in the early hours of...Read more...</span></span></span>

What makes Nine Mornings especially interesting from a folklore perspective is the way it combines religious origins with popular community creativity. Several accounts trace the tradition to early-morning Christmas services associated with Catholic and Anglican observances. One explanation holds that enslaved Africans accompanied slaveholders to these services but were not permitted to participate fully, leading them to create their own forms of social gathering while waiting nearby. Over time these activities developed into a distinctive Vincentian festival that eventually became larger than its church origins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discoversvgpro.com/nine-mornings-2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discoversvgpro.com">[discoversvgpro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discoversvgpro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nine Mornings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vincent and the Grenadines Expert...January 8, 2026 — The festival is believed to have evolved from slaves being required to accompany t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 8, 2026</span></span></span>

The festival also functions as a seasonal ritual calendar. Folklorists often note that traditions survive best when tied to recurring moments in the year, and Nine Mornings demonstrates this principle clearly. Every December, communities reactivate customs that may include traditional music, local foods, village competitions, light displays and performances passed between generations. The result is a living cycle of cultural transmission rather than a single annual event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.vc/index.php/media-center/4088-nine-mornings-launched" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gov.vc">[gov.vc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gov.vc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gov. of St. Vincent &amp; GrenadinesNine Mornings LaunchedThe Nine Mornings Festival remains a cornerstone of Vincentian culture, bringing co...</span></span></span>

Another striking feature is the widespread belief that Nine Mornings is unique to Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. Although Christmas festivals occur throughout the Caribbean, Vincentians frequently present Nine Mornings as a national tradition found nowhere else in quite the same form. That claim has become part of the festival's folklore in its own right, reinforcing a sense of national distinctiveness and cultural pride.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/Searchlight1/posts/the-nine-mornings-festival-in-st-vincent-and-the-grenadines-officially-launched-/1487625963363196/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[facebook.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Nine Mornings Festival in St. Vincent and...Nine Mornings is unique to St. Vincent and the Grenadines (SVG) and features ear...</span></span></span>

## Union Island Big Drum

If Nine Mornings demonstrates the survival of seasonal folklore, Big Drum illustrates the endurance of African-derived performance traditions in the Grenadines.

On Union Island, Big Drum involves drumming, singing, chanting and dancing performed during celebrations, festivals and important community occasions. The tradition is especially associated with Maroon festivities linked to the agricultural cycle and communal gatherings. Participants perform songs and dances that preserve memories of African ancestry and cultural continuity across generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discoversvg.com/island/union-island/history-culture/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discoversvg.com">[discoversvg.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discoversvg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Discover SVGUnion Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History &amp; CultureBig Drumming on Union Island where people gather in a public square to drum and dance to celebrate special occasions and...</span></span></span>

The drum itself occupies a symbolic place within the tradition. Accounts of Union Island culture describe public gatherings where drummers, singers and dancers come together in a shared performance space, creating an experience that is simultaneously entertainment, ceremony and cultural remembrance. The practice reflects broader Afro-Caribbean traditions found elsewhere in the Grenadines, but on Union Island it has become a particularly important marker of local identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discoversvg.com/island/union-island/history-culture/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discoversvg.com">[discoversvg.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discoversvg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Discover SVGUnion Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History &amp; CultureBig Drumming on Union Island where people gather in a public square to drum and dance to celebrate special occasions and...</span></span></span>

Historical comparisons with related Big Drum traditions elsewhere in the Grenadines suggest deeper connections to ancestral memory. Across the region, Big Drum ceremonies have often been linked to weddings, boat launchings, communal celebrations and remembrance of forebears. Even when performed today as heritage events, the dances and songs continue to evoke ideas of lineage, community belonging and respect for ancestors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://folkways.si.edu/the-big-drum-dance-of-carriacou/caribbean-world/music/album/smithsonian" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folkways.si.edu">[si.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folkways.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian Folkways RecordingsThe Big Drum Dance of CarriacouReally a long series of dances, the Big Drum Dance is prepared for special...</span></span></span>

The annual Maroon festival on Union Island provides one of the clearest examples of folklore operating in public life. Government and tourism descriptions identify the event as a celebration associated with traditional songs, Nation Dance and Big Drum performance. ## Performance, Heritage and Public Culture

Both Nine Mornings and Big Drum show that folklore is not limited to legends about supernatural beings. In Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, folklore is often expressed through performance.

Songs, drumming patterns, dance movements and festival rituals can function as carriers of cultural memory just as effectively as spoken stories. A child who learns a traditional Christmas performance during Nine Mornings, or a dancer who joins a Big Drum celebration on Union Island, is participating in a form of heritage transmission. Knowledge is passed through participation rather than through books alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.vc/index.php/media-center/4088-nine-mornings-launched" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gov.vc">[gov.vc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gov.vc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gov. of St. Vincent &amp; GrenadinesNine Mornings LaunchedThe Nine Mornings Festival remains a cornerstone of Vincentian culture, bringing co...</span></span></span>

These traditions also demonstrate how folklore adapts. Modern Nine Mornings events may include contemporary music, organised competitions and national sponsorship while retaining their historic pre-dawn structure. Likewise, Big Drum performances may appear at cultural showcases, tourism events and heritage festivals while continuing to draw authority from older communal practices. Rather than disappearing, the traditions have evolved alongside changing social conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.vc/index.php/media-center/4088-nine-mornings-launched" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gov.vc">[gov.vc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gov.vc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gov. of St. Vincent &amp; GrenadinesNine Mornings LaunchedThe Nine Mornings Festival remains a cornerstone of Vincentian culture, bringing co...</span></span></span>

For many Vincentians, this adaptability is precisely why the traditions matter. They preserve a sense of continuity between past and present without freezing culture in a museum display. Folklore remains visible in streets before sunrise, in village squares, in drum circles, in seasonal gatherings and in the public celebrations that continue to shape national identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.vc/index.php/media-center/4088-nine-mornings-launched" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gov.vc">[gov.vc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gov.vc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gov. of St. Vincent &amp; GrenadinesNine Mornings LaunchedThe Nine Mornings Festival remains a cornerstone of Vincentian culture, bringing co...</span></span></span>

## Why These Festivals Matter to Folklore

Nine Mornings and Big Drum reveal an important truth about folklore in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines: some of the country's most significant traditions survive not because they are written down, but because people continue to perform them.

The pre-dawn gatherings of Nine Mornings keep seasonal customs alive through annual repetition. The drumming, songs and dances of Union Island's Big Drum preserve ancestral memory through embodied performance. Together they demonstrate how folklore can exist simultaneously as celebration, heritage, community identity and living cultural practice.
