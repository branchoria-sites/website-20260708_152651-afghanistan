## Introduction

Awang Semaun occupies a special place in [Brunei]({{ 'why-bruneis-legends-still-point-to/' | relative_url }})’s legendary past because he stands at the point where heroic folklore, royal origin stories and cultural memory meet. Although presented as the younger brother of Awang Alak Betatar—the figure traditionally identified with Brunei’s first Muslim ruler—Awang Semaun often emerges as the more dramatic character in popular storytelling: a warrior of extraordinary strength, a giant-like hero, a founder, explorer and creator of landmarks. His adventures survive most famously through the *Syair Awang Semaun*, a long narrative poem that preserves a mixture of oral tradition, court memory and mythic history. Rather than providing a straightforward historical account, these stories explain how Brunei began, why certain places matter, and how the early kingdom imagined its own origins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Shah_of_Brunei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muhammad Shah of Brunei</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muhammad Shah of Brunei</span></span></span>

## The Hero in *Syair Awang Semaun*

The main source for Awang Semaun’s legendary career is the *Syair Awang Semaun*, one of the best-known works of traditional Bruneian literature. Written in the Malay poetic form known as *syair*, the work survives in several versions and appears to have been assembled from older oral traditions before eventually being written down. Scholars often describe it as a Brunei-Malay epic because of its scale, its heroic themes and its role in preserving cultural memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/128732887/A_Comparative_Analysis_of_the_La_Galigo_and_Syair_Awang_Semaun" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[academia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Comparative Analysis of the La Galigo and Syair Awang...Druce The La Galigo and Syair A wang Semaun are major works of poetic...</span></span></span>

Within the poem, Awang Semaun is not merely a supporting character in a royal genealogy. He is the central hero whose feats drive the narrative. Stories portray him as exceptionally strong, fearless and capable of deeds beyond ordinary human ability. Modern retellings frequently describe him as giant-like, reflecting the tendency of heroic folklore to magnify physical power into something almost supernatural.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dayakdaily.com/upai-semaring-and-awang-semaun-the-lundayeh-and-the-brunei-royal-house/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dayakdaily.com">[DayakDaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dayakdaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">upai semaring and awang semaun the lundayeh and the brunei royal house</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Upai Semaring and Awang Semaun: The Lundayeh...Mar 18, 2024 — In Brunei, many stories are told about the exploits of Awang Sem...</span></span></span>

The poem also preserves traces of a much older worldview than modern Brunei. Some versions begin with heavenly ancestors, miraculous births and journeys from celestial realms. These elements connect the story to wider Southeast Asian traditions in which rulers and heroes gain legitimacy through links to the sky, divine ancestry or sacred origins. Such motifs are typical of origin legends rather than historical chronicles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/41493513" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[jstor.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BRUNEI DARUSSALAM AND THE MODERN NOVELby CH GALLOP · 2004 · Cited by 9 — royal dynasty is found in the poem Syair Awang Semaun,which...</span></span></span>

## Founders, Brothers and Brunei Bay

One reason Awang Semaun remains important is that he appears in Brunei’s foundational stories. Traditional accounts describe him as one of fourteen siblings born to a remarkable family whose descendants would shape the future kingdom. Among these brothers was Awang Alak Betatar, who later became Sultan Muhammad Shah in Bruneian tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Shah_of_Brunei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muhammad Shah of Brunei</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muhammad Shah of Brunei</span></span></span>

The stories are less concerned with exact dates than with explaining how a kingdom came into being. The siblings spread across different settlements, established communities and helped organise the territory that became Brunei. In these narratives, the founding of Brunei is a collective achievement involving brothers, allies and local leaders rather than the work of a single monarch. Awang Semaun therefore functions as both warrior and nation-builder.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Shah_of_Brunei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muhammad Shah of Brunei</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muhammad Shah of Brunei</span></span></span>

Several legends linked to Brunei Bay and nearby islands are woven into this origin cycle. Traditional accounts recorded in later retellings associate places such as Lumut Lunting and Pulau Pilong-Pilongan with events described in the *Syair Awang Semaun*. These stories connect the beginnings of the Brunei Sultanate to visible features of the landscape, allowing geography to serve as a memory map of heroic deeds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bruneiresources.com/goldenlegacy/tgl_brunei_bay_legends.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bruneiresources.com">[Brunei Resources]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bruneiresources.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Awang Alak Betatar was the first ruler of the new Brunei Sultanate and as a...Read more...</span></span></span>

This blending of founders, siblings and landscapes is one of the most distinctive features of Bruneian folklore. Rather than separating myth from territory, the stories make rivers, islands and settlements part of the narrative itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bruneiresources.com/goldenlegacy/tgl_brunei_bay_legends.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bruneiresources.com">[Brunei Resources]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bruneiresources.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Awang Alak Betatar was the first ruler of the new Brunei Sultanate and as a...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why the Origin Stories Matter

Like many national origin legends, the stories surrounding Awang Semaun operate on several levels at once.

**They explain political beginnings.** The tales provide a memorable account of how Brunei’s ruling line emerged and how early settlements were established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Shah_of_Brunei" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muhammad Shah of Brunei</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muhammad Shah of Brunei</span></span></span>

**They preserve older beliefs.** Heavenly ancestors, miraculous births and superhuman heroes hint at pre-Islamic layers of tradition that survived within later Bruneian culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/41493513" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[jstor.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BRUNEI DARUSSALAM AND THE MODERN NOVELby CH GALLOP · 2004 · Cited by 9 — royal dynasty is found in the poem Syair Awang Semaun,which...</span></span></span>

**They celebrate ideal qualities.** Courage, loyalty, strength and service to the kingdom are embodied by Awang Semaun throughout the narrative tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bruneiresources.blogspot.com/2008/07/awang-semaun.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bruneiresources.blogspot.com">[bruneiresources.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bruneiresources.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">awang semaun</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jul 1, 2008 — According to legends, Awang Semaun is said to be the younger brother of Awang Alak Betatar (who eventually became the first...</span></span></span>

**They connect people to place.** The stories continually return to rivers, hills, islands and settlements that can still be identified today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bruneiresources.com/goldenlegacy/tgl_brunei_bay_legends.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bruneiresources.com">[Brunei Resources]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bruneiresources.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Awang Alak Betatar was the first ruler of the new Brunei Sultanate and as a...Read more...</span></span></span>

Because of these functions, Awang Semaun is remembered not simply as a legendary strongman but as a symbolic ancestor whose exploits help explain Brunei’s cultural origins.

## Spinning Tops, Stones and Heroic Landmarks

Perhaps the most famous folk tradition associated with Awang Semaun concerns a giant spinning top. According to the legend, Awang Semaun and his nephew Awang Sinuai were playing a game of spinning tops at Bukit Patoi in Temburong. When Awang Semaun launched his top, it struck his opponent’s top and flew an impossible distance across the landscape. The story survives because it is tied to a real landmark. A rock formation known as Batu Gasing Awang Semaun is traditionally identified as the hero’s spinning top turned to stone.
