## Introduction

Tchiloli is often described as a theatre performance, but in São Tomé and Príncipe it functions as something larger: a public reflection on justice, authority, memory and social order. Built around a centuries-old story from the European Charlemagne cycle, the drama asks a deceptively simple question. What happens when the son of an emperor commits a crime? Must power protect him, or must the law apply equally to everyone? That question sits at the heart of Tchiloli and explains why the tradition remains one of the most distinctive forms of folk performance in the African Atlantic world. Over time, a medieval European tale was transformed into a São Toméan cultural institution whose meaning reaches far beyond its original plot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tchiloli-living-theatre-of-sao-tome-and-principe-in-the-quest-of-justice-02309" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Music plays an important role in the performance</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageTchiloli, living theatre of Sao Tome and Principe in the ...UNESCO Tchiloli is a traditional form of open-air...</span></span></span>

## How Did the Charlemagne Story Reach the Islands?

The story performed in Tchiloli ultimately comes from the medieval legends surrounding Charlemagne and his court. Through a long chain of literary adaptations, the tale travelled from the Carolingian tradition into Iberian popular theatre. The version that became central to Tchiloli derives from the sixteenth-century play *The Tragedy of the Marquis of Mantua and Emperor Charlemagne*, attributed to the Portuguese playwright Baltasar Dias. Scholars have traced how this narrative moved through European literary traditions before reaching São Tomé during the colonial period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/294590875_Charlemagne_in_Africa_Origins_and_phenomenology_of_tchiloli_of_Sao_Tome" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Charlemagne in Africa. The exact date of its arrival remains debated. Popular accounts often present Tchiloli as a direct survival from the sixteenth century, but researchers and heritage specialists note that documentary evidence for an uninterrupted tradition from that era is limited. What is not disputed is that the drama became deeply rooted in local society and evolved far beyond its European source material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gulbenkian.pt/en/news/tchiloli-the-european-tragedy-which-is-part-of-sao-tomes-cultural-heritage/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gulbenkian.pt">[Fundação Calouste Gulbenkian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gulbenkian.pt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fundação Calouste GulbenkianTchiloli: the European tragedy which is part of São Tomé&#x27;s...September 17, 2020 — 17 Sept 2020 — Tchiloli ha...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 17, 2020</span></span></span>

The plot centres on a murder. Don Carloto, son of Emperor Charlemagne, kills Valdevinos, the nephew of the Marquis of Mantua. The crime sparks a legal and moral struggle involving rulers, nobles, lawyers and witnesses. The drama unfolds as a public search for justice, culminating in judgement against the emperor's own son. ## Why a Medieval Trial Became a Local Debate About Power

The lasting significance of Tchiloli cannot be explained by the story alone. Many communities inherited European texts during the colonial era, but few transformed them as thoroughly as São Tomé did.

In performance, the trial of Don Carloto became more than a tale of royal wrongdoing. Audiences and commentators have long interpreted the drama through the islands' history of slavery, plantation labour, racial hierarchy and colonial authority. The central question—whether power can place itself above justice—acquired obvious local resonance. The emperor's dilemma echoes broader concerns about privilege, inequality and the abuse of authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/48593977" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[jstor.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gazes from the Southby A Nolasco · 2020 · Cited by 3 — At the same time that the Tchiloli reactivates the memory of the African ance...</span></span></span>

This does not mean that every performance functions as an explicit political protest. Rather, the story creates a symbolic space in which difficult questions can be explored through ritualised drama. Because the conflict is set in a distant legendary court, performers and audiences can examine issues of power without reducing the play to a direct commentary on any single event. The result is a tradition that feels simultaneously historical, mythical and relevant to contemporary life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/48593977" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[JSTOR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gazes from the Southby A Nolasco · 2020 · Cited by 3 — At the same time that the Tchiloli reactivates the memory of the African ance...</span></span></span>

For many observers, this transformation is what makes Tchiloli so remarkable. A European courtly tragedy became a distinctly São Toméan cultural expression, carrying meanings shaped by local history rather than by its original authors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/294590875_Charlemagne_in_Africa_Origins_and_phenomenology_of_tchiloli_of_Sao_Tome" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Charlemagne in Africa. ## What Happens During a Tchiloli Performance?

Tchiloli is performed in open-air settings during festivals and community celebrations. Rather than being confined to a theatre building, it unfolds in public space, drawing audiences into a shared cultural event. UNESCO describes it as a living form of theatre involving performers, musicians and active audience engagement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tchiloli-living-theatre-of-sao-tome-and-principe-in-the-quest-of-justice-02309" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Music plays an important role in the performance</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageTchiloli, living theatre of Sao Tome and Principe in the ...UNESCO Tchiloli is a traditional form of open-air...</span></span></span>

## Costumes, Music and the Art of Cultural Transformation

One of the most striking aspects of Tchiloli is its visual world. Costumes evoke European courts, military figures and aristocratic dress, yet they appear within a performance shaped by African island traditions. The result can seem deliberately anachronistic, mixing different eras and cultural references in a way that reflects the islands' layered history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchiloli" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Music is equally important. Drums, wind instruments and rhythmic accompaniment help structure scenes and create emotional intensity. Rather than serving as a background element, music guides transitions, highlights dramatic moments and reinforces the communal nature of the performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tchiloli-living-theatre-of-sao-tome-and-principe-in-the-quest-of-justice-02309" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Music plays an important role in the performance</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageTchiloli, living theatre of Sao Tome and Principe in the ...UNESCO Tchiloli is a traditional form of open-air...</span></span></span>

Scholars frequently describe Tchiloli as a hybrid tradition, but that word can understate what happened. The performance is not simply a blend of European and African elements. Over generations, local communities reshaped inherited material into something with its own identity. ## Why Tchiloli Matters Today

Tchiloli remains one of the clearest examples of how folklore and performance can preserve collective memory while continually adapting to new circumstances. It demonstrates that traditional culture is not frozen in time. Instead, communities repeatedly reinterpret inherited stories to address their own concerns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/48593977" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[JSTOR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gazes from the Southby A Nolasco · 2020 · Cited by 3 — At the same time that the Tchiloli reactivates the memory of the African ance...</span></span></span>

For visitors, Tchiloli can appear unusual: an African island drama centred on Charlemagne, medieval nobles and a royal murder trial. Yet that apparent contradiction is precisely the point. The tradition reveals how stories travel, how communities remake them, and how questions about justice can remain meaningful across centuries and continents.
