## Introduction

Stambeli is one of the most distinctive ritual traditions in [Tunisia]({{ 'what-lives-inside-tunisias-folklore/' | relative_url }}) because it brings together music, trance, healing and the historical memory of Black Tunisian communities. More than a musical style, it is a ceremonial practice in which musicians, healers and participants seek relief from spiritual affliction, emotional distress or misfortune through rhythmic performance and controlled trance states. Its roots lie in the experiences of people brought north from sub-Saharan Africa through trans-Saharan migration and slavery, who carried spirit-possession traditions into Tunisia and adapted them to a North African Muslim environment. Today, Stambeli stands at the intersection of folklore, religion, music and historical memory, offering a rare window into how communities transformed painful histories into living cultural practice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://stambeli.com/the-stambeli/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stambeli.com">[stambeli.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stambeli.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Black spirits, white saints</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stambēlī is a music and trance healing tradition first developed in Tunisia by slaves and other displaced sub-Saharans.Read more...</span></span></span>

## What Does Stambeli Reveal About Tunisia?

Many forms of Tunisian folklore revolve around saints, jinn, sacred places or healing customs. Stambeli is unusual because it connects those themes directly to the African diaspora in North Africa. The tradition preserves memories of displacement and cultural encounter while remaining part of Tunisian society rather than existing outside it. Scholars of Tunisian ritual culture describe Stambeli as a product of centuries of contact between sub-Saharan Africans and North Africans, expressed through music, dance and spirit healing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/30336143/Black_Spirits_White_Saints_Music_Spirit_Possession_and_Sub_Saharans_in_Tunisia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[academia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Black Spirits, White Saints: Music, Spirit Possession, and...Stambeli serves as a healing ritual that connects sub-Saharan spiri...</span></span></span>

For this reason, Stambeli is often discussed not simply as a possession cult or musical genre but as a cultural archive. Through songs, rhythms, spirit names and ritual performance, it preserves traces of journeys across the Sahara that rarely survived in written records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fondazionegalleriamilano.it/en/special-project/%F0%9D%9F%AD-listening-to-the-invisible-music-possession-rituals-and-trance-in-tunisian-stambeli/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fondazionegalleriamilano.it">[fondazionegalleriamilano.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fondazionegalleriamilano.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fondazione Galleria Milano#𝟭 — Listening to the Invisible</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Music, possession rituals...Stambēlī is also an embodied historical archive—a “third space” where music, healing, historical memory, the...</span></span></span>

## Music, Trance and Healing Practice

At the centre of Stambeli is the belief that certain illnesses, emotional troubles or recurring misfortunes may be connected to relationships with spirits. Ritual specialists use music to identify, summon and appease these beings. Healing is not understood as defeating a spirit but as restoring balance between humans and the unseen forces affecting them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9780226392196" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliovault.org">[bibliovault.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliovault.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stambeli: Music, Trance, and Alterity in TunisiaStambeli music calls upon an elaborate pantheon of sub-Saharan spirits and Nor...</span></span></span>

Ceremonies typically build in intensity through repeated rhythms, singing and dancing. As the music grows denser and more insistent, some participants enter trance states. Within the tradition, these experiences are interpreted as signs that a particular spirit has responded to the ritual. The resulting trance allows healers to determine which spirit is involved and what actions may be required for reconciliation or healing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17411910701554021" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tandfonline.com">[tandfonline.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tandfonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">I had been researching stambeli, a ritual healing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Taylor &amp; Francis OnlineMusic, Spirit Possession and the In-Betweenby RC Jankowsky · 2007 · Cited by 89 — The first time I made musical co...</span></span></span>

The musical ensemble itself is crucial. Rhythmic patterns are not simply accompaniment but the mechanism through which communication with spirits becomes possible. Particular songs and rhythmic sequences are associated with specific supernatural beings, and the order of performance follows established ritual rules developed over generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226723501.003.0004" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[doi.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ritual Alterity: The Musical Management of Sub-Saharan...This chapter analyzes two approaches to the silsila ritual musical form of s...</span></span></span>

Unlike a public concert, a traditional Stambeli ceremony is aimed at producing a spiritual effect. Music is therefore inseparable from healing. Participants are not merely listening; they are engaging physically, emotionally and ritually with the performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pan-african-music.com/en/stambali-the-last-dance-with-the-spirits/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pan-african-music.com">[pan-african-music.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pan-african-music.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PAM &#124; Pan African Music Stambeli: the last dance with the spirits</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PAM &#124; Pan African MusicStambeli: the last dance with the spiritsJanuary 25, 2021 — 25 Jan 2021 — A pantheon of spirits born in sub-Sahara...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 25, 2021</span></span></span>

## Sub-Saharan Spirits and Tunisian Saints

One of the most fascinating aspects of Stambeli folklore is its supernatural landscape. The ritual world includes both spirits associated with sub-Saharan Africa and saints connected to North African Islam. Rather than replacing one tradition with another, Stambeli brings them into a shared ceremonial framework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9780226392196" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliovault.org">[bibliovault.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliovault.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stambeli: Music, Trance, and Alterity in TunisiaStambeli music calls upon an elaborate pantheon of sub-Saharan spirits and Nor...</span></span></span>

Researchers often describe the pantheon as involving both “Black” spirits linked to sub-Saharan origins and “White” saints associated with the Islamic sacred geography of North Africa. These categories are ritual rather than biological. They express different historical and spiritual lineages brought together within a single tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/30336143/Black_Spirits_White_Saints_Music_Spirit_Possession_and_Sub_Saharans_in_Tunisia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[academia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Black Spirits, White Saints: Music, Spirit Possession, and...Stambeli serves as a healing ritual that connects sub-Saharan spiri...</span></span></span>

Saints occupy an important place in Tunisian popular religion, and Stambeli adapted itself to that environment. Rituals may be connected to [saint shrines]({{ 'shrines/' | relative_url }}), pilgrimages or local holy figures familiar to other Tunisians. At the same time, songs continue to invoke spirits whose origins are remembered as lying further south across the Sahara. The result is a uniquely Tunisian form of spirit healing that neither abandons African heritage nor rejects local religious traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stambali" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

This blending is one reason Stambeli matters to folklore studies. It demonstrates how traditions change when communities migrate, encounter new religious landscapes and adapt without losing older memories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226723501.003.0004" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[doi.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ritual Alterity: The Musical Management of Sub-Saharan...This chapter analyzes two approaches to the silsila ritual musical form of s...</span></span></span>

## Memory, Identity and the Legacy of Slavery

The history behind Stambeli is inseparable from the history of slavery and migration in North Africa. Many practitioners trace the tradition to ancestors who arrived from regions south of the Sahara between the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, although some elements may be older. The music and rituals preserve memories of those movements long after written records have faded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9780226392196" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliovault.org">[bibliovault.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliovault.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stambeli: Music, Trance, and Alterity in TunisiaStambeli music calls upon an elaborate pantheon of sub-Saharan spirits and Nor...</span></span></span>

For Black Tunisians, Stambeli has often served as a way of maintaining cultural identity and ancestral connections. Yet it has never belonged exclusively to one community. Historical accounts and ethnographic studies show that Arab Tunisians also sought out Stambeli healers and participated in ceremonies, creating a broader social role for the tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/30336143/Black_Spirits_White_Saints_Music_Spirit_Possession_and_Sub_Saharans_in_Tunisia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Black Spirits, White Saints: Music, Spirit Possession, and...Stambeli serves as a healing ritual that connects sub-Saharan spiri...</span></span></span>

Because of this wider participation, Stambeli became more than a remembrance of African origins. It evolved into a shared space where questions of difference, belonging and cultural exchange could be negotiated through ritual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/30336143/Black_Spirits_White_Saints_Music_Spirit_Possession_and_Sub_Saharans_in_Tunisia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[academia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Black Spirits, White Saints: Music, Spirit Possession, and...Stambeli serves as a healing ritual that connects sub-Saharan spiri...</span></span></span>

## Heritage, Performance and Survival Today

Modern Stambeli faces significant challenges. Urbanisation, changing religious attitudes, social stigma around spirit possession and the decline of traditional ritual networks have reduced the number of active practitioners.
