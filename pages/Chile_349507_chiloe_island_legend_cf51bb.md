## Introduction

The Chiloé archipelago in southern [Chile]({{ 'where-chiles-landscapes-become-legend/' | relative_url }}) has long been described as the country’s great “myth island”. Scattered across foggy channels, dense forests and isolated coastal settlements, the islands developed a folklore tradition unlike any other in Chile. Sea travel was essential, storms were dangerous, and communities often lived far from political centres. Out of that environment emerged stories of ghost ships, forest spirits, witches’ societies and strange creatures that still shape Chiloé’s cultural identity today. Rather than a single legend, Chiloé offers an interconnected world in which the sea, the woods and hidden supernatural powers constantly overlap.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilote_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Chilote mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 3, 2025 — The Chilote mythology or Chilota mythology is formed by the myths, legends and beliefs of the people who live in the C...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 3, 2025</span></span></span>

What makes these traditions especially notable is the way they blend Indigenous beliefs, colonial influences and local experience. The result is a folklore landscape where a phantom ship may transport the dead, a forest being may explain social anxieties, and secret witch organisations may be imagined as exercising power alongside official authorities. Many of these stories remain widely known through festivals, museums, tourism, literature and popular culture, even as modern islanders understand them in different ways today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/66860784/The_Cultural_Mutations_of_Chilo%C3%A9_myths_and_legends_in_the_neoliberal_modernity_of_the_island" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[academia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cultural Mutations of Chiloé: myths and legends in...Mythological beings such as Trauco, the Caleuche, the Pincoya, the Vola...</span></span></span>

## Why Chiloé Became Chile's Myth Island

Chiloé’s geography encouraged the growth of distinctive folklore. For centuries, travel between communities depended more on boats than roads. Thick forests covered much of the landscape, while fog, rough seas and shipwrecks were familiar realities. In such conditions, stories became a way of explaining danger, uncertainty and the hidden forces thought to influence everyday life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilote_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Chilote mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 3, 2025 — The Chilote mythology or Chilota mythology is formed by the myths, legends and beliefs of the people who live in the C...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 3, 2025</span></span></span>

The archipelago also occupied a cultural crossroads. Indigenous traditions, especially those associated with the Huilliche people, mixed with Spanish Catholic ideas and local storytelling. Rather than replacing older beliefs, new influences often merged with them. The resulting mythology is filled with hybrid figures: sea spirits, enchanted ships, magical beings and witches who operate within a world that is recognisably Chilote rather than purely Indigenous or European.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilote_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Chilote mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 3, 2025 — The Chilote mythology or Chilota mythology is formed by the myths, legends and beliefs of the people who live in the C...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 3, 2025</span></span></span>

## The Caleuche and the Haunted Sea

Among all Chilean legends, none is more closely associated with Chiloé than the Caleuche, a ghost ship said to appear suddenly among the islands at night. Witnesses in the stories describe a brilliantly illuminated vessel accompanied by music, laughter and celebration. It often vanishes as quickly as it appears, sometimes disappearing into fog or even beneath the water.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleuche" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The ship’s crew varies according to the version of the tale. In some accounts, it is populated by sailors who drowned at sea and were granted a strange afterlife aboard the vessel. In others, the ship is linked to powerful sorcerers who use it to travel between islands or conduct supernatural business. Some traditions claim that fishermen who encounter the ship may be taken aboard and never return to ordinary life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vayaadventures.com/blog/el-caleuche-the-ghost-ship-of-chiloe/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vayaadventures.com">[vayaadventures.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vayaadventures.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vaya AdventuresEl Caleuche: The Ghost Ship of ChiloéNov 1, 2017 — Some say the Caleuche is a living being, luring sailors and fishermen a...</span></span></span>

The Caleuche matters because it reflects the central importance of the sea in Chilote life. Fishing communities depended on waters that could provide wealth one day and death the next. The legend transforms those uncertainties into a vivid supernatural narrative. Like ghost ship traditions elsewhere in the world, the Caleuche may have absorbed European influences, including comparisons with the Flying Dutchman, but it developed into a distinctly Chilote story with its own characters and meanings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleuche" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Even today, the ship remains one of Chile’s most recognisable folklore symbols. It appears in novels, films, songs and tourism materials, and its image continues to represent the mysterious maritime identity of the archipelago.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0433533/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: imdb.com">[imdb.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">imdb.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Caleuche: The Call of the Sea (2012)A young woman who suffers a strange disease must travel to a mysterious island in South America...</span></span></span>

## The Trauco and Forest Anxieties

If the Caleuche rules the sea, the Trauco dominates the forests. This creature is usually described as a small, ugly humanoid being that lives deep among the trees. Despite his appearance, he possesses an irresistible supernatural attraction. According to the traditional stories, women who fall under his influence cannot resist him, while men may fear his power and even his gaze.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trauco" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The Trauco legend has often been interpreted as more than a monster story. Historically, it functioned as a social explanation for pregnancies that could not easily be discussed within conservative communities. By attributing an unexpected pregnancy to the Trauco, blame could be shifted away from a woman and onto a supernatural being. Folklore scholars and social historians have therefore viewed the legend as reflecting community attitudes toward sexuality, reputation and social control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trauco" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The creature also reveals another side of Chilote folklore. Whereas the Caleuche belongs to open water and communal experience, the Trauco inhabits the hidden spaces of the forest. His stories centre on vulnerability, desire and fear rather than storms and navigation. Together, the two figures create a symbolic map of the archipelago’s most important environments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trauco" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Witches, Caves and Secret Authority

Perhaps the most unusual aspect of Chilote folklore is the tradition of organised witchcraft. Unlike the solitary witches of many European fairy tales, Chiloé developed stories about a secret society of sorcerers that supposedly operated across the islands. These figures, often called brujos, were believed to possess magical powers, control supernatural beings and influence local events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cascada.travel/blog/the-haunting-mythology-of-chile-s-southern-islands" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cascada.travel">[Cascada Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cascada.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Haunting Mythology of Chile&#x27;s Southern IslandsPerhaps Chiloé&#x27;s most well-known and disturbing legends involve the brujos, a coven of...</span></span></span>

The centre of many stories is a hidden cave where witches allegedly met, trained initiates and guarded magical secrets. Nineteenth-century legal investigations into alleged witchcraft associations on Chiloé helped cement these stories in public memory. Although historians treat the claims with caution, the trials themselves demonstrate that beliefs about organised witchcraft were taken seriously enough to attract official attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cascada.travel/blog/the-haunting-mythology-of-chile-s-southern-islands" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cascada.travel">[Cascada Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cascada.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Haunting Mythology of Chile&#x27;s Southern IslandsPerhaps Chiloé&#x27;s most well-known and disturbing legends involve the brujos, a coven of...</span></span></span>

The witches were linked to several famous creatures. One was the Invunche, a grotesque guardian figure said to protect secret places and serve the sorcerers. In many versions of the folklore, witches also maintained connections with the Caleuche, creating a unified supernatural network that linked the sea, the land and hidden centres of power.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://esoterx.com/2013/01/22/dead-and-loving-it-spectral-seafaring-with-the-ghost-ship-el-caleuche-of-the-chilean-chilotes/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esoterx.com">[esoterx.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esoterx.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dead and Loving It: Spectral Seafaring with the Ghost Ship...Jan 22, 2013 — Essentially, El Caleuche is a party boat for the drow...</span></span></span>

What makes these stories particularly interesting is their political dimension. The witches often appear as a parallel authority structure, operating outside church and state institutions. In folklore, they can reward, punish, control information and influence prosperity. This gives the tales a social role beyond simple entertainment, reflecting concerns about power, secrecy and community leadership in isolated island settlements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cascada.travel/blog/the-haunting-mythology-of-chile-s-southern-islands" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cascada.travel">[Cascada Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cascada.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Haunting Mythology of Chile&#x27;s Southern IslandsPerhaps Chiloé&#x27;s most well-known and disturbing legends involve the brujos, a coven of...</span></span></span>

## Monsters Between Land and Sea
