## Introduction

Rozafa Castle stands above the city of Shkodër as one of [Albania]({{ 'albania/' | relative_url }})’s most recognisable landmarks, but its fame comes from more than its ancient walls. For many Albanians, the fortress is inseparable from the legend of Rozafa, a young mother who was sacrificed so that a castle could finally stand. The story combines a real place with one of the most powerful themes in Balkan folklore: the belief that lasting works sometimes demand a terrible human cost. Over centuries, the legend has become far more than a tale about construction. It is a story about keeping one’s word, maternal devotion, communal survival and the painful sacrifices made for future generations. Today the castle functions simultaneously as an archaeological monument, a tourist destination and a living symbol in Albanian cultural memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://akt.gov.al/en/heritage/The-rozafa-castle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: akt.gov.al">[Faqja Zyrtare e Turizmit]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">akt.gov.al</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Faqja Zyrtare e Turizmit Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This name is also associated with a legend, which speaks of one of...Read more...</span></span></span>

## The castle, the brothers and the broken walls

The real castle has a long history. Rising on a rocky hill above the rivers Buna and Drin, the site was fortified in antiquity and served successive Illyrian, Roman, medieval, Venetian and Ottoman powers. Much of what visitors see today reflects later rebuilding, especially from the Venetian period, although the fortress itself has roots reaching back to the early centuries before the Common Era.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozafa_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rozafa Castle</span></span></span>

The folklore attached to the site tells a very different story. According to the best-known version, three brothers attempted to build a fortress. Every day they raised walls, and every night those walls mysteriously collapsed. Unable to understand why their work would not endure, they sought advice from an old man or wise stranger. He revealed that the structure would stand only if a living human sacrifice were enclosed within its walls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozafa_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rozafa Castle</span></span></span>

The brothers swore an oath that none would warn his wife. The next day, whichever wife arrived first with food for the workers would become the sacrifice. Two brothers secretly broke their promise and warned their wives. The youngest brother remained faithful to his oath. As a result, his wife, Rozafa, unknowingly came to the building site and was chosen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozafa_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rozafa Castle</span></span></span>

The story is often presented as a test of character as much as a tale of sacrifice. The walls collapse because something essential is missing. The fortress can only be completed when a promise is honoured, even though the result is tragic. In this way, the legend links physical construction with moral obligations.

One reason the story became so influential is that it is not merely attached to an imaginary place. Visitors standing inside Rozafa Castle encounter the same landscape associated with the tale, allowing folklore and geography to reinforce one another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://akt.gov.al/en/kala/Shkodra-Castle-Rozafa-Castle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: akt.gov.al">[Faqja Zyrtare e Turizmit]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">akt.gov.al</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Faqja Zyrtare e TurizmitShkodra Castle (Rozafa Castle) - Official Tourism WebsiteThe legend of Rozafa, a famous story of sacrifice and lo...</span></span></span>

## Motherhood, oath-keeping and sacrifice

The most memorable part of the legend begins after Rozafa learns her fate. Rather than protesting or attempting escape, she accepts the sacrifice but makes a final request. One breast, one hand, one eye and one foot should remain outside the wall so that she can continue to nurse, comfort and watch over her infant son. In many retellings, she asks to remain a mother even after becoming part of the fortress itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozafa_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rozafa Castle</span></span></span>

This detail transforms the story from a simple foundation legend into something emotionally distinctive. The central figure is not a warrior, ruler or saint, but a mother. Her sacrifice is meaningful because it preserves both the castle and her relationship with her child. The legend therefore combines two powerful values:

* **Keeping one’s oath**, even when the consequences are severe.
* **Maternal devotion**, which continues beyond ordinary human limits.

The oath is especially important in Albanian interpretations. The youngest brother’s refusal to betray his word is often presented as a demonstration of honour. The tragedy emerges not from deceit by enemies but from conflicting duties: loyalty to family, loyalty to one’s promise and loyalty to the wider community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozafa_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rozafa Castle</span></span></span>

Folklore scholars have also drawn attention to the story’s symbolic treatment of motherhood. Some interpretations connect Rozafa’s exposed breast and continuing nourishment of her child with older ideas about fertility, protection and the nurturing power of the earth itself. These readings do not necessarily describe the original meaning of the legend, but they help explain why the image of the nursing mother remained so memorable across generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozafa_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rozafa Castle</span></span></span>

Local tradition adds another striking detail. White mineral deposits on parts of the castle have long been associated with Rozafa’s milk. Folk belief held that these traces represented the continuing flow of nourishment from the sacrificed mother, turning a natural feature of the site into physical evidence of the story. Women were reported to collect or scrape the deposits for traditional purposes connected with nursing and motherhood.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozafa_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rozafa Castle</span></span></span>

## A legend with deep Balkan roots

Although Rozafa is strongly identified with Albania, the underlying motif belongs to a much wider family of Balkan foundation legends. Stories about bridges, monasteries, castles or fortresses that require a human sacrifice appear across southeastern Europe. Folklorists often refer to this pattern as the “immurement” or “foundation sacrifice” motif.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozafa_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rozafa Castle</span></span></span>

The Albanian version is distinctive because of its emotional focus on the mother and child. Similar stories exist elsewhere, but Rozafa’s request to continue caring for her son gives the tale a particularly memorable identity. Rather than being remembered primarily as a victim, she becomes the active protector of the structure and of future generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozafa_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rozafa Castle</span></span></span>

The legend is also surprisingly old in written form. The story was recorded by the Albanian humanist Marin Barleti in the early sixteenth century, showing that it was already well established centuries ago. That written testimony makes Rozafa one of the best-attested foundation legends in the Albanian tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozafa_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rozafa Castle</span></span></span>

## How a local legend became a national symbol

Many castle legends remain local curiosities. Rozafa became something larger. Over time, the story moved from oral tradition into literature, education, tourism and national symbolism. The castle itself became one of Albania’s best-known historic sites, and the legend came to represent themes that many readers and listeners considered distinctly Albanian: endurance, honour, loyalty and sacrifice for the common good.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://akt.gov.al/en/heritage/The-rozafa-castle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: akt.gov.al">[Faqja Zyrtare e Turizmit]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">akt.gov.al</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Faqja Zyrtare e Turizmit Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This name is also associated with a legend, which speaks of one of...Read more...</span></span></span>

Modern writers helped extend its influence. The motif of the walled-in woman attracted literary reinterpretations, including works by major Albanian authors who used the legend to explore questions of power, truth, memory and collective responsibility. Scholars have noted that these retellings transformed an old folk narrative into a vehicle for reflecting on modern society while preserving its emotional core.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/20064784" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[JSTOR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard argue that Kadare&#x27;s Raymond photo. novel and its precursor legend of sacrifice have helped to preserve this ancient...Read more...</span></span></span>

Tourism has also played a role. Visitors often arrive at Rozafa Castle because of its history and panoramic views, but the legend remains central to how the site is presented. The story gives emotional meaning to the ruins, allowing the fortress to function not only as an archaeological monument but also as a place of storytelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://akt.gov.al/en/heritage/The-rozafa-castle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: akt.gov.al">[akt.gov.al]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">akt.gov.al</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Faqja Zyrtare e Turizmit Rozafa Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This name is also associated with a legend, which speaks of one of...Read more...</span></span></span>

What makes Rozafa endure is the tension at the heart of the tale. The castle survives because a mother is lost. The community gains security through personal tragedy. The story never fully resolves whether that sacrifice was justified, and that unresolved question keeps the legend alive.
