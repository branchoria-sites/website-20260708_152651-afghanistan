## Introduction

Few figures in Indonesian folklore are as famous, feared and widely discussed as Nyai Roro Kidul, the Queen of the Southern Sea. Along the Indian Ocean coast of Java, especially around the beaches of Yogyakarta and West Java, she is believed to rule a powerful spirit realm beneath the waves. Stories about her help explain the dangers of one of [Indonesia]({{ 'why-indonesias-folklore-still-haunts-the/' | relative_url }})’s most treacherous coastlines, where strong currents, high surf and sudden waves have claimed lives for centuries. Rather than being merely a ghost story, the tradition connects landscape, royal authority, local ritual, popular culture and everyday ideas about respect for nature. Today, Nyai Roro Kidul remains a living presence in folklore, tourism, art, film and local belief, making her one of the most influential supernatural figures in Indonesia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1984_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[Persée]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the Indian Ocean off the southern coast of Java. ## Who Is Java's Queen of the Southern Sea?

### The Spirit Queen and the South Coast

Nyai Roro Kidul is usually described as the ruler of the Southern Ocean, the stretch of the Indian Ocean that lies beyond Java’s southern shoreline. In different traditions she appears as a beautiful woman, a spirit queen, a mermaid-like being, or a shape-shifting supernatural ruler. Her story exists in both Javanese and Sundanese traditions, and there is no single agreed version of her origin. Some legends portray her as a cursed princess who entered the sea and became a supernatural queen, while others connect her to much older beliefs about the ocean as a sacred and dangerous realm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyai_Roro_Kidul" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nyai Roro Kidul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nyai Roro Kidul</span></span></span>

Scholars have noted that the figure seems to combine several layers of tradition. The powerful Indian Ocean off southern Java inspired both fear and reverence, and over time the sea came to be imagined as the domain of a female ruler who controlled storms, waves and unseen forces. Rather than representing a single mythological origin, Nyai Roro Kidul became a cultural symbol through which communities explained the unpredictable power of the coast.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1984_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[persee.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the Indian Ocean off the southern coast of Java. One reason the tradition became so influential is its connection to the historic Javanese courts. Since at least the period of the Mataram kingdom, rulers have been linked symbolically to the Queen of the Southern Sea. Folklore recounts meetings between the spirit queen and royal founders, especially Panembahan Senopati, the creator of Mataram. These stories helped place political authority within a larger sacred landscape stretching from volcanoes to palaces and finally to the southern ocean.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1984_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[persee.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the Indian Ocean off the southern coast of Java. ### Why the Coast Became Haunted

The southern coast of Java is famous for powerful surf, rip currents and dramatic waves. In many places there are few protective reefs, allowing the force of the Indian Ocean to strike directly against the shore. Folklore transformed these natural dangers into a spirit landscape populated by the Queen and her supernatural followers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyai_Roro_Kidul" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nyai Roro Kidul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nyai Roro Kidul</span></span></span>

Stories often claim that the Queen occasionally takes people into her underwater realm. Modern accounts sometimes connect drownings or disappearances to her influence. From a folkloric perspective, such stories serve an important social function. They transform environmental danger into memorable narratives that encourage caution and respect. Rather than treating the sea as ordinary, the tradition teaches that it possesses power and demands proper behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.phdeed.com/articles/dangerous-and-powerful-guardian-spirit-queen-south-seas-kanjeng-ratu-kidul" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phdeed.com">[phdeed.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phdeed.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Therefore, like</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Dangerous and Powerful Guardian Spirit Queen of the...As queen of the Southern Ocean, Kanjeng Ratu Kidul (or Nyai Loro Kidul) is hel...</span></span></span>

One of the most famous beliefs concerns the colour green. Visitors to parts of Java’s southern coast are often warned against wearing green clothing because the colour is associated with the Queen. Explanations vary: some say she favours the colour and dislikes others wearing it, while others connect the belief to the green appearance of the sea itself. Whatever its origin, the warning remains one of Indonesia’s best-known pieces of coastal folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyai_Roro_Kidul" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nyai Roro Kidul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nyai Roro Kidul</span></span></span>

## Fishing Communities, Danger and Protection

For many coastal communities, Nyai Roro Kidul is not simply a frightening figure. She can also be viewed as a protector who deserves respect and ritual attention. Along parts of the south coast, ceremonies involving offerings to the sea seek harmony between humans and the forces believed to inhabit the ocean. These practices reflect a broader Indonesian pattern in which potentially dangerous spirits may also provide protection when approached correctly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1997_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[Persée]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nyai Roro Kidul is known all along the southern</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PerséeNyai Roro Kidul in Puger: Local Applications of a Mythby R Wessing · 1997 · Cited by 31 — One such mythological figure in Java is...</span></span></span>

The Queen’s importance varies from place to place. Anthropological studies have found that local communities adapt the tradition according to their own concerns. In some areas she is primarily associated with the sea’s dangers. Elsewhere she is linked to prosperity, spiritual power, protection of fishermen or the moral order of the community. The result is not one fixed legend but a family of related traditions spread across Java’s southern shoreline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1997_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[persee.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nyai Roro Kidul is known all along the southern</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PerséeNyai Roro Kidul in Puger: Local Applications of a Mythby R Wessing · 1997 · Cited by 31 — One such mythological figure in Java is...</span></span></span>

Certain beaches have become especially famous within the folklore. Parangtritis and Parangkusumo in Yogyakarta are among the best-known locations associated with the Queen. These sites attract visitors seeking spiritual experiences, participating in rituals or simply engaging with one of Indonesia’s most famous legends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyai_Roro_Kidul" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nyai Roro Kidul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nyai Roro Kidul</span></span></span>

## From Local Belief to Film and Tourism

Nyai Roro Kidul’s influence extends far beyond oral tradition. She has become a recurring figure in Indonesian literature, theatre, painting, television and cinema. Traditional performances have retold her stories for generations, while modern films have reinvented her as everything from a mysterious protector to a supernatural seductress. These portrayals often reveal more about contemporary anxieties and popular tastes than about older folklore, but they have helped keep the legend visible to new audiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyai_Roro_Kidul" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nyai Roro Kidul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nyai Roro Kidul</span></span></span>

Tourism has also reshaped the tradition. Hotels, beaches and attractions sometimes incorporate imagery associated with the Queen. One famous example is the long-discussed Room 308 at the Samudra Beach Hotel in West Java, maintained according to local tradition as a room reserved for her spirit presence. Whether visitors regard such places as sacred, historical or simply intriguing, they demonstrate how folklore can become part of a region’s cultural economy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyai_Roro_Kidul" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nyai Roro Kidul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nyai Roro Kidul</span></span></span>
