## Introduction

Mount Paektu is the single most important mythic landscape in [North Korea]({{ 'north-korea/' | relative_url }}), but it is also one of the clearest examples of how ancient sacred tradition and modern political storytelling can become intertwined. Rising on the border between North Korea and China, the volcanic mountain has long been regarded as a holy place in Korean culture. It is associated with origin legends, mountain worship, sacred geography and the idea of a Korean ancestral homeland. At the same time, the North Korean state has transformed the mountain into the symbolic birthplace of its revolution and the ruling Kim family. Understanding Mount Paektu therefore requires separating older folklore from modern state mythology while recognising that both draw power from the same dramatic landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/iggp/mt-paektu-unesco-global-geopark" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mt Paektu UNESCO Global Geopark</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mt Paektu UNESCO Global Geopark...</span></span></span>

## Sacred Mountain Traditions

Long before North Korea existed, Mount Paektu occupied a special place in Korean imagination. The mountain's crater lake, forests, volcanic slopes and remote northern location helped create an image of a place closer to the heavens than ordinary human settlements. Across centuries, Koreans treated mountains not merely as geographical features but as spiritually significant landscapes inhabited by powerful forces and mountain [spirits]({{ 'spirits-dcf29a/' | relative_url }}). Mount Paektu stood at the summit of that sacred geography.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Paektu" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mount Paektu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mount Paektu</span></span></span>

The mountain became especially important because it was linked to the most famous Korean foundation legend: the story of [Dangun]({{ 'dangun/' | relative_url }}), the mythical founder of the first Korean kingdom. In many retellings, Mount Paektu serves as the setting for the descent of heavenly power into the human world. Whether understood as myth, national origin story or symbolic narrative, the association gave the mountain a status unlike any other place on the Korean Peninsula.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Paektu" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mount Paektu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mount Paektu</span></span></span>

Its significance extends beyond a single tale. Historians and scholars of Korean religion have noted that Paektu became a focal point where ideas of ancestry, landscape and identity merged. Even today, many Koreans in both North and South Korea regard the mountain as a symbolic homeland regardless of political borders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://keia.org/analysis/the-mountain-at-the-heart-of-koreas-past-and-future/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: keia.org">[keia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">keia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the mountain at the heart of koreas past and future</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Korea Economic Institute of AmericaThe Mountain at the Heart of Korea&#x27;s Past and Future3 Oct 2018 — According to North Korean legend, the...</span></span></span>

The mountain's physical appearance reinforces its legendary status. The enormous crater lake, often called Heaven Lake in English, sits within the remains of a colossal volcanic eruption. The dramatic setting naturally encouraged stories of divine origins, sacred power and national destiny. UNESCO's recognition of Mount Paektu as a Global Geopark reflects both its geological importance and its cultural significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/iggp/mt-paektu-unesco-global-geopark" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mt Paektu UNESCO Global Geopark</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mt Paektu UNESCO Global Geopark...</span></span></span>

## The Revolutionary Mountain Story

North Korea did not invent Mount Paektu's sacred status, but it radically reshaped its meaning.

After the establishment of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea, official narratives increasingly portrayed the mountain as the centre of the anti-Japanese revolutionary struggle led by Kim Il Sung. State histories describe guerrilla bases on or near the mountain and present Paektu as the birthplace of revolutionary spirit. The mountain gradually became known in official language as the “sacred mountain of the revolution.”<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://keia.org/analysis/the-mountain-at-the-heart-of-koreas-past-and-future/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: keia.org">[keia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">keia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the mountain at the heart of koreas past and future</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Korea Economic Institute of AmericaThe Mountain at the Heart of Korea&#x27;s Past and Future3 Oct 2018 — According to North Korean legend, the...</span></span></span>

This transformation allowed the state to connect modern political authority to much older ideas about sacred landscape. Rather than presenting the revolution as merely a twentieth-century political event, propaganda linked it to the deepest layers of Korean mythic history. The result was a powerful symbolic chain: Korea began at Mount Paektu, the revolution was born at Mount Paektu, and the ruling leadership derived its legitimacy from Mount Paektu.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.2711711.9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[jstor.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PAEKTUSAN – SACRED MOUNTAIN OF THE REVOLUTIONPaektusan or Mount Paektu is considered the most prominent mountain on the. A key element of this narrative is the claim that Kim Jong Il was born at a secret guerrilla camp on the mountain. North Korean accounts describe extraordinary signs accompanying the birth, including miraculous natural phenomena and heavenly omens. Outside historians, however, overwhelmingly conclude that Kim Jong Il was actually born in the Soviet Union while his parents were connected to anti-Japanese forces operating under Soviet authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_the_Shining_Star" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Day of the Shining Star</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Day of the Shining Star</span></span></span>

The importance of the claim lies less in the historical question than in its symbolic function. By locating Kim Jong Il's birth at Korea's most sacred mountain, state mythology places him within a landscape already associated with national origins and spiritual legitimacy. The mountain becomes not just a backdrop but a source of authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paektusan_Secret_Camp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Paektusan Secret Camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Paektusan Secret Camp</span></span></span>

## How Folklore Became Political Myth

One reason Mount Paektu fascinates folklorists is that it shows how modern states can adapt older mythic structures.

Traditional folklore often uses sacred mountains as places where heaven and earth meet, heroes are born and the fate of nations is decided. North Korean propaganda borrowed these familiar narrative patterns and applied them to contemporary political leaders. Stories of miraculous births, prophetic signs, sacred bloodlines and destiny are all common features of legendary traditions worldwide. In North Korea they were repurposed to support a hereditary political system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/new-goddess-on-mount-paektu-myth-and-transformation-in-north-kore/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: research.ed.ac.uk">[ed.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">research.ed.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">new goddess on mount paektu myth and transformation in north kore</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">inburgh ResearchNew Goddess on Mount Paektu: Myth and Transformation in...by R Winstanley-Chesters · 2020 · Cited by 2 — This book sug...</span></span></span>

The concept of the “Mount Paektu bloodline” is perhaps the clearest example. Official ideology presents the descendants of Kim Il Sung as possessing a unique revolutionary inheritance connected to the mountain itself. The phrase turns a family lineage into something resembling a sacred dynasty rooted in a mythic landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_family_%28North_Korea%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kim family (North Korea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 11, 2026 — The Kim family is the three-generation lineage of North Korean leadership, descending from the country&#x27;s founder and first...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 11, 2026</span></span></span>

Researchers have also noted how state narratives imitate older folk motifs. Trees allegedly bearing prophetic inscriptions, heavenly signs announcing a birth and miraculous natural events are all devices familiar from legends and saintly biographies in many cultures. Their appearance in modern propaganda demonstrates how effectively folklore structures can be adapted to political purposes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_the_Shining_Star" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Day of the Shining Star</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Day of the Shining Star</span></span></span>

## Old Belief Versus State Narrative

The most important distinction for readers is that Mount Paektu's sacred status did not originate with the North Korean state.

The mountain was already revered centuries before the Kim family emerged. Its role in Korean origin myths, mountain worship traditions and cultural identity is documented across multiple historical periods and across political boundaries. These older traditions belong to Korean culture as a whole rather than to any modern government.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Paektu" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mount Paektu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mount Paektu</span></span></span>

The revolutionary mythology, by contrast, is largely a twentieth-century creation. It takes genuine historical elements—anti-Japanese resistance, the symbolic importance of northern borderlands and the mountain's sacred reputation—and combines them with narratives designed to legitimise the ruling family. Historians therefore distinguish between the mountain's ancient folkloric significance and the state's later political reinterpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://keia.org/analysis/the-mountain-at-the-heart-of-koreas-past-and-future/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: keia.org">[keia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">keia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the mountain at the heart of koreas past and future</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Korea Economic Institute of AmericaThe Mountain at the Heart of Korea&#x27;s Past and Future3 Oct 2018 — According to North Korean legend, the...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because discussions of Mount Paektu sometimes collapse the two traditions into one. The mountain's connection to Dangun and sacred geography belongs to a broad and long-lived cultural tradition. Claims about miraculous births, prophetic signs and hereditary revolutionary destiny belong primarily to North Korean state mythology. ## Why the Mountain Still Matters

Mount Paektu remains a powerful symbol because it continues to operate on several levels at once. It is a geological wonder, a sacred landscape, a symbol of Korean identity and a political icon. Leaders visit it during significant moments, state media regularly invokes it, and its image appears throughout North Korean political culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Paektu" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mount Paektu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mount Paektu</span></span></span>

Yet its significance extends beyond politics. For many Koreans, including those outside North Korea, the mountain remains tied to ideas of ancestry, cultural memory and national origins. That broader cultural meaning helps explain why the state has invested so heavily in claiming the mountain's symbolism. A political myth is most effective when it grows from stories people already know.
