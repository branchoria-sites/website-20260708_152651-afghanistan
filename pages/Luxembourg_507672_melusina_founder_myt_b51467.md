## Introduction

Why does [Luxembourg]({{ 'where-luxembourgs-legends-still-live/' | relative_url }}) have a mermaid founder? The short answer is that the country's best-known legend transforms the historical founding of Luxembourg City into a story about love, secrecy and a broken promise. In history, Count Siegfried acquired the rocky Bock promontory in 963 and established the stronghold known as Lucilinburhuc, the nucleus of the future city. In folklore, that same landscape becomes the setting for his marriage to Melusina, a mysterious woman who is revealed to be part mermaid and who disappears forever when her husband violates a sacred taboo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxembourg-city.com/en/about-luxembourg-city/meng-stad-my-city/details/melusina-and-siegfried" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxembourg-city.com">[luxembourg-city.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxembourg-city.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In 963, he exchanged land and acquired the Bock promontory above the Alzette valley. The legend matters because it does more than explain a city's origins. It gives Luxembourg a founding myth rooted in a specific place: the cliffs of the Bock, the waters of the Alzette and the valleys beneath the old fortress. Over centuries, Melusina became a symbol of the capital itself, linking a documented medieval foundation to a supernatural tale that still shapes public memory, tourism, art and local identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxembourg-city.com/en/about-luxembourg-city/meng-stad-my-city/details/melusina-and-siegfried" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxembourg-city.com">[Luxembourg City]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxembourg-city.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In 963, he exchanged land and acquired the Bock promontory above the Alzette valley. ## Siegfried, the Bock and Lucilinburhuc

The historical core of the story is unusually clear. In 963, Count Siegfried acquired the Bock promontory overlooking the Alzette valley and built a castle there. The name Lucilinburhuc, often translated as “little castle”, appears in connection with this foundation and is regarded as the origin of the name Luxembourg. The Bock therefore occupies a unique place in both history and legend: it is simultaneously the real birthplace of the city and the mythical home of Melusina.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxembourg-city.com/en/about-luxembourg-city/meng-stad-my-city/details/melusina-and-siegfried" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxembourg-city.com">[luxembourg-city.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxembourg-city.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In 963, he exchanged land and acquired the Bock promontory above the Alzette valley. On this promontory...</span></span></span>

According to the traditional legend, Siegfried was hunting near the Alzette when he heard an enchanting song. Following the voice, he encountered a beautiful woman named Melusina. He fell in love immediately and asked her to marry him. She agreed, but only on one condition: he must never disturb her or attempt to see her on Saturdays. Siegfried accepted the condition, and the pair married. In many versions of the story, their union is linked directly to the creation or flourishing of the castle on the Bock, turning the city's foundation into a supernatural marriage between a human ruler and a water spirit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxembourg-city.com/en/about-luxembourg-city/meng-stad-my-city/details/melusina-and-siegfried" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxembourg-city.com">[luxembourg-city.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxembourg-city.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In 963, he exchanged land and acquired the Bock promontory above the Alzette valley. On this promontory...</span></span></span>

This combination of documented history and legendary narrative helps explain the tale's endurance. Unlike many foundation myths that float free of geography, Melusina's story is attached to landmarks that still exist. Visitors can stand on the Bock and look down into the same valley where the legend places the first meeting between Siegfried and the mysterious woman.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitluxembourg.com/guided-tour/siegfried-melusina" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitluxembourg.com">[Visit Luxembourg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitluxembourg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit LuxembourgSiegfried &amp; MelusinaThis guided tour of approximately one hour invites you to immerse yourself in one of Luxembourg&#x27;s mos...</span></span></span>

## The Saturday taboo and Melusina's disappearance

At the heart of the legend lies a familiar folkloric theme: a supernatural spouse who demands secrecy. Similar stories appear across Europe, but Luxembourg's version is anchored firmly to its capital city and its founding count. Melusina's condition was simple yet absolute. Every Saturday she required complete privacy. For years, Siegfried respected the agreement. Eventually curiosity overcame him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxembourg-city.com/en/about-luxembourg-city/meng-stad-my-city/details/melusina-and-siegfried" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxembourg-city.com">[luxembourg-city.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxembourg-city.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In 963, he exchanged land and acquired the Bock promontory above the Alzette valley. On this promontory...</span></span></span>

When Siegfried secretly watched her, he discovered the truth. Melusina was not entirely human. Depending on the version, she appeared as a mermaid, a woman with a fish tail, or a supernatural water being whose true form emerged only in private. The moment he broke his promise, the marriage ended. Melusina vanished into the waters or disappeared into the rock beneath the Bock, beyond human reach.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxembourg-city.com/en/about-luxembourg-city/meng-stad-my-city/details/melusina-and-siegfried" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxembourg-city.com">[luxembourg-city.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxembourg-city.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In 963, he exchanged land and acquired the Bock promontory above the Alzette valley. The emotional power of the story comes from the fact that no villain is required. The tragedy grows from distrust and curiosity rather than malice. Siegfried loses both his wife and the magical world she represented because he cannot honour the boundary she set. For that reason, the legend is often remembered not merely as a fairy tale but as a cautionary story about promises and trust.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxembourg-city.com/en/about-luxembourg-city/meng-stad-my-city/details/melusina-and-siegfried" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxembourg-city.com">[Luxembourg City]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxembourg-city.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In 963, he exchanged land and acquired the Bock promontory above the Alzette valley. Many later retellings expanded the ending. One widely repeated version says that Melusina returns every seven years near the Bock, seeking someone brave enough to free her from her enchantment. She appears briefly before disappearing again into the depths of the rock. These additions strengthened her connection with Luxembourg City's landscape and helped keep the story alive long after its medieval origins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxembourg-city.com/en/about-luxembourg-city/meng-stad-my-city/details/melusina-and-siegfried" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxembourg-city.com">[Luxembourg City]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxembourg-city.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In 963, he exchanged land and acquired the Bock promontory above the Alzette valley. ## A Luxembourg version of a wider European legend

Melusina did not originate in Luxembourg alone. She belongs to a much larger family of European legends about water spirits, fairy brides and women who conceal a supernatural form. Medieval literature connected Melusine figures with noble dynasties in France and neighbouring regions, and stories about mysterious wives who become serpents, dragons or mermaids were already circulating centuries before modern Luxembourg emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melusine" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

What makes the Luxembourg tradition distinctive is the way it localises this broader myth. Instead of being attached to a distant fairy kingdom or an unnamed castle, the story is anchored to a specific historical figure, Count Siegfried, and to a specific place, the Bock. The legend effectively claims that the city's origins lie at the meeting point of human history and supernatural destiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxembourg-city.com/en/about-luxembourg-city/meng-stad-my-city/details/melusina-and-siegfried" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxembourg-city.com">[luxembourg-city.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxembourg-city.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In 963, he exchanged land and acquired the Bock promontory above the Alzette valley. On this promontory...</span></span></span>

This blending of imported medieval motifs with local geography is common in European folklore. A familiar legendary figure becomes culturally powerful when communities connect it to recognisable landscapes and historical memories. In Luxembourg, that process produced a national folk heroine rather than merely another fairy tale character.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melusine" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## How the legend survives in the modern city

Melusina remains one of the most visible figures in Luxembourgish folklore. Far from being confined to old story collections, she is woven into the city's public image. Guided tours regularly trace the route from the Alzette valley to the Bock while recounting the tale of Siegfried and Melusina, presenting visitors with a landscape where history and legend overlap.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitluxembourg.com/guided-tour/siegfried-melusina" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitluxembourg.com">[Visit Luxembourg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitluxembourg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit LuxembourgSiegfried &amp; MelusinaThis guided tour of approximately one hour invites you to immerse yourself in one of Luxembourg&#x27;s mos...</span></span></span>

One of the most recognisable modern reminders is the Melusina statue in the Grund district beside the Alzette.
