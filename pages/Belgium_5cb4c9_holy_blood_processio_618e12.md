## Introduction

In Bruges, one of [Belgium]({{ 'belgium/' | relative_url }})’s most famous folklore traditions is also one of its most overtly religious. The annual Procession of the Holy Blood transforms the medieval city into a vast piece of sacred theatre, combining Catholic devotion, civic pride, historical pageantry and living community memory. What makes it especially important within Belgian folklore is that it blurs the line between religion and folk tradition. The event is centred on a relic believed by devotees to contain the blood of Christ, yet its survival over centuries has depended not only on faith but also on guilds, neighbourhood groups, volunteers, costume makers and local families who pass participation from one generation to the next. Today, the procession remains one of the clearest examples of how sacred ritual became cultural heritage in Belgium. It has been recognised by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity and continues to draw thousands of spectators each year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/procession-of-the-holy-blood-in-bruges-00263" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageProcession of the Holy Blood in BrugesLed by the thirty city notables of the Brotherhood of the Holy Blood an...</span></span></span>

## When Belgian Folklore Becomes Sacred Theatre

Many people think of folklore as fairy tales, ghosts or legendary creatures. In Bruges, however, folklore often appears in the form of public ritual. The Holy Blood procession demonstrates how religious belief can become part of a city's shared storytelling tradition.

The event takes place annually on Ascension Day and has roots that reach back to the late medieval period. Historical records show that processions connected with the relic existed by the late thirteenth and early fourteenth centuries, and the tradition has continued with remarkable persistence despite political upheaval, religious change and modernisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitbruges.be/en/whats-on/events-calendar/procession-holy-blood-2026" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitbruges.be">[visitbruges.be]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitbruges.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">procession holy blood 2026</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit BrugesProcession of the Holy Blood 2026 - Visit Bruges...</span></span></span>

The procession is not simply a church ceremony. It is a carefully staged narrative that moves through the streets of Bruges. Biblical scenes, historical episodes and representations of local tradition unfold before spectators, turning the city itself into a performance space. In this sense, it resembles other forms of European civic folklore in which communities retell foundational stories through public drama.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitbruges.be/en/whats-on/events-calendar/procession-holy-blood-2026" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitbruges.be">[Visit Bruges]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitbruges.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">procession holy blood 2026</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit BrugesProcession of the Holy Blood 2026 - Visit Bruges...</span></span></span>

## The Relic at the Centre

At the heart of the tradition is a relic preserved in Bruges’ Basilica of the Holy Blood. According to long-standing tradition, the relic contains the blood of Christ and was brought to Flanders by Count Thierry of Alsace after a crusade in the twelfth century. The story became deeply embedded in local memory and helped establish Bruges as a destination for devotion and pilgrimage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Holy_Blood" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Basilica of the Holy Blood</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Basilica of the Holy Blood</span></span></span>

Modern historians are more cautious. Documentary evidence for the relic in Bruges appears only in the thirteenth century, and some researchers suggest it may have arrived after the Fourth Crusade rather than directly from the Holy Land. The exact route by which the relic reached Bruges remains debated. What matters from a folklore perspective is that the story of its arrival became part of the city’s identity regardless of historical uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Holy_Blood" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Basilica of the Holy Blood</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Basilica of the Holy Blood</span></span></span>

This distinction between historical evidence and traditional belief is important. Folk religion often operates through stories that communities regard as meaningful even when scholars continue to debate their origins. In Bruges, the relic’s power lies not only in what it is believed to be but also in the centuries of rituals, memories and narratives that have accumulated around it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Holy_Blood" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Basilica of the Holy Blood</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Basilica of the Holy Blood</span></span></span>

## Citizens, Costumes and Procession Routes

The modern procession remains a large-scale community undertaking. More than 1,700 participants take part, while thousands of spectators line the streets. The event includes musicians, actors, riders, volunteers and members of the Noble Brotherhood of the Holy Blood, the organisation that traditionally accompanies the relic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/procession-of-the-holy-blood-in-bruges-00263" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageProcession of the Holy Blood in BrugesLed by the thirty city notables of the Brotherhood of the Holy Blood an...</span></span></span>

One of the most striking features is the use of elaborate costumes and historical staging. Participants recreate scenes from the Old Testament, the life of Christ and the history of the relic itself. The structure of the procession intentionally links biblical history with the story of Bruges, suggesting that local identity forms part of a larger sacred narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitbruges.be/en/whats-on/events-calendar/procession-holy-blood-2026" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitbruges.be">[Visit Bruges]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitbruges.be</span><span class="citation-popover-title">procession holy blood 2026</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit BrugesProcession of the Holy Blood 2026 - Visit Bruges...</span></span></span>

This theatrical quality has deep roots. Medieval towns across the Low Countries often used guilds, religious brotherhoods and civic associations to stage public performances. Bruges preserved that tradition particularly successfully. The procession still evokes the atmosphere of the Burgundian era, when urban communities expressed faith through highly visual public spectacles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitbruges.be/en/things-to-do/about-bruges/world-heritage-city/procession-holy-blood" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitbruges.be">[Visit Bruges]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitbruges.be</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit BrugesProcession of the Holy BloodThe Procession of the Holy Blood is an event that still moves the whole city to this day and was...</span></span></span>

Families return year after year, costumes are maintained and repaired, and roles are passed between generations. This process of transmission is one reason UNESCO recognised the tradition as living heritage rather than a museum piece.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/procession-of-the-holy-blood-in-bruges-00263" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageProcession of the Holy Blood in BrugesLed by the thirty city notables of the Brotherhood of the Holy Blood an...</span></span></span>

## Folk Religion Beyond Official Doctrine

The Holy Blood tradition illustrates how folk religion differs from formal theology. Official Catholic teaching focuses on liturgy, doctrine and sacramental life. Folk religion adds local customs, emotional attachment to sacred objects, pilgrimage practices and community rituals.

In Bruges, devotion to the relic became woven into everyday urban identity. The relic was not simply a church possession; over time it became associated with the city itself. Medieval processions carried it around the town boundaries, symbolically linking divine protection with the civic community. The event therefore expressed both religious devotion and local solidarity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procession_of_the_Holy_Blood" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Procession of the Holy Blood</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Procession of the Holy Blood</span></span></span>

This mixture of sacred and civic elements is common in Belgian tradition. Across the country, processions, [giants]({{ 'giants/' | relative_url }}), saints’ festivals and ceremonial parades often combine religious themes with expressions of local pride. Bruges offers one of the clearest examples because the relic, the city and the annual performance have become inseparable in public imagination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitbruges.be/en/things-to-do/about-bruges/world-heritage-city/procession-holy-blood" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitbruges.be">[Visit Bruges]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitbruges.be</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit BrugesProcession of the Holy BloodThe Procession of the Holy Blood is an event that still moves the whole city to this day and was...</span></span></span>

## Faith, Heritage and Tourism Today

The meaning of the procession has broadened over time. For some participants, it remains primarily an act of faith. For others, it is a celebration of Bruges’ history or a cherished family tradition. Many spectators attend because of its cultural significance rather than personal religious belief. These different motivations coexist within the same event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bloedprocessiebrugge.be/en/i-am/visitor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bloedprocessiebrugge.be">[Heilig Bloedprocessie Brugge]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bloedprocessiebrugge.be</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heilig Bloedprocessie BruggeVisit the Procession of the Holy Blood in BrugesThe Procession of the Holy Blood is an annual event where gen...</span></span></span>

UNESCO’s recognition in 2009 reinforced this dual identity. The procession is now presented both as a religious observance and as an example of living cultural heritage. Heritage organisations emphasise community participation, continuity and craftsmanship alongside spiritual devotion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/procession-of-the-holy-blood-in-bruges-00263" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[Intangible Cultural Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural HeritageProcession of the Holy Blood in BrugesLed by the thirty city notables of the Brotherhood of the Holy Blood an...</span></span></span>

Tourism has also become part of the story. The Holy Blood procession demonstrates that Belgian folklore is not limited to legends of giants, ghosts or mythical creatures. It also includes rituals that transform belief into public performance.
