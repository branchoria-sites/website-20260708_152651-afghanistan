## Introduction

South-western [Saudi Arabia]({{ 'what-haunts-saudi-arabias-living/' | relative_url }}) has a folklore landscape that looks very different from the familiar image of endless desert stories. In the mountain regions of Asir and Jazan, steep valleys, terraced farms, stone villages and isolated settlements created traditions shaped by highland life rather than nomadic travel. Here, folk culture centres on village storytelling, local legends attached to mountains and ravines, oral poetry, and distinctive household arts preserved by generations of women. The result is one of the most regionally distinctive folklore traditions in Saudi Arabia. Evidence from heritage institutions, researchers and cultural preservation projects shows that the south-west has maintained strong local identities, with storytelling and decorative arts acting as important carriers of memory and community values.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asda.gov.sa/en/from-aseer/authenticity/folklore" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asda.gov.sa">[asda.gov.sa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asda.gov.sa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aseer Development AuthorityAuthenticity-Popular Folklore3 May 2026 — Poetry is an important part of Aseer&#x27;s folklore, with poems written...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>

## Why the South-West Developed Its Own Folklore

The Asir Mountains run along Saudi Arabia's south-western edge, overlooking the Red Sea and extending towards the Yemeni frontier. Historically, the region contained thousands of villages separated by ridges, valleys and difficult terrain. These conditions encouraged the development of highly local traditions, with stories often tied to specific settlements, peaks, caves, springs or abandoned buildings. National Council on U.S.-Arab Relations<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ncusar.org/blog/2022/07/asir-arabian-highlands-en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ncusar.org">[ncusar.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ncusar.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It is the fourth largest region in Saudi Arabia and encompasses four thousand villages.Read more...</span></span></span>

Unlike folklore associated with long-distance desert journeys, mountain folklore often emerged from settled agricultural life. Local poems, songs and stories reflected farming, grazing, building, seasonal weather and village relationships. Regional heritage sources note that oral poetry remains a central part of Asiri folk culture, with verses linked to everyday work and community life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asda.gov.sa/en/from-aseer/authenticity/folklore" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asda.gov.sa">[Aseer Development Authority]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asda.gov.sa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aseer Development AuthorityAuthenticity-Popular Folklore3 May 2026 — Poetry is an important part of Aseer&#x27;s folklore, with poems written...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>

The geography also encouraged a strong sense of place. A mountain village could develop its own stories about unusual rock formations, dangerous valleys or mysterious events remembered by local families. Many such traditions were transmitted orally and were never formally collected, meaning that folklore in the region survives unevenly, often through family memory rather than extensive written archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kirj.ee/public/trames_pdf/2017/issue_2/Trames-2017-2-161-171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kirj.ee">[Estonian Academy Publishers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kirj.ee</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Estonian Academy PublishersHISTORY OF SAUDI FOLKLORE AND FACTORS THAT...by M Altwaiji · Cited by 15 — Folk heritage includes the unrecor...</span></span></span>

## Jazan Legends and Mountain Beings

Jazan, south of Asir, occupies one of the most culturally varied areas of Saudi Arabia. Its folklore draws on coastal, island and mountain influences, creating a mixture of traditions that differs noticeably from central Arabian storytelling.

As in much of Arabia, stories involving jinn appear in local narratives, but mountain settings often give these tales a distinctive flavour. Ravines, caves, isolated paths and abandoned settlements frequently appear as locations where unusual encounters are said to occur. Such stories function partly as entertainment and partly as cautionary tales, warning listeners about dangerous terrain, travelling alone or venturing into unfamiliar places after dark.

Many local legends are attached to specific landscapes rather than to named supernatural creatures. A remote cliff, a ruined village or an unusually shaped rock formation may acquire a reputation through repeated storytelling. These narratives often blur the boundary between history, memory and the supernatural. A tragic event, a vanished settlement or an unexplained occurrence may gradually evolve into a legend passed between generations.

What distinguishes many south-western legends is their intimate scale. Rather than focusing on grand heroes or famous national myths, stories frequently revolve around ordinary villagers, family histories, local disputes, unexplained disappearances or encounters in the mountains. This local focus reflects the long-standing importance of village identity in the region.

## Village Storytelling Beyond the Desert Stereotype

One of the most persistent misunderstandings about Saudi folklore is the assumption that it is primarily desert folklore. The south-west challenges that idea.

Village storytelling traditions developed around communal gathering spaces, family visits and seasonal celebrations. Oral transmission was especially important because many stories were never formally written down. Folklore scholars note that oral poetry, tales and songs have historically been among the most significant vehicles for preserving cultural memory across Saudi Arabia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kirj.ee/public/trames_pdf/2017/issue_2/Trames-2017-2-161-171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kirj.ee">[Estonian Academy Publishers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kirj.ee</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Estonian Academy PublishersHISTORY OF SAUDI FOLKLORE AND FACTORS THAT...by M Altwaiji · Cited by 15 — Folk heritage includes the unrecor...</span></span></span>

In mountain communities, storytelling often served practical as well as cultural purposes. Narratives could:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-stack" markdown="1">

* Explain unusual natural features.
* Preserve local history.
* Reinforce social values.
* Warn against risky behaviour.
* Celebrate admired individuals.
* Maintain community identity.

The stories were rarely separated from everyday life. A tale about a mountain path might also be a lesson about caution. A legend concerning a village ancestor could preserve collective memory while entertaining younger listeners. The boundary between folklore, history and moral instruction was often fluid.

Music and poetry formed part of the same cultural environment. Heritage documentation from Asir highlights the importance of poems connected to farming, grazing and rural life, sometimes accompanied by traditional instruments such as shepherds' flutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asda.gov.sa/en/from-aseer/authenticity/folklore" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asda.gov.sa">[Aseer Development Authority]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asda.gov.sa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aseer Development AuthorityAuthenticity-Popular Folklore3 May 2026 — Poetry is an important part of Aseer&#x27;s folklore, with poems written...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>

## How Al-Qatt Al-Asiri Became a Folk Art Tradition

If south-western folklore has a visual symbol, it is Al-Qatt Al-Asiri. UNESCO describes it as a traditional form of interior wall decoration created primarily by women in the Asir region and recognises it as an element of Intangible Cultural Heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/al-qatt-al-asiri-female-traditional-interior-wall-decoration-in-asir-saudi-arabia-01261" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[ICH UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ration, is an ancient art form considered as a key element of the identity of the region of Asir...</span></span></span>

Traditionally, women decorated the reception rooms of family homes with bold geometric designs painted directly onto interior walls. The practice was not simply decoration. It was a marker of household pride, artistic skill and community participation. Women often worked together, inviting relatives and neighbours to assist, creating a social setting in which artistic knowledge could be passed from one generation to the next.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/decisions/12.COM/11.B.27" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[ICH UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICH UNESCODecision of the Intergovernmental Committee: 12.CO M 11</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Al-Qatt Al-Asiri, a traditionally female interior wall decoration, is an ancient art form considered as a key element of the identity of...</span></span></span>

The art is characterised by:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Strong geometric patterns.
* Bright colours such as red, yellow, green and black.
* Repeating bands and borders.
* Abstract representations inspired by local life and environment.
* Hand-painted, largely free-form execution rather than rigid templates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/al-qatt-al-asiri-female-traditional-interior-wall-decoration-in-asir-saudi-arabia-01261" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ration, is an ancient art form considered as a key element of the identity of the region of Asir...</span></span></span>

</div>

Unlike many forms of elite art, Al-Qatt developed within ordinary homes. Its primary setting was the domestic interior, making it part of everyday life rather than a specialised ceremonial tradition.

## The Women's Culture Behind the Paintings

The most remarkable aspect of Al-Qatt Al-Asiri is that it represents a female-centred artistic tradition. Men typically built houses, while women shaped their visual identity through decoration. The artistic knowledge was learned through observation, participation and family teaching rather than formal instruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qatt_Al-Asiri" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Al-Qatt Al-Asiri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Al-Qatt Al-Asiri</span></span></span>

Because women frequently collaborated on painting projects, the art strengthened social relationships within villages. UNESCO notes that the tradition enhances solidarity among women and remains closely connected to community life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/nine-elements-inscribed-representative-list-intangible-cultural-heritage-humanity" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nine elements inscribed on the Representative List of...20 Apr 2023 — Al-Qatt Al-Asin, a female traditional interior wall decorati...</span></span></span>

## From Household Tradition to National Heritage

For centuries, Al-Qatt existed mainly within villages and private homes. Today it has become one of the most internationally recognised expressions of Saudi folk culture.

## What Makes South-Western Folklore Different?

The folklore of Asir and Jazan stands apart because it combines mountain storytelling, village memory and household art into a single cultural landscape.

Rather than focusing primarily on desert travel and tribal wandering, south-western traditions emerge from settled communities surrounded by dramatic terrain. Legends are often tied to specific villages and mountain locations. Oral poetry reflects agricultural life. Decorative traditions transform domestic spaces into expressions of identity. And women play a particularly visible role in preserving cultural heritage through artistic practice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asda.gov.sa/en/from-aseer/authenticity/folklore" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asda.gov.sa">[asda.gov.sa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asda.gov.sa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aseer Development AuthorityAuthenticity-Popular Folklore3 May 2026 — Poetry is an important part of Aseer&#x27;s folklore, with poems written...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>
