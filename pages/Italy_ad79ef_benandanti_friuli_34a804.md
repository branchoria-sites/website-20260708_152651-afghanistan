## Introduction

The benandanti of Friuli occupy a unique place in Italian folklore because they are not known mainly through legends told centuries later. Instead, they appear in detailed records created by church investigators during the late sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. According to their own testimony, the benandanti were not witches at all. They described themselves as protectors of their communities who left their bodies in spirit during certain nights of the year and fought malevolent witches to safeguard crops, livestock and fertility. What makes the tradition remarkable is that the evidence comes from inquisitorial interrogations in which villagers explained their beliefs in their own words before church authorities gradually reinterpreted those beliefs through the framework of witchcraft. The surviving records provide one of the clearest windows into an early modern European visionary tradition and have become a famous case study in both folklore and history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Battles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Night Battles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 13, 2024 — The Night Battles: Witchcraft and Agrarian Cults in the Sixteenth and... ## Were the Benandanti Witches or Protectors?

The earliest testimonies portray the benandanti as defenders rather than servants of evil. They believed that certain people were marked from birth, often by being born with a caul, the membrane covering the head at birth. In local belief this sign indicated a special destiny and supernatural responsibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benandanti" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BenandantiIn Ginzburg&#x27;s interpretation of the evidence, the benandanti was a &quot;fertility cult... When questioned by church officials, benandanti explained that on particular nights their spirits travelled from their sleeping bodies. Men often described joining supernatural battles, while women more commonly reported attending visionary gatherings or feasts. These journeys were understood as real experiences with practical consequences for the community. A successful struggle meant healthy harvests and abundance; defeat meant crop failure and hardship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benandanti" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BenandantiIn Ginzburg&#x27;s interpretation of the evidence, the benandanti was a &quot;fertility cult... What stands out in the records is the moral reversal of the usual witchcraft story. The benandanti did not claim to curse fields. They claimed to protect them. They did not describe themselves as servants of the Devil. They presented themselves as loyal Christians carrying out a necessary task for the common good. Early inquisitors often seemed uncertain how to classify such claims because they did not fit established categories of heresy or witchcraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Battles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Night Battles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 13, 2024 — The Night Battles: Witchcraft and Agrarian Cults in the Sixteenth and... ## Spirit Battles for the Crops

The most famous element of the tradition is the series of nocturnal battles that gave Carlo Ginzburg's landmark study its title, *The Night Battles*. According to testimony preserved in the records, benandanti fought witches in spirit form during seasonal periods connected with agricultural fertility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Battles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Night Battles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 13, 2024 — The Night Battles: Witchcraft and Agrarian Cults in the Sixteenth and... To modern readers, these stories may resemble dreams, visions or ecstatic experiences. Whatever their origin, the participants themselves treated them as genuine events affecting the material world. The importance of the harvest in a largely rural society helps explain why such beliefs carried real social significance.

## What the Inquisition Records Reveal

The surviving evidence comes largely from inquisitorial investigations in Friuli beginning in the 1570s. Historians consider these records unusually valuable because they preserve long exchanges between villagers and church authorities. Rather than a brief accusation, many cases reveal a dialogue in which inquisitors tried to understand beliefs that initially seemed unfamiliar even to them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Battles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Night Battles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 13, 2024 — The Night Battles: Witchcraft and Agrarian Cults in the Sixteenth and... One early and influential case involved Paolo Gasparutto, questioned in 1575. During interrogation he described the nocturnal journeys of the benandanti and their role in protecting crops. His testimony helped reveal a wider network of people who shared similar beliefs. The records also reveal a subtle change over time. Initially, some inquisitors treated the claims as strange popular beliefs rather than evidence of witchcraft. However, church authorities increasingly interpreted spirit travel, nocturnal assemblies and supernatural combat through the established model of the witches' sabbath. Once questioned within that framework, witnesses often began to describe their experiences using language that more closely resembled accepted demonological ideas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Battles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Night Battles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 13, 2024 — The Night Battles: Witchcraft and Agrarian Cults in the Sixteenth and... This gradual shift is one of the most important findings in the evidence. The records suggest that inquisitors did not simply discover a ready-made witch cult. Instead, a local folk tradition was progressively redefined through the assumptions of learned witchcraft theory. By the seventeenth century, the distinction between benandanti and witches had become increasingly blurred in official eyes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Battles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Night Battles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 13, 2024 — The Night Battles: Witchcraft and Agrarian Cults in the Sixteenth and... ## The Debate Over Ancient Survivals

The benandanti became internationally famous because of the interpretation offered by the Italian historian Carlo Ginzburg. Working from the Friulian trial records, he argued that the tradition might preserve fragments of a much older fertility cult centred on the protection of crops and seasonal abundance. He also drew comparisons with visionary traditions elsewhere in Europe, including stories of spirit journeys and supernatural defenders of fertility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Battles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Night Battles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 13, 2024 — The Night Battles: Witchcraft and Agrarian Cults in the Sixteenth and... Considering the benandanti to be &quot;a fertilit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 13, 2024</span></span></span>

This interpretation proved highly influential but also controversial. Critics accepted the importance of the records while questioning whether the evidence was strong enough to prove survival from a pre-Christian cult. Historians such as Norman Cohn and Ronald Hutton argued that similarities between traditions do not necessarily demonstrate a continuous ancient religion. They suggested that the surviving documents may show a local early modern belief system without requiring a direct line back to pagan antiquity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benandanti" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BenandantiIn Ginzburg&#x27;s interpretation of the evidence, the benandanti was a &quot;fertility cult... As a result, modern scholarship generally agrees on several points:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The benandanti were a genuine and well-documented Friulian tradition.
* Participants described visionary journeys and battles linked to agricultural fertility.
* Inquisitorial records preserve these beliefs in unusual detail.
* Church authorities gradually reinterpreted the tradition as witchcraft.

</div>

## Why the Benandanti Matter in Italian Folklore

Many European witchcraft traditions are known only through accusations made by authorities. The benandanti are different because the records capture a moment before complete assimilation into the stereotype of the witch. Their testimony offers a rare glimpse of how ordinary rural people explained dreams, visions, harvest anxiety and communal responsibility in their own terms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_Battles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Night Battles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 13, 2024 — The Night Battles: Witchcraft and Agrarian Cults in the Sixteenth and...
