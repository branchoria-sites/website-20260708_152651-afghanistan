## Introduction

[Latvia]({{ 'why-are-latvias-songs-filled-with-gods/' | relative_url }})’s most important folklore treasure is not a single epic, sacred book, or legendary cycle. It is a vast body of short folk songs known as dainas. These compact verses preserve fragments of mythology, family customs, seasonal rituals, agricultural life, and everyday emotions. More than a literary tradition, they became one of the foundations of modern Latvian national identity. During the nineteenth-century national awakening, collectors and scholars presented dainas as evidence that Latvians possessed a rich and distinctive culture of their own. The songs later helped sustain cultural continuity through political upheaval, occupation, and exile, and they remain deeply connected to Latvia’s image of itself as a “singing nation”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/dainu-skapis-cabinet-folksongs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dainu Skapis - Cabinet of Folksongs - Memory of the WorldCarried out by Latvians themselves, it was made the basis for their nation...</span></span></span>

Unlike many European cultures that preserved mythology through long heroic narratives, Latvian traditional culture often survived in thousands of brief song-poems. Together, those songs form an extraordinary cultural archive: a record of how generations of people understood nature, community, work, fate, and belonging.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kulturaskanons.lv/en/archive/latvju-dainas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kulturaskanons.lv">[Kultūras Kanons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kulturaskanons.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kultūras KanonsLatvian Folksongs – DainasThey not only form part of the cultural heritage of Latvia but are also classed as symbols of La...</span></span></span>

## What Makes a Daina Unique?

A daina is typically very short, often only two to four lines long. Rather than telling a complete story, it captures a moment, image, observation, or symbolic relationship. A single song might describe the rising sun, a wedding ritual, a field of rye, a mother’s advice, or a mythological figure crossing the sky. Thousands of such fragments together create a picture of traditional Latvian life and belief.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kulturaskanons.lv/en/archive/latvju-dainas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kulturaskanons.lv">[kulturaskanons.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kulturaskanons.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kultūras KanonsLatvian Folksongs – DainasThey not only form part of the cultural heritage of Latvia but are also classed as symbols of La...</span></span></span>

This brevity distinguishes Latvian folklore from traditions built around large narrative epics. Dainas generally do not focus on legendary heroes or dramatic adventures. Instead, they connect cosmic themes with everyday experience. Mythological beings appear alongside farmers, families, and seasonal labour, making the sacred feel closely woven into ordinary life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daina_%28Latvia%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Daina (Latvia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Daina (Latvia</span></span></span>

Their themes include:

## The Cabinet of Folksongs

The transformation of dainas from oral tradition into a national archive is closely associated with the folklorist Krišjānis Barons. Beginning in the late nineteenth century, collectors across Latvia gathered folk songs from singers and communities. Barons organised this material into what became known as the Cabinet of Folksongs, or Dainu skapis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/dainu-skapis-cabinet-folksongs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dainu Skapis - Cabinet of Folksongs - Memory of the WorldCarried out by Latvians themselves, it was made the basis for their nation...</span></span></span>

UNESCO describes the collection effort as part of the wider era of European national awakenings. Nearly 218,000 song texts were assembled, classified, and published under Barons’s direction between 1894 and 1915. The collection was created by Latvians themselves and became a key resource in defining national culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/dainu-skapis-cabinet-folksongs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dainu Skapis - Cabinet of Folksongs - Memory of the WorldCarried out by Latvians themselves, it was made the basis for their nation...</span></span></span>

The cabinet itself became a cultural symbol. Thousands of handwritten slips containing songs were arranged in specially designed drawers, creating a physical monument to collective memory. UNESCO later added the Cabinet of Folksongs to its Memory of the World Register, recognising its significance as a documentary heritage collection of international importance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/dainu-skapis-cabinet-folksongs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dainu Skapis - Cabinet of Folksongs - Memory of the WorldCarried out by Latvians themselves, it was made the basis for their nation...</span></span></span>

What makes the Cabinet especially important is that it transformed oral tradition into evidence. At a time when many European peoples were defining themselves through language, history, and folklore, the collection provided a tangible demonstration of the depth and richness of Latvian cultural life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/dainu-skapis-cabinet-folksongs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dainu Skapis - Cabinet of Folksongs - Memory of the WorldCarried out by Latvians themselves, it was made the basis for their nation...</span></span></span>

## Why Songs Became a Foundation of National Identity

The nineteenth century was a crucial period for Latvia. Intellectuals, writers, teachers, and collectors sought ways to demonstrate that Latvian culture was not merely a regional peasant tradition but the heritage of a distinct people. Dainas became central to that effort.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/dainu-skapis-cabinet-folksongs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dainu Skapis - Cabinet of Folksongs - Memory of the WorldCarried out by Latvians themselves, it was made the basis for their nation...</span></span></span>

Several factors made the songs especially powerful as symbols of identity.

**They were widespread.** The songs came from many regions and social backgrounds rather than from a single author or elite institution. This allowed them to be presented as the authentic voice of the people.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/dainu-skapis-cabinet-folksongs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dainu Skapis - Cabinet of Folksongs - Memory of the WorldCarried out by Latvians themselves, it was made the basis for their nation...</span></span></span>

**They preserved cultural continuity.** Even where written historical records were limited, dainas preserved memories of customs, values, and worldviews stretching back through generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kulturaskanons.lv/en/archive/latvju-dainas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kulturaskanons.lv">[Kultūras Kanons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kulturaskanons.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kultūras KanonsLatvian Folksongs – DainasThey not only form part of the cultural heritage of Latvia but are also classed as symbols of La...</span></span></span>

**They linked language and heritage.** The songs showcased the expressive power of the Latvian language at a time when linguistic identity was becoming politically and culturally important.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/dainu-skapis-cabinet-folksongs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dainu Skapis - Cabinet of Folksongs - Memory of the WorldCarried out by Latvians themselves, it was made the basis for their nation...</span></span></span>

**They connected ordinary life with national culture.** Instead of focusing solely on rulers or military events, the songs celebrated everyday experience. This helped create an image of the nation rooted in shared cultural memory rather than dynastic history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kulturaskanons.lv/en/archive/latvju-dainas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kulturaskanons.lv">[Kultūras Kanons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kulturaskanons.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kultūras KanonsLatvian Folksongs – DainasThey not only form part of the cultural heritage of Latvia but are also classed as symbols of La...</span></span></span>

By the early twentieth century, dainas had become far more than folklore texts. They were widely treated as symbols of Latvian distinctiveness and cultural endurance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kulturaskanons.lv/en/archive/latvju-dainas/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kulturaskanons.lv">[kulturaskanons.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kulturaskanons.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kultūras KanonsLatvian Folksongs – DainasThey not only form part of the cultural heritage of Latvia but are also classed as symbols of La...</span></span></span>

## Songs, Identity and Memory

Readers searching for “Latvian mythology” are often surprised to discover that there is no single ancient text comparable to the Icelandic Eddas or Finland’s Kalevala.
