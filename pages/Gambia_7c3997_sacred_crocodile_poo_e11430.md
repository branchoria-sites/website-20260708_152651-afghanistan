## Introduction

Among the most distinctive sacred landscapes in Gambian tradition are its crocodile pools: places where water, animals, fertility hopes and local custodianship come together. Unlike stories about distant mythical creatures, these sites are living places that people still visit for blessings, healing, prayers and family ceremonies. The best known is Kachikally in Bakau, but Folonko in Kartong and Berending on the North Bank form part of the same wider tradition. Across these sites, crocodiles are not treated simply as dangerous reptiles. They are regarded as sacred guardians associated with fertility, prosperity, protection and the spiritual power of particular pools. The result is a form of folklore rooted in landscape itself, where belief is tied to specific water sources and to the families entrusted with caring for them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kachikally.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kachikally.com">[kachikally.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kachikally.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Sacred Crocodile Pool of Kachikally: A Cultural in...The Sacred Crocodile Pool of Kachikally has been an essential locatio...</span></span></span>

## Kachikally and the Sacred Pool Tradition

Kachikally, located in Bakau near the Atlantic coast, is the most famous of The [Gambia]({{ 'where-gambias-spirits-still-meet/' | relative_url }})'s sacred crocodile pools. Local traditions connect the site to centuries of ritual use, and stories about its origins are intertwined with the history of the community itself. The pool is not simply a wildlife attraction. For many Gambians it has long been a place of pilgrimage where people seek blessings, fertility and spiritual assistance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kachikally.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kachikally.com">[kachikally.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kachikally.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Sacred Crocodile Pool of Kachikally: A Cultural in...The Sacred Crocodile Pool of Kachikally has been an essential locatio...</span></span></span>

Tradition holds that the crocodiles inhabiting the pool are guardians of the sacred waters. Visitors may be surprised by the unusual relationship between people and animals here. At Kachikally, crocodiles are famous for their tolerance of human presence, and touching one is often presented as a symbolic act connected with receiving blessing or good fortune. This unusual closeness has become one of the defining images of Gambian heritage tourism, but it emerged from older local beliefs rather than from tourism itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/kachikally-crocodile-pool/1022359" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lonelyplanet.com">[lonelyplanet.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lonelyplanet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lonely Planet Kachikally Crocodile Pool</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lonely PlanetKachikally Crocodile Pool - The GambiaOne of The Gambia&#x27;s most popular tourist attractions is a sacred site for locals. Kachikally is also associated with particular custodial families. The site has historically been managed by members of the Bojang family, whose role extends beyond practical maintenance. In local understanding, sacred sites require guardians who preserve rituals, stories and proper conduct around the pool. The sacred landscape is therefore protected not only by belief in the crocodiles themselves but also by inherited responsibilities within the community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachikally_Museum_and_Crocodile_Pool" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The importance of Kachikally has helped shape the wider idea of sacred crocodile pools throughout The Gambia. Other pools are often understood through comparison with it, even though each has its own local traditions and guardianship arrangements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachikally_Museum_and_Crocodile_Pool" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Crocodiles, Fertility and Blessing

The strongest and most widespread belief associated with the sacred pools concerns fertility. For generations, women and couples hoping for children have visited these sites to pray, wash with sacred water or participate in rituals overseen by local custodians. The crocodile functions as a symbol of life-giving power, and successful pregnancies are sometimes interpreted as evidence that blessings have been received.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kachikally.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kachikally.com">[kachikally.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kachikally.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Sacred Crocodile Pool of Kachikally: A Cultural in...The Sacred Crocodile Pool of Kachikally has been an essential locatio...</span></span></span>

Stories explaining these beliefs vary. One common tradition links Kachikally's sacred status to a woman who struggled with infertility and later conceived after ritual contact with the pool. Whether understood literally, spiritually or symbolically, such narratives reinforce the pool's reputation as a place where life and renewal are possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kachikally.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kachikally.com">[Kachikally]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kachikally.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Sacred Crocodile Pool of Kachikally: A Cultural in...The Sacred Crocodile Pool of Kachikally has been an essential locatio...</span></span></span>

The fertility connection extends beyond Kachikally. Folonko and Berending are likewise associated with health, luck and blessing. In these traditions, the sacred water itself matters as much as the crocodiles. The animals are viewed as guardians or intermediaries linked to the power of the place rather than as the sole source of that power. The combination of water, wildlife and ritual creates a distinctive Gambian sacred landscape in which nature and spirituality are inseparable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berending_%28Kultst%C3%A4tte%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berending (Kultstätte</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berending (Kultstätte</span></span></span>

Folklore surrounding crocodiles also reaches beyond the pools. In Gambian and wider Senegambian tradition, crocodiles have long been associated with strength, fertility and good fortune. Older sayings and symbolic references present the animal as more than an ordinary creature, helping explain why crocodile pools became centres of ritual importance in the first place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berending_%28Kultst%C3%A4tte%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berending (Kultstätte</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berending (Kultstätte</span></span></span>

## Respect, Tourism and Local Custodianship

Today the sacred crocodile pools stand at the meeting point of folklore, religion, conservation and tourism. Thousands of visitors come each year to see crocodiles at close range, learn about local traditions and experience places that are unlike most wildlife attractions. Yet local communities generally continue to present the pools as sacred sites first and tourist destinations second.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.my-gambia.com/mymagazine/kachikally-sacred-site-and-crocodile-pool/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: my-gambia.com">[my-gambia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">my-gambia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">My GambiaKachikally: Sacred Site And Crocodile PoolAlongside more than 200 crocodiles living in Kachikally, you can also do the forest wa...</span></span></span>

This balance can be delicate. Tourism brings income and visibility, but it can also encourage simplified versions of older traditions. Fertility rituals, blessing ceremonies and sacred stories are sometimes retold for visitors in ways that emphasise spectacle. At the same time, many Gambians continue to visit the pools for personal and spiritual reasons that have little to do with tourism. The sites therefore operate in two worlds at once: heritage attractions for outsiders and meaningful sacred places for local communities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowTopic-g293794-i9249-k9984342-Blessing_at_Kachikally_Sacred_Crocodile_Pool-Gambia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tripadvisor.com">[tripadvisor.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tripadvisor.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">I never saw it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blessing at Kachikally Sacred Crocodile Pool - Gambia...Nov 6, 2016 — Allegedly the bathing at Kachikally was a fertility rit...</span></span></span>

The role of guardianship remains central. Sacred pools survive because communities regard themselves as custodians rather than owners. Folonko has traditionally been cared for by a local family in Kartong, while Berending's pool has been the focus of preservation efforts intended to protect both the site and its cultural significance. Government and heritage advocates have increasingly discussed conservation measures, but local knowledge and community stewardship remain fundamental to how these places are understood.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folonko" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

One striking feature of the Gambian tradition is that sacred authority is attached not to a temple or monument but to a living ecosystem. The pools depend on water, vegetation, crocodiles and community memory. If any of these disappear, part of the tradition disappears with them.
