## Introduction

The worship of the Hng Kings occupies a distinctive place in Vietnamese culture because it treats the nations origin story as a living family inheritance rather than a distant myth. In Vietnamese tradition, the Hng Kings are remembered as the founding rulers of the ancient state of Vn Lang and as the common ancestors of the Vietnamese people. Their story is closely linked to the famous legend of the dragon lord Lc Long Qun and the fairy mother u C, whose descendants are said to have become the ancestors of [Vietnam]({{ 'vietnam/' | relative_url }}). Today, that ancestral narrative is not merely retold in books or folklore collections. It is expressed through pilgrimage, offerings, festivals, temple rituals and a national day of commemoration that draws people back to what many describe as the symbolic roots of the nation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/worship-of-hung-kings-in-phu-th-00735" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO ICHWorship of Hùng kings in Phú ThọThe tradition embodies spiritual solidarity and provides an occasion to acknowledge national or...</span></span></span>

## The ancestral founders in Vietnamese legend

At the heart of Hng Kings worship is a powerful ancestry narrative. According to Vietnamese legend, the dragon-associated Lc Long Qun and the fairy-like u C became the parents of a hundred children. The story explains that some descendants followed their father towards the coast while others followed their mother into the mountains, creating a symbolic origin for the diverse communities that would eventually form the Vietnamese people. The Hng Kings are traditionally presented as descendants of this union and as the rulers who established the first Vietnamese state.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vietnamlawmagazine.vn/hung-kings-temple-in-the-ancestral-land-of-vietnamese-people-79303.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vietnamlawmagazine.vn">[vietnamlawmagazine.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vietnamlawmagazine.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">hung kings temple in the ancestral land of vietnamese people 79303</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hung Kings Temple in the ancestral land of Vietnamese...25 Apr 2026 — Legend has it that they were the descendants of Lac Long Quan and...</span></span></span>

For folklorists, the importance of this legend lies less in historical proof than in cultural meaning. The tale links mountains and waterways, two landscapes that have shaped Vietnamese life for centuries. It also offers an explanation for national unity: although people may come from different regions, they share common ancestors. The story therefore functions both as mythology and as a statement about belonging.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/worship-of-hung-kings-in-phu-th-00735" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO ICHWorship of Hùng kings in Phú ThọThe tradition embodies spiritual solidarity and provides an occasion to acknowledge national or...</span></span></span>

Unlike many founding myths that remain confined to literature, the Hng Kings narrative became embedded in ritual life. Temples dedicated to the Hng Kings and figures associated with their era appear across Vietnam, but the most important concentration is in Ph Th Province, widely regarded as the ancestral homeland of the tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.vietnamplus.vn/hung-kings-temple-historical-relic-site-post265195.vnp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.vietnamplus.vn">[vietnamplus.vn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.vietnamplus.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">hung kings temple historical relic site post265195.vnp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vietnam+ (VietnamPlus)Hung Kings Temple historical relic siteApr 28, 2023 — A festival to honour and showcase Vietnam&#x27;s UNESCO-recognised...</span></span></span>

## How a national origin myth became a living tradition

Ancestor worship has long been a central feature of Vietnamese culture, but Hng Kings worship expands the idea from the family level to the national level. Scholars have noted that the tradition effectively creates a form of collective ancestor veneration in which the founders of the nation are honoured as shared forebears. This helps explain why the practice resonates far beyond a single temple or locality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ijsshr.in/v5i8/Doc/40.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ijsshr.in">[IJSSHR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ijsshr.in</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Universality of Belief of Worshiping Hung Kings in the...August 18, 2022 — 8 Aug 2022 — Stemming from the geo-political and ge...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 18, 2022</span></span></span>

Historical dynasties encouraged reverence for the Hng Kings, and later governments continued to support commemorations. Over time, the tradition evolved from local worship into a symbol of national continuity. Historical records and official commemorations repeatedly presented the Hng Kings as the forefathers of the Vietnamese people, reinforcing the connection between ancestry and national identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://english.vov.vn/en/society/worship-of-the-hung-kings-the-mainstay-of-great-national-unity-post1017111.vov" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english.vov.vn">[VOV.VN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english.vov.vn</span><span class="citation-popover-title">worship of the hung kings the mainstay of great national unity post1017111.vov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">of the Hung Kings – the mainstay of great national...Apr 30, 2023 — The worship of the Hung Kings in Phu Tho province was inscribed on t...</span></span></span>

What makes the tradition especially notable in folklore studies is its durability. While many origin myths survive mainly through storytelling, the Hng Kings remain part of annual ritual practice. ## The temple festival and public ritual

The most visible expression of the tradition is the Hng Kings Temple Festival in Ph Th. The main commemoration takes place on the tenth day of the third lunar month, although festival activities extend across several days. Pilgrims travel to the temple complex on Ngha Lnh Mountain to offer incense, honour the ancestral founders and pray for prosperity, health and favourable conditions for the coming year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/archives/multimedia/document-3768" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">document 3768</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Worship of Hùng Kings In Phú Tho &#124; Intangible HeritageDec 4, 2014 — Annually, millions of people converge on the Hùng temple at Ngh...</span></span></span>

The rituals include formal processions, ceremonial offerings and acts of remembrance. UNESCO describes the tradition as involving ceremonies, pilgrimages and cultural performances maintained by communities in Ph Th and elsewhere in Vietnam. The festival therefore combines religious devotion, local custom and public celebration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ich.unesco.org/en/decisions/7.COM/11.36" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ich.unesco.org">[UNESCO ICH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ich.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO ICHDecision of the Intergovernmental Committee: 7.COM 11.36R.1: The worship of Hùng kings includes ceremonies, offerings, pilgrima...</span></span></span>
