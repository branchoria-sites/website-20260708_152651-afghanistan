## Introduction

Qat is the best-known creator and culture hero in the traditional stories of northern [Vanuatu]({{ 'why-vanuatus-folklore-lives-island-by/' | relative_url }}), especially the Banks Islands. Depending on the island and language, he may be known as Qat, Qet, Iqet, Kpwet or by other related names. Rather than being a distant god who receives worship, he appears in oral tradition as a maker, teacher, problem-solver and trickster whose actions explain how human life came to be organised. Through stories about Qat and his rivals, northern communities explored some of the biggest questions people can ask: Where did humans come from? Why do people die? Why is there night? Why do societies have rules?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qat_%28deity%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Qat (deity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Qat (deity</span></span></span>

## The Banks Islands Story World

The Qat stories belong primarily to the Banks Islands and nearby northern islands of Vanuatu. Unlike many creation myths that begin with a completely empty universe, the Banks Islands traditions often present Qat as entering an existing world and reshaping it. He becomes the organiser of reality rather than its sole creator from nothing. In different versions he forms islands, creates plants and animals, fashions human beings and introduces key elements of social life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pantheon.org/articles/q/qat.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pantheon.org">[pantheon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pantheon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Qat &#124; Facts, Information, and MythologyMar 3, 1997 — His friend and foe is the spider spirit Marawa, who both opposes and helps h...</span></span></span>

The stories vary from island to island. Modern linguistic research has shown that the figure recorded as “Qat” in Mota-speaking areas appears under several related names elsewhere in northern Vanuatu. Some communities know the same cultural hero as Kpwet, Ikpwet, Qo' or other local forms. This diversity reflects the remarkable linguistic richness of northern Vanuatu rather than disagreement about the character himself. The hero remains recognisably the same figure even when details differ.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hal.science/hal-05479737/file/Bessis-Fran%25C3%25A7ois_2025_Myths-tales-or-history_TeReo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hal.science">[hal.science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hal.science</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Myths, tales or history?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">On two storytelling traditions in...by S Bessis · 2025 · Cited by 2 — The cultural hero of the Banks islands – named Qat by Codrington (...</span></span></span>

Many narratives also place Qat among a group of brothers, often including both wise and foolish companions. Their interactions provide opportunities for humour, rivalry and cautionary lessons. The stories frequently explain why the world is imperfect despite the efforts of a powerful culture hero.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesian_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Melanesian mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Melanesian mythology</span></span></span>

## Creator, Trickster and Culture-Bringer

What makes Qat distinctive is that he combines several roles that are often separated in other mythologies.

### Making the First Humans

One of the most famous Banks Islands stories tells how Qat created the first people from wood. After shaping male and female figures, he brought them to life through ritual actions involving music, dancing or drumming. The tale presents humanity as an intentional creation rather than an accident. It also links human origins with performance and cultural knowledge, themes that remain important throughout Vanuatu traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pantheon.org/articles/q/qat.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pantheon.org">[pantheon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pantheon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Qat &#124; Facts, Information, and MythologyMar 3, 1997 — His friend and foe is the spider spirit Marawa, who both opposes and helps h...</span></span></span>

### Bringing Night to the World

Another celebrated story explains the origin of night. In the earliest age, according to the tradition, daylight never ended. Qat learned that another land possessed darkness and sleep, travelled there, acquired Night and brought it back to his people. The arrival of darkness allowed rest, dreams and the daily rhythm that humans now take for granted. In some versions, the first dawn follows when Qat cuts open the darkness and releases morning light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesian_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Melanesian mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Melanesian mythology</span></span></span>

This story is especially notable because it reverses assumptions familiar from many Western traditions. Night is not the original state from which light emerges; instead, daylight comes first and darkness is a valuable cultural acquisition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesian_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Melanesian mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Melanesian mythology</span></span></span>

### Establishing Social Order

Northern Vanuatu traditions also credit Qat with introducing important social institutions. Folklore associates him with marriage rules, restrictions on incest and other foundations of orderly community life. Some traditions even attribute the origin of special ceremonial song language to him. In this sense Qat is not merely a creator of physical things but a creator of culture itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qat_%28deity%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Qat (deity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Qat (deity</span></span></span>

## Why Death Exists: The Rivalry with Marawa

Some of the most memorable Qat stories concern the origin of death. These tales often introduce Marawa, a spider spirit who acts as rival, companion, imitator or occasional helper. Marawa's role is not simply evil. Instead, he represents the dangers of copying wisdom without understanding it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pantheon.org/articles/q/qat.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pantheon.org">[pantheon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pantheon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Qat &#124; Facts, Information, and MythologyMar 3, 1997 — His friend and foe is the spider spirit Marawa, who both opposes and helps h...</span></span></span>

In a well-known version, Qat successfully creates living humans from carved wooden figures. Marawa attempts to repeat the achievement but uses different materials and methods. His creations fail. When he buries them and later uncovers them, they have decayed. Their corruption becomes the explanation for why death entered the human world. The story contrasts successful creativity with flawed imitation and provides a mythic answer to one of humanity's oldest questions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Melanesians%3A_Studies_in_their_Anthropology_and_Folklore/Chapter_10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[wikisource.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Chapter 10</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Studies in their Anthropology and Folklore/Chapter 10Aug 9, 2017 — But Marawa made his of another tree, the tavisoviso; he work...</span></span></span>

Other Banks Islands traditions offer different explanations for mortality. In some versions humans originally renewed themselves by shedding their skins. Death arrives because of a mistake, misunderstanding or failure to follow instructions. These variations show that there was never a single fixed doctrine. Instead, storytellers explored mortality through multiple narrative possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesian_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Melanesian mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Melanesian mythology</span></span></span>

## Why Qat Is Also a Trickster

Although often described as a creator, Qat is equally a trickster. He wins contests through intelligence rather than brute force. Stories portray him outwitting giants, fooling enemies, solving practical problems and escaping dangerous situations. His cleverness is celebrated, but it is not always solemn or majestic.
